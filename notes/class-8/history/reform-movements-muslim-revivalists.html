Reform Movements and Muslim Revivalists

Complete question-and-answer notes on the Muslim reformers and revivalist movements of the subcontinent — free from W.H. Academy.

Take the Quiz Download PDF

Learning Objectives

  • Describe the challenges faced by the Muslims after the decline of the Mughals.
  • Explain the contributions of Mujaddid Alf-e-Sani and Shah Waliullah to the revival of Islam.
  • Describe the Faraizi Movement of Haji Shariatullah and the Jihad Movement of Syed Ahmed Barelvi.
  • Explain the social, political and educational contributions of Sir Syed Ahmed Khan and the Aligarh Movement.
  • Identify the roles of Deoband, Nadvat-ul-Ulema and the Hindu revivalist movements of the same period.

Questions & Answers

1. Colonialism and the Revival of Islam

What kind of natives did the British want to create through their new policies?
  • Phrasing 1: What kind of natives did the British want to create through their new policies?
  • Phrasing 2: What was the aim of the new British educational and social policies?

Ans: They wanted to create natives who were Indian by birth but European in their outlook and sensibilities.

How were the people of India colonised, besides physically?
  • Phrasing 1: How were the people of India colonised, besides physically?
  • Phrasing 2: What does it mean that Indians were colonised "psychologically"?

Ans: Along with being ruled physically, their thinking and mindset were also influenced, so they became colonised mentally as well as physically.

Why did Muslim scholars begin to revive Islamic teachings?

Ans: To improve the moral and social conditions of the Muslims and to encourage them to reclaim their former political glory.

What was the main aim of the Muslim revivalists?
  • Phrasing 1: What was the main aim of the Muslim revivalists?
  • Phrasing 2: What did the reformers want to achieve for the Muslims?

Ans: To create awareness among Muslims about Islam and to unite them against the imperial (British) forces.

In whose revival were these figures instrumental?

Ans: They were instrumental in the revival of Muslim Nationalism in the subcontinent.

How are these scholars remembered today?

Ans: They are seen as the pioneers in creating a sense of national identity among the Muslims of the subcontinent.

What is meant by a "revivalist" in this chapter?

Ans: A scholar or reformer who worked to revive true Islamic teachings and the moral, social and political condition of the Muslims.

2. Mujaddid Alf-e-Sani (Sheikh Ahmad Sirhindi)

By what title is Sheikh Ahmad Sirhindi commonly known?

Ans: He is commonly known as Mujaddid Alf-e-Sani.

What does the word "Mujaddid" literally mean?

Ans: The word "Mujaddid" literally means "reviver".

In which year was Ahmad Sirhindi born?

Ans: He was born in 1564.

Whose descendants did Ahmad Sirhindi’s family claim to be?

Ans: They claimed to be direct descendants of the second Caliph, Hazrat Umar Ibn Al-Khattab.

Who was Ahmad Sirhindi’s father and what was he known for?

Ans: His father was Shaikh Abdul Ahad, a well-known sufi who made sure his son learned the Holy Quran, Hadith and religious theology.

Which subjects did Ahmad Sirhindi study to further his education?

Ans: He furthered his studies in Hadith, Tafseer and philosophy.

About what did Ahmad Sirhindi correspond with other scholars?

Ans: About the relationship of mysticism with Islamic Shariah (laws).

What was Ahmad Sirhindi’s main aim as a reformer?
  • Phrasing 1: What was Ahmad Sirhindi’s main aim as a reformer?
  • Phrasing 2: What did Mujaddid Alf-e-Sani try to purify?

Ans: To purify Muslim practices and beliefs that had become mixed with the beliefs and practices of other religions in the region.

Which religion did Emperor Akbar introduce, and why?
  • Phrasing 1: Which religion did Emperor Akbar introduce, and why?
  • Phrasing 2: What was Deen-e-Illahi?

Ans: Akbar introduced Deen-e-Illahi, which mixed Hindu and Muslim practices, to reduce the religious differences among his subjects.

How did Ahmad Sirhindi try to influence the Muslims and nobles?

Ans: As a gifted writer he used oration and wrote letters to the nobles in Akbar’s court, urging Muslims to fulfil their religious duties.

Why was Ahmad Sirhindi given the title Mujaddid Alf-e-Sani?

Ans: Because he acted as a reviver of the true essence of Islam in India.

Name some ways in which Ahmad Sirhindi worked to educate the Muslims.

Ans: By organising the Naqshbandiya order, giving sermons and lectures, meeting other scholars, and writing books.

Name the famous book written by Ahmad Sirhindi.

Ans: He wrote the famous book Tauheed-e-Shuhudi.

Which Mughal emperor noticed Ahmad Sirhindi’s work, and how was he different from his father?

Ans: Emperor Jehangir noticed his work; Jehangir was more orthodox (stricter in religion) than his father Akbar.

Why was Ahmad Sirhindi imprisoned, and for how long?

Ans: He was imprisoned for two years because he refused to bow before Emperor Jehangir — a rule that applied to all subjects.

What happened after Jehangir realised his mistake?

Ans: He released Ahmad Sirhindi and went on to reverse all the un-Islamic laws that Akbar had introduced.

In which year did Ahmad Sirhindi pass away?

Ans: He passed away in 1624 CE, preaching Islam even in his last days.

3. Shah Waliullah (1703–1762 CE)

In which years was Shah Waliullah born and when did he die?

Ans: He lived from 1703 to 1762 CE.

Where and in which subjects was Shah Waliullah educated?

Ans: In his father’s madrasah, Madrasah-e-Rahimia, in Tafseer-e-Quran, Hadith, sciences, logic and philosophy.

Into which language did Shah Waliullah translate the Holy Quran, and why?
  • Phrasing 1: Into which language did Shah Waliullah translate the Holy Quran, and why?
  • Phrasing 2: Which translation of the Quran did Shah Waliullah make?

Ans: Into Persian, because it was widely read and understood by the Muslims of India at that time, which made religion easier to understand.

What did Shah Waliullah try to restore among the Muslims?

Ans: The traditional values of the Islamic faith.

How did Shah Waliullah try to unite the Muslims?

Ans: Through his speeches and writings he tried to unite the different sects and build a strong sense of Muslim brotherhood.

What did Shah Waliullah warn the Muslims against?

Ans: Against the un-Islamic practices that were common at that time.

Which version of Islam did Shah Waliullah refer to?

Ans: The purest version of Islam, based on the simplicity and social justice of the days of Hazrat Muhammad (SAW).

How many books did Shah Waliullah write?

Ans: He wrote fifty-four books on different aspects of Islam.

Name Shah Waliullah’s famous book and its subject.

Ans: "Hujjat-ullah-al-Baligha", which explains the religious, moral, social and economic causes of the decline of the Muslims.

According to Shah Waliullah, what was the main reason for the decline of the Muslims?

Ans: That they had become prey to social evils and luxuries.

Whom did Shah Waliullah invite to India, and why?
  • Phrasing 1: Whom did Shah Waliullah invite to India, and why?
  • Phrasing 2: Whom did Shah Waliullah call to defeat the Marathas?

Ans: He invited Ahmed Shah Abdali to free the Muslims from indulgent rulers and to defeat the growing power of the Marathas.

What did Shah Waliullah want to achieve by inviting Ahmed Shah Abdali?

Ans: To reform and strengthen Muslim rule in India.

What did Shah Waliullah remind Muslims about the Islamic way of life?

Ans: That the Islamic way of life was different from that of other religions.

4. Haji Shariatullah and the Faraizi Movement (1781–1840 CE)

In which years did Haji Shariatullah live?

Ans: From 1781 to 1840 CE.

Where and when was Haji Shariatullah born?

Ans: In Faridpur, Bengal, in 1781.

Where did Haji Shariatullah go to study religion?

Ans: He went to Arabia to study religion.

What did Haji Shariatullah see on returning to India?

Ans: He was disappointed to see the poor condition of the Muslims of India.

Why had the East India Company taken control of Bengal?

Ans: To acquire agricultural land and to use the cheap labour of the area.

After which battle were the Muslims of Bengal facing hardships?

Ans: After the Battle of Plassey.

What changed the social fabric of Muslim life in Bengal?

Ans: Hindu dominance and Christian missionary activities.

When did the Faraizi Movement start and who led it?

Ans: It started in 1819 and was led by Haji Shariatullah.

What does the word "Faraiz" mean?

Ans: "Faraiz" means the commandments or duties of Allah.

What did Haji Shariatullah urge the Muslims to do?

Ans: To give up non-Islamic practices and follow only the commandments (Faraiz) of Allah.

What did the Faraizi Movement achieve?

Ans: It revived the moral and social conditions of the Muslims of Bengal and protected the rights of the peasants; it was a peaceful movement that grew rapidly.

Who continued the movement after Haji Shariatullah’s death?
  • Phrasing 1: Who continued the movement after Haji Shariatullah’s death?
  • Phrasing 2: By which name was Mohsin-ud-Din famously known?

Ans: His son Mohsin-ud-Din, famously known as Dadu Miyan.

For whom did Dadu Miyan (Mohsin-ud-Din) fight?

Ans: For the rights of the peasants who were oppressed by Hindu landlords.

When did the Faraizi Movement come to an end?

Ans: It ended with Mohsin-ud-Din’s death in 1862.

5. Syed Ahmed Shaheed Barelvi and the Jihad Movement (1786–1831 CE)

In which years did Syed Ahmed Shaheed Barelvi live?

Ans: From 1786 to 1831 CE.

Where and when was Syed Ahmed Shaheed Barelvi born?

Ans: In Northern India in 1786 CE.

Where was Syed Ahmed Barelvi educated?

Ans: In the Madrasah Rahimiyya, which was established by Shah Waliullah’s father.

Who ruled the subcontinent and the western subcontinent in the early 19th century?

Ans: The British ruled the subcontinent, while the western subcontinent was under Sikh rule.

When did Syed Ahmed Barelvi return from Arabia?

Ans: He returned from Arabia in 1823.

What movement did Syed Ahmed Barelvi start, and what was its aim?

Ans: He started the Jihad Movement, aiming to replace Sikh and British rule with Muslim rule.

What was Syed Ahmed Barelvi’s dream for his people?

Ans: He wanted to see the Muslims as a free nation.

When did Syed Ahmed gather his army, and what were its members called?

Ans: In 1826 CE he gathered an army of volunteers known as Mujahideen.

Why was the morale of the Muslims very high at that time?

Ans: Because they believed they could take action to safeguard their religious beliefs.

Which important city did the Muslim Mujahideen capture?

Ans: They captured Peshawar, where a Muslim state under Shariah rule was established.

Why did the Muslim state in Peshawar not last long?

Ans: Syed Ahmed was pushed back to Kashmir by the Sikh ruler Ranjit Singh.

How and where was Syed Ahmed Barelvi martyred?

Ans: He was martyred in a surprise attack by the Sikh army in Balakot (Khyber Pakhtunkhwa).

What kind of mind did Syed Ahmed Barelvi have?

Ans: He had a revolutionary mind and wanted to do something to help his people.

6. Condition of Muslims after 1857 and Sir Syed Ahmed Khan (1817–1898 CE)

Why did the British hold the Muslims responsible for the War of Independence?

Ans: Because of the Muslims’ support for the restoration of the Mughal Emperor.

What happened to the condition of the Muslims after 1857?

Ans: It became worse; they lost all respectable positions in government jobs.

Why were the Muslims discouraged from acquiring education after 1857?

Ans: Because of the East India Company’s education policies that promoted Christian values, and because Muslims saw modern education as an un-Islamic act.

How did the Hindus respond to Western education under the British Raj?

Ans: They took the opportunity of Western education and the benefits it offered.

Who undertook efforts to lead the Muslims towards reform and progress?

Ans: Sir Syed Ahmad Khan.

Where and when was Sir Syed Ahmed Khan born?

Ans: In Delhi in 1817.

What post did Sir Syed hold in the East India Company?

Ans: He became a local judge in 1857.

What did Sir Syed realise the Muslims needed to do to compete with the Hindus and the British?

Ans: They needed to overcome their ignorance and their resistance to modern ideas.

What did Sir Syed try to give the Muslims?

Ans: A separate identity.

What foundation did Sir Syed’s "Two Nation Theory" later become?

Ans: It became the foundation for the Pakistan Movement decades later.

Which pamphlet did Sir Syed write, and what did it argue?

Ans: "Causes of the Indian Revolt", which argued that the Muslims should not be blamed for the revolt.

To whom did Sir Syed send copies of his pamphlet?

Ans: To the Members of Parliament.

What was Sir Syed’s three-point agenda for the success of the Muslims?

Ans: To stay away from politics, to bridge the gap between the British and themselves, and to get educated so as to be eligible for government jobs.

7. Educational Services of Sir Syed and the Aligarh Movement

Why is Sir Syed Ahmed Khan important in the field of education?

Ans: He was the first Muslim leader to realise the importance of education for the Muslims.

Where did Sir Syed establish two madrasahs, and what did they teach?

Ans: In Muradabad and Ghaziabad; they taught modern education in English, Urdu and Persian.

What was the Scientific Society, and when was it established?

Ans: It was set up in 1864 to translate science books into Urdu.

Why did Sir Syed start the Aligarh Movement?

Ans: To remove misconceptions among the Muslims about British rule and policies.

When was the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental School established?

Ans: It was established in 1875.

What did the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental School later become?
  • Phrasing 1: What did the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental School later become?
  • Phrasing 2: What did the MAO School grow into?

Ans: It became Aligarh College in 1877, and later Aligarh University, chartered by the government in 1920.

In which year did the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental School become Aligarh College?

Ans: In 1877.

When and by whom was Aligarh University chartered?

Ans: It was chartered by the government in 1920.

When did Sir Syed inaugurate the Muhammadan Educational Conference, and what was its aim?

Ans: In 1886; its aim was to set up Islamic high schools in almost every town and city across India.

What did Sir Syed write about in Tehzib-ul-Ikhlaq?

Ans: He consistently wrote about ethics and stressed the importance of Urdu to the identity of the Muslim nation.

Which controversy influenced Sir Syed’s political views, and when?

Ans: The Hindi-Urdu controversy of 1867.

How did the Hindi-Urdu controversy affect Sir Syed?

Ans: It motivated him to publish extensively in Urdu and to provide modern education to the Muslims.

What unfairness did Sir Syed point out about the Civil Service Examinations?

Ans: They were held in England, so Indian candidates could not compete fairly with English candidates.

Why did Sir Syed oppose the Muslims joining the Indian National Congress?

Ans: Because he believed that the Muslims should have a separate voice in India.

What did Sir Syed propose in 1883, and what did it become the basis of?

Ans: He proposed a separate electorate for the Muslims, which became the basis of the Two Nation Theory.

What spirit motivated the students of Aligarh?

Ans: The spirit of Muslim Nationalism; they became pioneers of the Two Nation Theory.

What was the long-term result of the Aligarh Movement?

Ans: The intellectual revival of the Indian Muslims and, eventually, the creation of Pakistan.

When did Sir Syed Ahmed Khan pass away?

Ans: He passed away in 1898, before his dream came true.

8. Darul-Uloom Deoband (1866)

When was Darul-Uloom Deoband set up, and by whom?

Ans: It was set up in 1866 by several Muslim Ulema.

Where is Darul-Uloom Deoband located, and who headed it?

Ans: It is located in Deoband, India, and was headed by Maulana Muhammad Qasim Nanotvi.

Why was Darul-Uloom Deoband established?

Ans: To promote a traditional and progressive education for the Muslims.

On which syllabus was the Deoband curriculum based?

Ans: On the 17th-century Indo-Islamic syllabus known as "Dars-e-Nizami".

Was English taught at Darul-Uloom Deoband?

Ans: Yes, English was offered as an option to students who wished to study it.

What did Darul-Uloom Deoband provide for the independence movement?

Ans: It provided many prominent leaders for the independence movement.

9. Nadvat-ul-Ulema (1892)

When and where was Nadvat-ul-Ulema established?

Ans: It was established in 1892 at Kanpur.

Where was Nadvat-ul-Ulema later shifted?

Ans: It was later shifted to Lucknow in 1898 CE.

Name the renowned founder members of Nadvat-ul-Ulema.

Ans: Muhammad Ali Mongiri, Ashraf Ali Thanwi, Mahmud-ul-Hassan and Shibli Nomani.

What was the main objective of Nadvat-ul-Ulema?

Ans: To create harmony between Islamic education and modern demands, and to meet the challenges of Western education.

How was the curriculum of Nadvat-ul-Ulema improved?

Ans: With the help of eminent scholars, its Islamic curriculum was updated with the modern curriculum.

What challenge did Nadvat-ul-Ulema aim to meet?

Ans: The challenges of Western education.

10. Hindu Revivalist Movements — Bhakti, Sikhism, Arya Samaj

What was the Bhakti Movement, and when did it originate?

Ans: It was a Hindu religious movement that originated in the 8th century.

What does "Bhakti" mean?

Ans: "Bhakti" means devotion to God.

What did the Bhakti Movement believe in and reject?

Ans: It believed in the equality of men and rejected the caste system, idol worship and the worship of multiple gods.

To which religion did the Bhakti Movement give birth?

Ans: It gave birth to Sikhism.

What does the word "Sikh" literally mean?

Ans: The word "Sikh" literally means "learner".

Who founded Sikhism, and where?

Ans: Guru Nanak (1469–1539 CE) founded Sikhism in the Punjab.

What were the most famous teachings of Guru Nanak?

Ans: That there is only one God and that all human beings are equal; he condemned the Hindu caste system.

When and by whom was the Arya Samaj founded?

Ans: It was founded in Bombay in 1875 by Dayananda Sarasvati.

What did Dayananda Sarasvati believe about the Vedas?

Ans: He believed that the earliest Hindu scriptures, the Vedas, were the ultimate truth.

What does "Arya Samaj" mean, and why was it called so?

Ans: It is Sanskrit for "Society of Nobles", so called because it was organised in "samajas" (societies), each headed by democratically elected officers.

What did the Arya Samaj oppose?

Ans: Idol worship, animal sacrifice, temple offerings, and basing caste on birth instead of merit; it was monotheistic.

How did the reforms of the Arya Samaj affect Indian society?

Ans: They spread education, encouraged female education and social reform, and helped the growth of Indian nationalism.
