Part 1

Types of Mountains

What mountains are and how fold, block, residual and volcanic mountains form.

Mountains and Fold Mountains

1. What is a mountain? How are mountains classified?
• Define a mountain• On what basis are mountains divided into types?
A mountain is a large landform that rises steeply above its surroundings, to a peak or ridge above 600 metres.

Mountains are classified on the basis of how they are formed into four types:
1. Fold mountains — folding
2. Block mountains — faulting
3. Residual mountains — weathering and erosion
4. Volcanic mountains — volcanic eruption
2. How are fold mountains formed? Give examples.
• Explain the process of folding• How were the Himalayas formed?
Fold mountains form when two plates collide and the soft sedimentary rocks between them bend or buckle up — this process is called folding, caused by compressional forces, over millions of years.

The Himalayas formed when the Indian Plate moved towards the Eurasian Plate, 40 to 50 million years ago. Other examples: the Alps (Europe), Andes (South America), Rockies (North America) and Urals (Russia).

Block, Residual and Volcanic Mountains

3. How are block mountains formed? What is a horst?
• Explain faulting and block mountains• Name Pakistan's major faults
Block mountains form by faulting — when rocks develop cracks, the side blocks are pushed down and the central block is pushed up. A block mountain with a flat top is called a horst.

Pakistan's major fault lines: the Chaman Fault (west), the Pab Fault (south) and the Main Mantle Thrust (north).
4. How are residual and volcanic mountains formed?
• What are residual mountains?• Give examples of volcanic mountains
Residual mountains are the harder rocks left behind after long weathering and erosion wear away the soft rocks. Examples: Girnar hills, Nilgiri hills (India) and Sierra of Spain.

Volcanic mountains form when volcanoes erupt and pile layers of magma on the surface. Examples: Mount Vesuvius (Italy), Mount Fuji (Japan), Mount Kilimanjaro (Africa), Mount Pinatubo (Philippines) and Mount Erebus (Antarctica).
Part 2

Mountains of Pakistan & Earthquakes

The Himalayas, Karakoram and Hindu Kush, and why Pakistan has frequent earthquakes.

Northern Ranges of Pakistan

5. Describe the location and importance of the Himalayas, Karakoram and Hindu Kush.
• Name the three ranges the Himalayas split into• Which rivers start from these ranges?
The Himalayas are like a wall separating the subcontinent from central Asia and extend nearly 320 km into Pakistan, splitting (south to north) into the Pir Panjal, Zaskar and Ladakh ranges. To the north lies the Karakoram and to its west the Hindu Kush.

Melting snow feeds the rivers: Jhelum (Pir Panjal), Indus (Zaskar & Ladakh) and Shyok (Karakoram). The Karakoram Highway — the highest paved road, the "eighth wonder of the world" — is the only overland link between Pakistan and China.

Earthquakes in Pakistan

6. Why do earthquakes occur frequently in Pakistan?
• Why are the Himalayas still growing?• Describe the 2005 earthquake
Pakistan lies at the boundary of two continental plates — the Indian Plate and the Eurasian Plate — which cuts through the centre of the country in a north-east to south-west direction. Because both plates have the same density, folding occurred and formed the Himalayas.

Convergent movement continues, so the Himalayas rise about 1 cm every year and earthquakes occur frequently. One of Pakistan's deadliest earthquakes struck Azad Jammu and Kashmir in 2005 (7.6 on the Richter Scale, about 79,000 dead).
Part 3

Plateaus & Valleys

Plateaus, the types of valleys, and the famous valleys of Pakistan.

Plateaus

7. What is a plateau? Name the plateaus of Pakistan.
• How is a plateau different from a mountain?• Which is the largest plateau in the world?
A plateau is a raised, flat-surfaced landform, also called table land or a flat-topped mountain (about 500 m to thousands of metres high). Plateaus cover 45% of the Earth's land surface; the Tibetan Plateau (Central Asia, ~4,500 m) is the largest and highest — the "roof of the Earth".

Pakistan has two plateaus: the Potohar Plateau (Jhelum on the East, Indus on the West, Margalla Hills on the North, Salt Range on the South) and the Balochistan Plateau (west of the Sulaiman and Kirthar ranges).

Valleys

8. What are the three types of valleys and how does each form?
• Explain V-shaped and U-shaped valleys• What is a rift valley?
A valley is a long depression surrounded by higher ground.

1. V-shaped valley — cut by rivers and streams into a narrow, steep "V" (a river valley), e.g. the Chitral Valley.
2. U-shaped valley — eroded and widened by glaciers into a broad "U" (a glacier valley).
3. Rift valley — an elongated lowland between two highlands formed by faulting, e.g. the East African Rift Valley system.
9. Name and describe some famous valleys of Pakistan.
• Which valley has the world's highest polo ground?• Where is the Ladyfinger peak?
Chitral Valley (KPK) — a V-shaped valley at 1,128 m, famous for the world's highest polo ground at Shandur.
Kaghan Valley (KPK) — glaciers, lakes and waterfalls; visited in summer.
Murree — in the Himalaya (Pir Panjal) range, ~30 km NE of Islamabad, built in British rule.
Hunza (Gilgit Baltistan) — famous for its scenery and the Ladyfinger peak.
Bagrot (Karakoram range) — known for fruits and vegetables.
Naran (KPK) — 2,500 m above sea level.
10. How do lifestyles differ on mountains, plateaus and valleys? Why are the Himalayas important?
• Compare Ahmed, Faisal and Abdullah• How do the Himalayas affect Pakistan's rainfall?
People at high altitude face harsh conditions. Ahmed (mountains) farms on terraces and travels by jeep or donkey; Faisal (Potohar Plateau) grows wheat and cotton and works at the Attock oil refinery; Abdullah (Pir Panjal) does trans-human farming.

The Himalayas are rich in minerals (limestone, rock salt, marble) and, with the Karakoram, act as a barrier for the monsoon winds, affecting Pakistan's precipitation pattern.
📝 Landforms are shaped by three processes: folding, faulting and volcanic eruptions.
