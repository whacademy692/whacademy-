Part 1 MATH-01

Scientific Notation

Section 2.1 — write huge and tiny numbers as \(a\times10^{n}\), and convert back.

Key points
  • Scientific notation: \(a\times10^{n}\), with \(1\le a<10\) and \(n\) an integer.
  • Number ≥ 10: \(n = \) (digits before the point) − 1. Number < 1: \(n=-(\text{zeros after the point}+1)\).
  • Power of 10 = characteristic (used again in Section 2.3).

Writing numbers as a × 10ⁿ

1. What is scientific notation?
• Define scientific notation• What is the standard form of a number?

A very large or very small number written in the form

\(a \times 10^{n}\)

where \(1 \le a < 10\) (exactly one non-zero digit before the decimal point) and \(n\) is an integer. Here \(a\) is the coefficient and \(n\) is the power of 10.

Example: \(78{,}000{,}000 = 7.8\times10^{7}\) and \(0.000006 = 6\times10^{-6}\).

📝 If the number is ≥ 10 the power is positive; if it is < 1 the power is negative; if it lies between 1 and 10 the power is 0.
2. How do I convert a LARGE number into scientific notation? (Decimal Hop trick)
• Express 78,000,000 in scientific notation• Convert a large number to a × 10ⁿ
Short Trick — “Decimal Hop”
  • Put the decimal point just after the first non-zero digit — this gives the coefficient \(a\).
  • Count how many places the point hopped.
  • Big number → point hopped LEFT → power is +hops.

Even faster: for a number \(\ge 1\), power \(n =\) (number of digits before the point) \(-1\).

78,000,000 has 8 digits → \(n = 8-1 = 7\)
\(78{,}000{,}000 = 7.8\times10^{7}\)
5,230,000 has 7 digits → \(n=6\) → \(5.23\times10^{6}\)
\(4{,}500\) has 4 digits → \(n=3\) → \(4.5\times10^{3}\)
📝 Yaad rakhein: “Bara number → point left → power plus.”
3. How do I convert a SMALL number (less than 1) into scientific notation?
• Express 0.000006 in scientific notation• Write 0.0000000315 in the form a × 10ⁿ

Hop the point to just after the first non-zero digit — this time it moves to the right, so the power is negative.

Even faster: for a number \(<1\), power \(n = -(\text{zeros after the point}+1)\).

0.000006 → 6 hops to the right → \(6\times10^{-6}\)
0.0000000315 has 7 zeros after the point → \(n=-(7+1)=-8\) → \(3.15\times10^{-8}\)
0.00042 has 3 zeros → \(n=-4\) → \(4.2\times10^{-4}\)
📝 “Chhota number → point right → power minus.”
4. How do I convert scientific notation back to an ordinary number?
• Convert 3.47 × 10⁶ to ordinary notation• Convert 5.2 × 10⁻⁴ to an ordinary number
Short Trick — “Power tells the direction”
  • Power positive → move the decimal point RIGHT that many places (add zeros).
  • Power negative → move the decimal point LEFT that many places (add zeros in front).
\(3.47\times10^{6}\) → move 6 places right → 3,470,000
\(5.2\times10^{-4}\) → move 4 places left → 0.00052
\(1.0\times10^{3}\) → 1000
⛔ Check the size: a negative power must give a number smaller than 1, a positive power a number bigger than 1.

• Express the diameter of Mars in standard form• Express the diameter of Earth in standard form

6. Why is the power of 10 so important for logarithms?
• What is the link between scientific notation and characteristic?
Short Trick — The power of 10 IS the characteristic

In Part 3 you will meet the characteristic of a logarithm. It is simply the power \(n\) in \(N=a\times10^{n}\). So if you master this section, you already know half of Section 2.3.

\(59.28 = 5.928\times10^{1}\) → characteristic \(=1\)
\(0.0567 = 5.67\times10^{-2}\) → characteristic \(=-2\)
Part 2 MATH-02

Meaning of Logarithm

Section 2.2 — a logarithm is just a power. Convert between the two forms and find unknowns.

Key points
  • Log ⇄ exponential: \(\log_{b}x=y\iff b^{y}=x\) (b > 0, b ≠ 1, x > 0).
  • Log ka jawab = Power. \(\log_{b}1=0\), \(\log_{b}b=1\), \(\log0\) undefined.
  • To evaluate: break the number into powers of the base.

Logarithm — meaning, forms and values

1. What is a logarithm? Define it.
• Define logarithm• What is meant by log_b x = y?• Who introduced the word logarithm?

The logarithm of a number \(x\) to the base \(b\) is the power to which \(b\) must be raised to get \(x\):

\(\log_{b}x = y \iff b^{y}=x\)

Conditions: \(b>0\), \(b\ne1\), \(x>0\).

📝 The word logarithm was introduced by the Scottish mathematician John Napier (logos = ratio, arithmos = number). Log ka jawab = Power.

Example: \(\log_{2}8\) asks “2 to which power gives 8?” — since \(2^{3}=8\), \(\log_{2}8=3\).

2. Why can the base not be 1, and why must x be positive?
• Why is the base of a logarithm never 1?• Is log 0 defined?

If \(b=1\), then \(1^{y}=1\) for every \(y\), so \(1^{y}\) can never give any number other than 1 — the logarithm would be meaningless.

A positive base raised to any power always gives a positive result, so we can never get 0 or a negative number. Hence \(\log 0\) and the log of a negative number are undefined (in real numbers).

Log form: \(\log_{b}x=y\) → base stays the base; the number inside the log is the result; the answer after “=” is the power.

4. How do I evaluate a logarithm like log₂ 8, log₃ 81 or log₅ (1/125)?

  • Whole number: \(8=2^{3}\), \(81=3^{4}\), \(125=5^{3}\).
  • Fraction → negative power: \(\tfrac{1}{27}=3^{-3}\).
  • Root → fractional power: \(3=9^{1/2}\).
\(\log_{2}8\): \(8=2^{3}\) → 3

Part 3 MATH-03 MATH-04 MATH-05

Common Logarithm

Section 2.3 — characteristic, mantissa, log table, antilog table and natural logarithm.

Key points
  • log N = characteristic + mantissa; mantissa is always positive and depends only on the digits.
  • Characteristic: number ≥ 1 → (digits before the point) − 1; number < 1 → −(zeros after the point + 1), written with a bar.
  • R-C-M: Row (first 2 digits) · Column (3rd digit) · Mean difference (4th digit) — then add the characteristic.
  • Antilog: same R-C-M, then place the point: char \(n\ge0\) → \(n+1\) digits before the point; \(\bar n\) → \(n-1\) zeros after it.

Common & natural logarithm, characteristic, mantissa

1. What is a common logarithm? How is it different from a natural logarithm?
• Define common logarithm• Differentiate between common and natural logarithm• Who introduced the symbol e?

A common logarithm has base 10 and is written \(\log_{10}x\) or simply \(\log x\). It was developed by Henry Briggs, who also introduced the logarithm table. A natural logarithm has base e and is written \(\ln x\).

Common logarithmNatural logarithm
Base10\(e\approx2.71828\)
Notation\(\log_{10}x\) or \(\log x\)\(\ln x=\log_{e}x\)
Used ineveryday and scientific calculations (log tables)higher mathematics — calculus, growth and decay
📝 The letter e was introduced by the Swiss mathematician Leonhard Euler.
2. How do I evaluate natural logarithms like ln e⁴, ln(1/e) and 2 ln e + ln 1?
• Find ln e⁴• What are ln 1 and ln e?

Special values: \(\ln1=0\), \(\ln e=1\), \(\ln e^{n}=n\), \(\ln0\) undefined.

\(\ln e^{4}=4\ln e=4\times1=\mathbf4\)
\(\ln\tfrac1e=\ln e^{-1}=-1\times\ln e=\mathbf{-1}\)
\(2\ln e+\ln1=2(1)+0=\mathbf2\)
📝 Extra: the two logs are linked by the change-of-base law — \(\ln x=\dfrac{\log x}{\log e}=\dfrac{\log x}{0.4343}\approx2.303\log x\).
3. What are the characteristic and the mantissa of a logarithm?
• Define characteristic and mantissa• What is log N = char + mantissa?

Every common logarithm is written as

\(\log N=\text{characteristic}+\text{mantissa}\)
  • The characteristic is the integral part; it tells how big or small the number is.
  • The mantissa is the decimal part; it is always positive (between 0 and 1) and is found from the log table.

Example: \(\log 234.7=2.3705\) → characteristic \(2\), mantissa \(0.3705\).

Short Trick — Characteristic in 2 seconds
  • Number ≥ 1: count the digits before the point, then minus 1.
  • Number < 1: count the zeros right after the point, plus 1, and put a bar (negative sign) on it.

5. What is a “bar number” like \(\bar2.5433\)?
• What does the bar over a characteristic mean?

The bar is written only over the characteristic: \(\bar2\) means \(-2\). The mantissa stays positive.

\(\bar2.5433=-2+0.5433=-1.4567\)
⛔ Common mistake: giving the mantissa a negative sign. Mantissa is always positive — only the characteristic carries the bar.
6. Does the mantissa change if the decimal point moves?
• If log 3.177 = 0.5019, find log 3177• Find log 31.77 and log 0.03177
Short Trick — Mantissa depends ONLY on the digits

Numbers with the same digits (e.g. 3177, 31.77, 3.177, 0.03177) have the same mantissa. Only the characteristic changes.

Exercise 2.3 Q3: If \(\log3.177=0.5019\) (value given in the book), find \(\log3177\), \(\log31.77\) and \(\log0.03177\).

\(\log3177 = 3+0.5019=\mathbf{3.5019}\) (4 digits → char 3)
\(\log31.77=1+0.5019=\mathbf{1.5019}\) (2 digits → char 1)
\(\log0.03177=\bar2.5019\;(=-1.4981)\) (1 zero → char \(\bar2\))

Using the log table and antilog table

7. How do I read the LOG TABLE? (R-C-M trick)
• Find log 13.45 using the log table• How do you use the mean difference column?
Short Trick — “R-C-M”: Row · Column · Mean difference
  1. Round the number to 4 digits and ignore the decimal point.
  2. Row = first 2 digits.
  3. Column = 3rd digit → read the 4-digit entry.
  4. Mean difference = 4th digit, read in the “Mean Differences” columns of the same row → add it to the entry.
  5. Put the mantissa as 0.abcd and add the characteristic in front.

Example: find \(\log13.45\).

Characteristic = (digits before the point) − 1 = 1
Mantissa: digits 1345 → Row 13, Column 4 = 1271; Mean difference (row 13, column 5) = 16 → 1271 + 16 = 1287 → mantissa = 0.1287
log 13.45 = \(1.1287\)

9. How do I convert a NEGATIVE log (like −1.4567) into bar form? (9-complement trick)
• Convert −1.4567 into bar form

The antilog table needs a positive mantissa, so a negative decimal must first be changed to bar form.

Short Trick — 9-complement trick
  • Characteristic = −(integral part + 1) → write it with a bar.
  • Mantissa: subtract every digit from 9, but the last digit from 10.
\(-1.4567\) → characteristic \(=-(1+1)=\bar2\)
Mantissa: 9−4=5, 9−5=4, 9−6=3, 10−7=3 → 0.5433
So \(-1.4567=\bar2.5433\). Check: \(-2+0.5433=-1.4567\) ✓
10. How do I add, subtract, multiply and divide bar numbers?
• Divide 2̄.4564 by 3• How do you multiply a bar number?
Short Trick — Arithmetic with bar numbers

Treat the characteristic and mantissa separately; any carry from the mantissa goes into the characteristic.

Add: \(\bar2.7000+3.5000\): mantissas \(0.7+0.5=1.2\) → carry 1; \(-2+3+1=2\) → \(2.2000\)
Subtract: \(1.2000-2.7000=-1.5000\) → bar form: characteristic \(-(1+1)=\bar2\), mantissa \(1-0.5000=0.5000\) → \(\bar2.5000\)
Multiply: \(3\times\bar2.5000=3(-2+0.5)=-6+1.5=-4.5=\bar5.5000\)
Divide: \(\bar2.4564\div3\): make the characteristic divisible — \(-2+0.4564=-3+1.4564\) → \(\div3\) → \(-1+0.4855=\bar1.4855\)

Part 4 MATH-06 MATH-07

Laws of Logarithm

Section 2.4 — product, quotient, power and change-of-base laws; expanding, single logarithm and equations.

Key points
  • Product: \(\log_{b}(xy)=\log_{b}x+\log_{b}y\) · Quotient: \(\log_{b}\frac xy=\log_{b}x-\log_{b}y\)
  • Power: \(\log_{b}x^{n}=n\log_{b}x\) · Change of base: \(\log_{b}x=\frac{\log_{a}x}{\log_{a}b}\)
  • There is no law for \(\log(x+y)\) or \(\log(x-y)\).
  • Must-remember: \(\log2=0.3010,\ \log3=0.4771,\ \log5=0.6990,\ \log7=0.8451\).

Laws, proofs and using them

1. What are the laws of logarithms? State them with proof.
• State the product law• Prove that log_b (xy) = log_b x + log_b y• State the change of base law• Prove the power law of logarithm
LawStatement
Product law\(\log_{b}(xy)=\log_{b}x+\log_{b}y\)
Quotient law\(\log_{b}\!\left(\tfrac xy\right)=\log_{b}x-\log_{b}y\)
Power law\(\log_{b}(x^{n})=n\log_{b}x\) (roots: \(\sqrt x=x^{1/2}\), \(\sqrt[3]x=x^{1/3}\))
Change of base\(\log_{b}x=\dfrac{\log_{a}x}{\log_{a}b}\)

Proof of the product law. Let \(m=\log_{b}x\) and \(n=\log_{b}y\). Then \(x=b^{m}\), \(y=b^{n}\).

\(xy=b^{m}\cdot b^{n}=b^{m+n}\)
\(\Rightarrow\log_{b}(xy)=m+n=\log_{b}x+\log_{b}y\)

Proof of the quotient law. With the same \(m,n\): \(\dfrac xy=\dfrac{b^{m}}{b^{n}}=b^{m-n}\) → \(\log_{b}\dfrac xy=m-n=\log_{b}x-\log_{b}y\).

Proof of the power law. Let \(m=\log_{b}x\) → \(x=b^{m}\). Then \(x^{n}=(b^{m})^{n}=b^{mn}\) → \(\log_{b}x^{n}=mn=n\log_{b}x\).

Proof of change of base. Let \(y=\log_{b}x\) → \(x=b^{y}\). Take \(\log_{a}\) of both sides: \(\log_{a}x=y\log_{a}b\) → \(y=\dfrac{\log_{a}x}{\log_{a}b}\).

2. Which “laws” are NOT true? (the fake-law trap)
• Is log(x + y) = log x + log y?
❌ Wrong✅ Correct
\(\log(x+y)=\log x+\log y\)There is no law for \(\log(x+y)\); only \(\log(xy)=\log x+\log y\)
\(\log(x-y)=\log x-\log y\)Only \(\log\frac xy=\log x-\log y\)
\(\dfrac{\log x}{\log y}=\log x-\log y\)\(\dfrac{\log x}{\log y}=\log_{y}x\) (change of base)
\((\log x)^{n}=n\log x\)\(\log(x^{n})=n\log x\)
3. How do I expand a logarithm using the laws?
• Expand log (x y z⁶)• Expand log₃ ⁶√(m⁵n³) using laws of logarithms
Short Trick — Expand: root → power, then power law, then product/quotient

Write roots as fractional powers first.

(i) \(\log(xyz^{6})=\log x+\log y+6\log z\)
(ii) \(\log_{3}\sqrt[6]{m^{5}n^{3}}=\tfrac16\log_{3}(m^{5}n^{3})=\tfrac16(5\log_{3}m+3\log_{3}n)=\tfrac56\log_{3}m+\tfrac12\log_{3}n\)
(iii) \(\log\sqrt{8x^{3}}=\tfrac12(\log8+3\log x)=\tfrac12\log8+\tfrac32\log x\)
4. How do I write an expression as a SINGLE logarithm? (Expand ⇄ Single switch)
• Write 7 log x − 3 log y² as a single logarithm• Write 3 log 4 − log 32 as a single log
Short Trick — Single log
  • Push every coefficient up as a power (\(n\log x=\log x^{n}\)).
  • + → multiply, − → divide.
(i) \(7\log x-3\log y^{2}=\log x^{7}-\log y^{6}=\log\dfrac{x^{7}}{y^{6}}\)
(ii) \(3\log4-\log32=\log64-\log32=\log\dfrac{64}{32}=\log2\)
(iii) \(\tfrac13(\log_{5}8+\log_{5}27)-\log_{5}3=\tfrac13\log_{5}216-\log_{5}3=\log_{5}6-\log_{5}3=\log_{5}2\)
5. How do I evaluate logs “without a calculator” using the laws?
• Evaluate log₂ 18 − log₂ 9• Evaluate log₂ 64 + log₂ 2

Use the laws to combine, then break the number into powers of the base.

(i) \(\log_{2}18-\log_{2}9=\log_{2}\tfrac{18}{9}=\log_{2}2=\mathbf1\)
(ii) \(\log_{2}64+\log_{2}2=\log_{2}128=\mathbf7\) (since \(2^{7}=128\))
(iii) \(\tfrac13\log_{3}8-\log_{3}18=\log_{3}2-\log_{3}18=\log_{3}\tfrac{2}{18}=\log_{3}3^{-2}=\mathbf{-2}\)
6. Is there a shortcut to get many logs without the table? (log 2, log 3, log 7)

7. How do I solve logarithmic equations? (4-step trick)
• Solve log₂ x + log₂ (x − 2) = 3• Solve a logarithmic equation
Short Trick — Solving log equations in 4 steps
  1. Combine all logs of the same base into one log (single-log switch).
  2. Change to exponential form.
  3. Solve the resulting equation.
  4. Check: every log argument must stay positive — reject answers that make it 0 or negative.
Solve: \(\log_{2}x+\log_{2}(x-2)=3\)
\(\log_{2}[x(x-2)]=3\) → \(x(x-2)=2^{3}=8\)
\(x^{2}-2x-8=0\) → \((x-4)(x+2)=0\) → \(x=4\) or \(x=-2\)
Check: \(x=-2\) makes \(\log_{2}x\) undefined → reject. Answer: \(x=4\).

When both sides are powers of the same number, write them with the same base and equate the powers: \(2^{x+1}=32\) → \(2^{x+1}=2^{5}\) → \(x=4\).

1. What is the 6-step layout for calculations with log tables?
• Find (3.68 × 4.21)/5.234 with log table• Evaluate using logarithm table
Short Trick — 6-step layout
  1. Estimate the answer roughly first (round each number to 1 digit) — this checks your decimal point.
  2. Write “Let x = …”.
  3. Take log of both sides and use the laws: × → +, ÷ → −, power → × power, root → × 1/root.
  4. Find each log from the table (R-C-M) and write them one under another.
  5. Add/subtract (with bars if the number is below 1) to get log x.
  6. Antilog → place the point → compare with your estimate.

Board-Style Q&A and Exam Tips

Short questions that come in exams, plus the mistakes and tips that save marks.

16 short questions with answers

Q1. Define scientific notation.

Ans: A number written as \(a\times10^{n}\) where \(1\le a<10\) and \(n\) is an integer. Example: \(78{,}000{,}000=7.8\times10^{7}\).

Q2. Define logarithm.

Ans: \(\log_{b}x=y\iff b^{y}=x\) with \(b>0\), \(b\ne1\), \(x>0\): the power to which the base must be raised to get \(x\).

Q3. Write 2⁵ = 32 in logarithmic form and log₃ 9 = 2 in exponential form.

Ans: \(2^{5}=32\iff\log_{2}32=5\); \(\log_{3}9=2\iff3^{2}=9\).

Q4. Why can the base of a logarithm not be 1?

Ans: Because \(1^{y}=1\) for every \(y\), so 1 raised to any power can never give any other number.

Q5. What are log 1, log_b b and log 0?

Ans: \(\log1=0\) (\(b^0=1\)); \(\log_{b}b=1\) (\(b^1=b\)); \(\log0\) is undefined.

Q6. What is a common logarithm?

Ans: A logarithm with base 10, written \(\log_{10}\) or simply \(\log\). It was developed by Henry Briggs, who also introduced the logarithmic table.

Q7. Define characteristic and mantissa.

Ans: \(\log N=\) characteristic \(+\) mantissa. The characteristic is the integral part; the mantissa is the decimal part (always positive, found from the table).

Q8. State the rules for finding the characteristic.

Ans: Number > 1: (digits before the decimal point) − 1. Number < 1: −(zeros between the point and the first non-zero digit + 1), written with a bar.

Q9. Find the characteristic of log 0.0567 and log 234.7.

Ans: 0.0567: \(-(1+1)=-2=\bar2\). 234.7: \(3-1=2\).

Q10. What is an antilogarithm?

Ans: The inverse of a logarithm — the number whose logarithm is given. In \(4^{3}=64\), 64 is the antilogarithm.

Q11. What is a natural logarithm? How is it different from a common logarithm?

Ans: The logarithm with base \(e\approx2.71828\), written \(\ln\). A common log has base 10 and is used in everyday/scientific calculations; the natural log is used in higher mathematics (calculus, growth and decay).

Q12. State the product, quotient and power laws.

Ans: \(\log_{b}(xy)=\log_{b}x+\log_{b}y\); \(\log_{b}\frac xy=\log_{b}x-\log_{b}y\); \(\log_{b}x^{n}=n\log_{b}x\).

Q13. State the change-of-base law.

Ans: \(\log_{b}x=\dfrac{\log_{a}x}{\log_{a}b}\).

Q14. Prove the product law of logarithm.

Ans: Let \(m=\log_{b}x\), \(n=\log_{b}y\) → \(x=b^{m}\), \(y=b^{n}\). Then \(xy=b^{m+n}\) → \(\log_{b}(xy)=m+n=\log_{b}x+\log_{b}y\).

Q15. Who introduced the word logarithm?

Ans: The Scottish mathematician John Napier (logos = ratio, arithmos = number).

Q16. Who introduced the symbol e?

Ans: The Swiss mathematician and physicist Leonhard Euler.

Common mistakes
  • Forgetting the bar when the number is less than 1 (writing \(\log0.0036=3.5563\) instead of \(\bar3.5563\)).
  • Giving the mantissa a negative sign — the mantissa is always positive.
  • Forgetting to add the mean difference (4th digit), or adding it to the wrong place.
  • Placing the decimal point wrongly in an antilog.
  • Splitting \(\log(x+y)\) or \(\log(x-y)\) — there is no such law.
  • Not rejecting answers that make a log argument zero or negative.
  • Using a number with more than 4 digits directly in the table — round to 4 digits first.
  • Writing \(\log_{2}8=2^{3}\) — a log is a number: \(\log_{2}8=3\).
Exam tips
  • With tables, always write the estimate first.
  • Keep logs in a neat column and show every step — marks are given for steps.
  • Expand: root → power \(1/n\), then power law, then product/quotient laws.
  • Single log: push coefficients up as powers, then join.
  • Exponential equations: same base on both sides. Log equations: combine → exponential form → solve → check.
  • “Without a calculator” → use the laws. “With the help of logarithm table” → use the 6-step layout.

Key Vocabulary

Unit 2 Vocabulary

Important terms you must know: learn these definitions for exam questions.

TermMeaning
Scientific notationA number written as \(a\times10^{n}\) with \(1\le a<10\) and \(n\) an integer.
CoefficientThe number \(a\) in \(a\times10^{n}\) (one non-zero digit before the decimal point).
LogarithmThe power to which a base must be raised to get a number: \(\log_{b}x=y\iff b^{y}=x\).
BaseThe number \(b\) that is raised to a power; must be positive and not equal to 1.
Common logarithmLogarithm to base 10, written \(\log x\) (introduced by Henry Briggs).
Natural logarithmLogarithm to base \(e\approx2.71828\), written \(\ln x\).
CharacteristicThe integral part of a common logarithm (shows the size of the number).
MantissaThe positive decimal part of a common logarithm (found from the log table).
Bar numberA negative characteristic written with a bar, e.g. \(\bar2=-2\).
AntilogarithmThe number whose logarithm is given (inverse of a logarithm).
Mean differenceThe small number added for the 4th digit when reading a log or antilog table.
John NapierScottish mathematician who introduced the word logarithm.
Leonhard EulerSwiss mathematician who introduced the symbol \(e\).
