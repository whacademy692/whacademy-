The one idea behind this unit

1. What is the one idea that holds Unit 1 together?
• How should I study Complex Numbers?• What is this unit really testing?

A complex number is a point of the plane: the real part is how far you go along the x-axis and the imaginary part is how far you go up the y-axis. Once you see that, every operation has a picture:

  • adding is completing a parallelogram;
  • the conjugate is a mirror image in the real axis;
  • the modulus is a distance (Pythagoras);
  • multiplying by \(i\) is a quarter-turn;
  • multiplying in polar form is stretch and turn.

Section 1.0

Why Complex Numbers?

The need for a new kind of number, and what the book says about it.

Note

Complex numbers are used in many branches of science; quantum mechanics (a branch of Physics) depends heavily on them. Other uses named in the unit: cryptography, wave phenomena, voltage and current in circuits, velocity and pressure of a fluid.

Interactive diagram (needs JavaScript).

1. What is the imaginary unit \(i\)?
• Define \(i\) and state the value of \(i^{2}\).

\(i\) is the number introduced so that \(i^{2} = -1\), i.e. \(i = \sqrt{-1}\). The book calls \(i^{2} = -1\) Euler's notation for the imaginary unit.

2. Why do we need complex numbers?
• Why was the set of real numbers extended to the set of complex numbers?

Some polynomials have no solution in the real numbers. For example \(x^{2} + 1 = 0\) has none because no real number has square \(-1\). Introducing \(i\) with \(i^{2} = -1\) removes this difficulty.

3. Compare the solutions of \(x^{2} - 1 = 0\) and \(x^{2} + 1 = 0\).

\(x^{2} - 1 = 0\) has the real solutions \(x = \pm 1\). \(x^{2} + 1 = 0\) has no real solution (the book's point), but in complex numbers \(x = \pm i\).

4. Name a branch of Physics that depends heavily on complex numbers.

Quantum mechanics.

5. List four real-world uses of complex numbers mentioned in Unit 1.

Cryptography, wave phenomena, calculating voltage and current in circuits, and the velocity and pressure of a fluid. (Higher-level uses are left to higher classes; the unit works out simple harmonic motion, AC circuits and adding waves.)

Section 1.1

The Complex Number

Real part, imaginary part, the Argand plane and equality.

Definition. A complex number is an expression of the form \(x + iy\) where \(x, y \in \mathbb{R}\). It is denoted by \(z\), i.e. \(z = x + iy\), and the set of all complex numbers is denoted by \(C\). The number \(x + iy\) is also written as the ordered pair \((x, y)\); this is justified because there is a one-to-one correspondence between \(x + iy\) and \((x, y)\). Clearly \(i = 0 + i = (0, 1)\) and \(1 = 1 + 0i = (1, 0)\).

Interactive diagram (needs JavaScript).

11. Define a complex number.
• What is the standard form of a complex number? What is the set of complex numbers called?

An expression \(x + iy\) with \(x, y\) real is a complex number; we write \(z = x + iy\). The set of all complex numbers is \(C\).

12. Why can a complex number be written as an ordered pair?
• Write \(x + iy\) as an ordered pair.

\(x + iy = (x, y)\). The notation is justified because there is a one-to-one correspondence between \(x + iy\) and \((x, y)\).

13. Write \(i\) and \(1\) as ordered pairs.

\(i = 0 + i = (0, 1)\) and \(1 = 1 + 0i = (1, 0)\).

1.1.1 Real and Imaginary Parts

For \(z = x + iy\): \(x\) is the real part, \(\operatorname{Re}(z) = x\); \(y\) is the imaginary part, \(\operatorname{Im}(z) = y\). If the real part is zero, \(z\) is called pure imaginary; if the imaginary part is zero, \(z\) is called real. Every real number \(x = x + i0\) is complex, so \(\mathbb{R} \subset \mathbb{C}\), but not every complex number is real.

14. What are the real and imaginary parts of \(z = x + iy\)?
• For \(z = 2 + 3i\) write \(\operatorname{Re}(z)\) and \(\operatorname{Im}(z)\). (Example 1)

\(\operatorname{Re}(z) = x\), \(\operatorname{Im}(z) = y\). For \(z = 2 + 3i\): \(\operatorname{Re}(z) = 2\), \(\operatorname{Im}(z) = 3\).

15. Is \(\operatorname{Im}(z)\) the number \(iy\) or the number \(y\)?

\(\operatorname{Im}(z) = y\), a real number. The factor \(i\) is not part of it.

16. When is a complex number called pure imaginary? When is it real?

Pure imaginary when the real part is zero (e.g. \(3i\)). Real when the imaginary part is zero (e.g. \(5 = 5 + 0i\)).

17. Is every real number a complex number? Is every complex number real?
• How are \(R\) and \(C\) related?

Every real \(x\) is \(x + i0\), so every real number is complex and \(\mathbb{R} \subset \mathbb{C}\). Not every complex number is real; only those with imaginary part \(0\) are.

1.1.2 Condition for Equality of Two Complex Numbers

Two complex numbers are not comparable: we cannot say one is greater or less than another. They are equal if both have the same real part and the same imaginary part. If \(z_{1} = x_{1} + iy_{1}\) and \(z_{2} = x_{2} + iy_{2}\), then \(z_{1} = z_{2}\) iff \(x_{1} = x_{2}\) and \(y_{1} = y_{2}\). (Example 2)

18. State the condition for equality of two complex numbers.
• When is \(x_{1} + iy_{1} = x_{2} + iy_{2}\)?

Iff \(x_{1} = x_{2}\) and \(y_{1} = y_{2}\).

19. Can we say \(z_{1} > z_{2}\) for complex numbers?
• If \(z_{1} = 3 + 2i\) and \(z_{2} = 5 + 6i\), is \(z_{1} < z_{2}\)? (Review Ex Q1(vi) idea)

No. Complex numbers are not ordered, so \(<\) and \(>\) have no meaning between them. We can compare their moduli, which are real: \(|z_{1}| = \sqrt{13} < |z_{2}| = \sqrt{61}\).

20. If \(x + iy = 3 - 2i\) with \(x, y\) real, find \(x\) and \(y\).

Equal real parts and equal imaginary parts: \(x = 3\), \(y = -2\).

21. When is \(z = x + iy\) equal to zero?

Iff \(x = 0\) and \(y = 0\) (compare \(z = 0 + i0\)).

22. Classify \(5\), \(3i\), \(2 + 3i\), \(0\) as real, pure imaginary or neither.

\(5\) is real; \(3i\) is pure imaginary; \(2 + 3i\) has both parts non-zero so it is neither (a general complex number); \(0 = 0 + 0i\) is real.

Section 1.2

Basic Operations

Add, subtract, multiply and divide, with every book example.

Let \(z_{1} = x_{1} + iy_{1} = (x_{1}, y_{1})\) and \(z_{2} = x_{2} + iy_{2} = (x_{2}, y_{2})\).

OperationFormula
Sum (1.2.1)\(z_{1} + z_{2} = (x_{1} + x_{2}) + i(y_{1} + y_{2}) = (x_{1} + x_{2}, y_{1} + y_{2})\)
Difference (1.2.2)\(z_{1} - z_{2} = (x_{1} - x_{2}) + i(y_{1} - y_{2}) = (x_{1} - x_{2}, y_{1} - y_{2})\)
Product (1.2.3)\(z_{1} z_{2} = (x_{1}x_{2} - y_{1}y_{2}) + i(x_{1}y_{2} + x_{2}y_{1}) = (x_{1}x_{2} - y_{1}y_{2}, x_{1}y_{2} + x_{2}y_{1})\)
Quotient (1.2.4)\(\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}} = \frac{(x_{1}x_{2} + y_{1}y_{2}) + i(x_{2}y_{1} - x_{1}y_{2})}{x_{2}^{2} + y_{2}^{2}}\), \(z_{2} \ne 0\)

Why the product formula works: \((x_{1} + iy_{1})(x_{2} + iy_{2}) = x_{1}x_{2} + ix_{1}y_{2} + ix_{2}y_{1} + i^{2}y_{1}y_{2}\), and \(i^{2} = -1\).

Division: the denominator has two independent parts, so we rationalise: multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator. Then the denominator becomes the single real number \(x_{2}^{2} + y_{2}^{2}\).

Tip

To divide, never leave \(i\) in the denominator. \((a + ib)(a - ib) = a^{2} + b^{2}\) is real.

23. State the formula for the sum of two complex numbers.
• How do we add \(z_{1} = x_{1} + iy_{1}\) and \(z_{2} = x_{2} + iy_{2}\)?

Add real parts and imaginary parts separately: \(z_{1} + z_{2} = (x_{1} + x_{2}) + i(y_{1} + y_{2})\).

Example 3 — Find the sum of \(z_{1} = 2 + 3i\) and \(z_{2} = 6 + 8i\).Basic
Question:

Find the sum of \(z_{1} = 2 + 3i\) and \(z_{2} = 6 + 8i\).

\(z_{1} + z_{2} = (2 + 3i) + (6 + 8i)\)
\(= (2 + 6) + (3 + 8)i\)
\(= 8 + 11i = (8, 11)\)

Answer: \(8 + 11i\)

25. State the formula for the difference of two complex numbers.

\(z_{1} - z_{2} = (x_{1} - x_{2}) + i(y_{1} - y_{2})\).

Example 4 — If \(z_{1} = 4 - 3i\) and \(z_{2} = 7 + 6i\), find \(z_{1} - z_{2}\).Basic
Question:

If \(z_{1} = 4 - 3i\) and \(z_{2} = 7 + 6i\), find \(z_{1} - z_{2}\).

\(z_{1} - z_{2} = (4 - 3i) - (7 + 6i)\)
\(= (4 - 7) + (-3 - 6)i\)
\(= -3 - 9i\)

Answer: \(-3 - 9i\)

27. Derive the product of two complex numbers.
• State the formula for \(z_{1} z_{2}\).

\((x_{1} + iy_{1})(x_{2} + iy_{2}) = x_{1}x_{2} + ix_{1}y_{2} + ix_{2}y_{1} + i^{2}y_{1}y_{2} = (x_{1}x_{2} - y_{1}y_{2}) + i(x_{1}y_{2} + x_{2}y_{1})\), because \(i^{2} = -1\).

Example 5 — Find the product of the complex numbers \(z_{1} = (2, -6)\) and \(z_{2} = (4, 9)\).Basic
Question:

Find the product of the complex numbers \(z_{1} = (2, -6)\) and \(z_{2} = (4, 9)\).

\(z_{1} z_{2} = (2 - 6i)(4 + 9i)\)
\(= 8 + 18i - 24i - 54i^{2}\)
\(= 8 - 6i + 54\)
\(= 62 - 6i = (62, -6)\)

Answer: \((62, -6)\), i.e. \(62 - 6i\)

29. Why is division of complex numbers "not simple"? How is it done?
• What is rationalising the denominator for complex numbers?

The denominator \(x_{2} + iy_{2}\) has two independent parts. Multiply the numerator and denominator by the conjugate \(x_{2} - iy_{2}\); the denominator becomes \(x_{2}^{2} + y_{2}^{2}\) (real) and the division can be completed.

30. State the formula for \(\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}\).

\(\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}} = [\frac{x_{1}x_{2} + y_{1}y_{2}}{x_{2}^{2} + y_{2}^{2}}] + i[\frac{x_{2}y_{1} - x_{1}y_{2}}{x_{2}^{2} + y_{2}^{2}}]\), \(z_{2} \ne 0\).

Example 6 — If \(z_{1} = 3 + 7i\) and \(z_{2} = -4 + 6i\), find the sum, difference, product and quotient of the two complex numbers.Intermediate
Question:

If \(z_{1} = 3 + 7i\) and \(z_{2} = -4 + 6i\), find the sum, difference, product and quotient of the two complex numbers.

Sum: \(z_{1} + z_{2} = (3 - 4) + (7 + 6)i = -1 + 13i\)
Difference: \(z_{1} - z_{2} = (3 + 4) + (7 - 6)i = 7 + i\)
Product: \(z_{1} z_{2} = (3 + 7i)(-4 + 6i) = -12 + 18i - 28i + 42i^{2}\)
\(= -12 - 10i - 42 = -54 - 10i\)
Quotient: \(\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}} = \frac{3 + 7i}{-4 + 6i} \times \frac{-4 - 6i}{-4 - 6i}\)
Numerator: \(-12 - 18i - 28i - 42i^{2} = -12 - 46i + 42 = 30 - 46i\)
Denominator: \((-4)^{2} - (6i)^{2} = 16 + 36 = 52\)
\(= \frac{30 - 46i}{52} = \frac{15}{26} - \frac{23}{26}i\)

Answer: Sum \(-1 + 13i\); difference \(7 + i\); product \(-54 - 10i\); quotient \(\frac{15}{26} - \frac{23i}{26}\)

Example 7 — Write the complex number \(\frac{(2 + 3i)(2 + i)}{1 - i}\) in the form \(x + iy\).Intermediate
Question:

Write the complex number \(\frac{(2 + 3i)(2 + i)}{1 - i}\) in the form \(x + iy\).

Numerator: \((2 + 3i)(2 + i) = 4 + 2i + 6i + 3i^{2} = 1 + 8i\)
\(\frac{1 + 8i}{1 - i} \times \frac{1 + i}{1 + i} = \frac{1 + i + 8i + 8i^{2}}{1 - i^{2}}\)
\(= \frac{1 + 9i - 8}{1 + 1} = \frac{-7 + 9i}{2}\)

Answer: \(-\frac{7}{2} + \frac{9}{2}i\)

Example 8 — Find the values of \(x\) and \(y\) if \(\frac{x}{2 + 3i} - y(1 + 2i) = 1 + i\).Advance
Question:

Find the values of \(x\) and \(y\) if \(\frac{x}{2 + 3i} - y(1 + 2i) = 1 + i\).

\(\frac{x}{2 + 3i} = \frac{x(2 - 3i)}{(2 + 3i)(2 - 3i)} = \frac{2x - 3xi}{13}\)
So \(\frac{2x - 3xi}{13} - y - 2iy = 1 + i\)
Group: \(\left(\frac{2x}{13} - y\right) - \left(\frac{3x}{13} + 2y\right)i = 1 + i\)
Compare real parts: \(\frac{2x}{13} - y = 1\) … (1)
Compare imaginary parts: \(-\frac{3x}{13} - 2y = 1\) … (2)
(1) × 2: \(\frac{4x}{13} - 2y = 2\). Subtract (2) from this: \(\frac{7x}{13} = 1\), so \(x = \frac{13}{7}\).
Put in (1): \(\frac{2}{7} - y = 1\), so \(y = -\frac{5}{7}\).

Answer: \(x = \frac{13}{7}\), \(y = -\frac{5}{7}\)

34. How do you find real \(x\) and \(y\) from one equation containing complex numbers?

Simplify both sides to the form \(A + iB\), then equate the real parts and the imaginary parts. This gives two real equations in \(x\) and \(y\).

35. Find \((a + ib)(a - ib)\).

\(a^{2} - i^{2}b^{2} = a^{2} + b^{2}\), a real number.

36. Name the algebraic properties of complex numbers used in Exercise 1.2 Q2.W.H. extra
• State the commutative, associative and distributive laws for \(C\).

For all \(z_{1}, z_{2}, z_{3}\) in \(C\): \(z_{1} + z_{2} = z_{2} + z_{1}\), \(z_{1} z_{2} = z_{2} z_{1}\) (commutative); \((z_{1} + z_{2}) + z_{3} = z_{1} + (z_{2} + z_{3})\), \((z_{1} z_{2}) z_{3} = z_{1} (z_{2} z_{3})\) (associative); \(z_{1}(z_{2} + z_{3}) = z_{1} z_{2} + z_{1} z_{3}\) (distributive). Identities: \(0\) for addition, \(1\) for multiplication.

37. What are the additive inverse and the multiplicative inverse of \(z = x + iy\)?W.H. extra

Additive inverse \(-z = -x - iy\). Multiplicative inverse (\(z \ne 0\)): \(z^{-1} = \frac{1}{z} = \frac{x}{x^{2} + y^{2}} - i \frac{y}{x^{2} + y^{2}}\) (derived in Section 1.5 Type I).

Extra Example E — Find real \(x\) and \(y\) if \((x + iy)(2 - 3i) = 4 + 7i\).Advance
Question:

Find real \(x\) and \(y\) if \((x + iy)(2 - 3i) = 4 + 7i\).

\((x + iy)(2 - 3i) = 2x - 3xi + 2iy - 3i^{2}y = (2x + 3y) + i(2y - 3x)\)

\(x = \frac{4 - 6}{2} = -1\)

Section 1.3

The Conjugate

The mirror image of z, and why it makes division possible.

The conjugate of \(z = x + iy\) is \(\bar{z} = x - iy\). Geometrically \(\bar{z}\) is the mirror image of \(z\) about the x-axis; e.g. \(z = 3 + 4i\) is the point \(P(3, 4)\) and \(\bar{z} = 3 - 4i\) is \(P'(3, -4)\) (book figure: two points above and below the x-axis).

Key facts (from the book)

\(\operatorname{conj}(z_{1} z_{2}) = \bar{z}_{1} \bar{z}_{2}\) and \(\operatorname{conj}\left(\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}\right) = \frac{\bar{z}_{1}}{\bar{z}_{2}}\). Key Fact (book, p.10): To find the conjugate of a complex number we may simplify first and then take the conjugate, or take the conjugate first and then simplify.

43. Define the conjugate of a complex number.
• Write the conjugate of \(z = x + iy\).

\(\bar{z} = x - iy\). (Change the sign of the imaginary part.)

44. What is the geometrical meaning of the conjugate?
• The conjugate of \(3 + 4i\) is the mirror image in which axis?

Modulus (Magnitude)

The distance of z from the origin, and the modulus theorem.

For \(z = x + iy = (x, y)\) the modulus \(|z|\) is the distance of the point \(P(x, y)\) from the origin. Draw the perpendicular \(PA\) from \(P\) to the real axis; triangle \(PAO\) is right-angled, so by Pythagoras \(|OP|^{2} = |OA|^{2} + |AP|^{2}\), i.e. \(|z|^{2} = x^{2} + y^{2}\).

\(|z| = \sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}} = \sqrt{({\operatorname{Re} z})^{2} + ({\operatorname{Im} z})^{2}}\)

Key facts (from the book)

\(|z_{1} z_{2}| = |z_{1}||z_{2}|\) and \(|\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}| = \frac{|z_{1}|}{|z_{2}|}\).

Theorem. If \(z\) is a complex number then \(|z| \ge 0\), and \(|z| = 0\) iff \(z = 0\). Proof. Let \(z = x + iy\). Then \(|z| = \sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}\). The square of a real number is non-negative, so \(x^{2} + y^{2}\) is non-negative, and the square root of a non-negative number is non-negative; hence \(|z| \ge 0\). Now suppose \(|z| = 0\). Then \(\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}} = 0\), so \(x^{2} + y^{2} = 0\), which is possible only if \(x = 0\) and \(y = 0\); thus \(z = 0 + i0 = 0\). Conversely, if \(z = 0 + i0\) then \(|z| = \sqrt{0^{2} + 0^{2}} = 0\). ∎

Section 1.6

Solving Equations

Simultaneous linear equations with complex coefficients.

When more than one equation is considered together it is a system of equations; values of the variables that satisfy all the equations at the same time are the simultaneous solutions. If \(z\) and \(w\) are complex variables, an equation \(az + bw = p\) is a linear equation in two variables with complex coefficients (here \(a\) and \(b\) are not both zero, and they may themselves be complex numbers). The unit solves systems of two such equations.

Note (book)

A system of equations is consistent if it has at least one solution. A system which has no solution is called inconsistent.

1.6.1 Working Rule — Elimination Method (\(a_{1}z + b_{1}w = p_{1}\), \(a_{2}z + b_{2}w = p_{2}\))

  1. Multiply one or both equations by suitable numbers so that the coefficients of one variable become the same.
  2. Add or subtract the equations to eliminate that variable.
  3. The equation from Step 2 has one variable; find its value.
  4. Substitute it in either given equation to get the other variable.
  5. Write the solution as the ordered pair \((z, w)\).

1.6.2 Working Rule — Substitution Method

  1. Find one variable in terms of the other from either equation.
  2. Substitute it in the equation not yet used.
  3. This equation has one variable; find its value.
  4. Substitute that value in either given equation to find the other variable.
  5. Write the solution as the ordered pair \((z, w)\).
138. What is a linear equation in two variables with complex coefficients?
• Write the general form of such an equation and state the condition on \(a\) and \(b\).

\(az + bw = p\), where \(z, w\) are complex variables and \(a, b, p\) are complex numbers; \(a\) and \(b\) cannot both be zero.

139. What are simultaneous solutions of a system of equations?

Values of the variables that satisfy all the equations of the system at the same time.

140. Define a consistent and an inconsistent system.

Consistent: at least one solution. Inconsistent: no solution.

141. State the five steps of the elimination method.

(1) Make the coefficients of one variable equal by multiplying. (2) Add/subtract to eliminate it. (3) Solve the one-variable equation. (4) Substitute back to get the other variable. (5) Write the solution as \((z, w)\).

142. State the five steps of the substitution method.

(1) Express one variable in terms of the other from one equation. (2) Substitute in the other equation. (3) Solve the one-variable equation. (4) Substitute back for the other variable. (5) Write the solution as \((z, w)\).

143. In what form is the solution of a system in \(z\) and \(w\) written?

As the ordered pair \((z, w)\).

Section 1.7

Complex Polynomials

Linear factors and quadratics with real coefficients by completing the square.

If \(z\) is a complex variable, \(a_{0} + a_{1}z + a_{2}z^{2} + \ldots + a_{n}z^{n}\) is a complex polynomial of degree \(n\) if \(a_{n} \ne 0\) and \(n\) is a non-negative integer; \(a_{0}, \ldots , a_{n}\) are constants (real or complex). It is written \(P(z) = a_{0} + a_{1}z + a_{2}z^{2} + \ldots + a_{n}z^{n}\). When \(n = 1\) it is \(a_{0} + a_{1}z\), a linear polynomial. The unit factorises (i) \(P(z) = z^{2} + a^{2}\) (\(a\) real) and (ii) \(P(z) = az^{3} + bz^{2} + cz + d\) (\(a, b, c, d\) real, \(a \ne 0\)) into linear factors.

1.7.1 Factorisation of \(z^{2} + a^{2}\)

\(z^{2} + a^{2} = z^{2} - i^{2}a^{2} = z^{2} - (ia)^{2} = (z + ia)(z - ia)\). So \((z + ia)\) and \((z - ia)\) are the linear factors.

1.7.2 Factorisation of \(az^{3} + bz^{2} + cz + d\) (\(a \ne 0\))

Find one factor with the factor theorem, then do synthetic division to get the depressed (quadratic) equation. Recall: \(z - a\) is a factor of \(P(z)\) iff \(P(a) = 0\); then \(a\) is a root (zero) of \(P\). e.g. \(z - 2\) is a factor of \(P(z) = 2z^{3} + 3z^{2} + 6z - 40\) since \(P(2) = 16 + 12 + 12 - 40 = 0\). To choose trial roots: take the product of the coefficient of \(z^{3}\) and the last term, and try its factors (book's method for Example 16).

1.7.3 Solution by Completing the Square

To solve \(pz^{2} + qz + r = 0\) (\(p, q, r\) real): divide by \(p\), move the constant to the right, add the square of half the coefficient of \(z\) to both sides, write the left side as a perfect square, and take square roots on both sides.

Section 1.8

The Polar Coordinate System

Describing a point by distance and angle.

Another way to locate a point in the plane is the polar coordinate system. It has a fixed point \(O\) called the pole, and the horizontal line emerging from the pole is the initial line (polar axis). For a point \(P\), if \(r\) is its distance from the pole and \(\theta\) is the angle measured anticlockwise from the initial line to the line \(OP\), then the ordered pair \((r, \theta )\) gives the polar coordinates of \(P\). The angle \(\theta\) is also called \(\arg(z)\).

  • For \(z = 0\), \(\arg(z)\) is undefined, so \(z \ne 0\) whenever the polar system is used.
  • If \(z = x + iy\) has polar coordinates \((r, \theta )\), its conjugate \(\bar{z} = x - iy\) has polar coordinates \((r, -\theta )\).

Section 1.9

Complex Numbers in Polar Form

Argument, principal argument, products, quotients and simple loci.

1.9.1 Polar Representation of a Complex Number

Let \(z = x + iy\) be the point \(P(x, y)\); let \(r = |z|\) and let \(\theta\) be the positive angle that \(OP\) makes with the initial line (x-axis). Drop a perpendicular \(PL\) to the x-axis. By Pythagoras \(|OL|^{2} + |LP|^{2} = |OP|^{2}\), so \(x^{2} + y^{2} = r^{2}\), i.e. \(r = \sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}\). Also \(\frac{x}{r} = \cos \theta\), so \(x = r \cos \theta\); and \(\frac{y}{r} = \sin \theta\), so \(y = r \sin \theta\). Therefore

\(z = r \cos \theta + i r \sin \theta = r(\cos \theta + i \sin \theta )\), the polar form of \(z\).

Key facts (from the book)

\(\theta\) is the argument of \(z\), \(\theta = \arg(z)\). \(\cos \theta + i \sin \theta\) can also be written \(\operatorname{cis} \theta\), and \(\cos \theta + i \sin \theta = e^{i\theta }\) is Euler's formula.

1.9.2 Principal Argument

\(\arg(z)\) actually represents a set of values. The value \(\theta\) that lies in \(-\pi < \theta \le \pi\) is the principal value (principal argument) \(\operatorname{Arg}(z)\); it is unique. If \(\alpha\) is the reference (acute) angle with \(\alpha = \tan^{-1}|\frac{y}{x}|\):

Quadrant of \(z\)Principal argument \(\theta\)
I (\(x > 0, y > 0\))\(\alpha\)
II (\(x < 0, y > 0\))\(\pi - \alpha\)
III (\(x < 0, y < 0\))\(\alpha - \pi\), i.e. \(-(\pi - \alpha )\)
IV (\(x > 0, y < 0\))\(-\alpha\)
Key facts (from the book)

\(\sin(\theta + 2k\pi ) = \sin \theta\) and \(\cos(\theta + 2k\pi ) = \cos \theta\); so the general polar form is \(r[\cos(\theta + 2k\pi ) + i \sin(\theta + 2k\pi )]\). Key Facts (book): \(\operatorname{Arg}(z_{1} z_{2}) = \operatorname{Arg}(z_{1}) + \operatorname{Arg}(z_{2})\) and \(\operatorname{Arg}\left(\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}\right) = \operatorname{Arg}(z_{1}) - \operatorname{Arg}(z_{2})\) (as printed; true up to a multiple of \(2\pi\),).

\(z_{1} - z_{2} = (r_{1} \cos \theta _{1} - r_{2} \cos \theta _{2}) + i(r_{1} \sin \theta _{1} - r_{2} \sin \theta _{2})\).

204. State and prove the product rule in polar form.
• What happens to the modulus and the argument when two complex numbers are multiplied?

\(z_{1} z_{2} = r_{1} r_{2}[\cos(\theta _{1} + \theta _{2}) + i \sin(\theta _{1} + \theta _{2})]\). Proof: \(r_{1} r_{2}[(\cos \theta _{1} \cos \theta _{2} - \sin \theta _{1} \sin \theta _{2}) + i(\sin \theta _{1} \cos \theta _{2} + \cos \theta _{1} \sin \theta _{2})]\) (using \(i^{2} = -1\)) \(= r_{1} r_{2}[\cos(\theta _{1} + \theta _{2}) + i \sin(\theta _{1} + \theta _{2})]\). Moduli multiply, arguments add.

205. State and prove the quotient rule in polar form.

\(\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}} = \left(\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\right)[\cos(\theta _{1} - \theta _{2}) + i \sin(\theta _{1} - \theta _{2})]\). Proof: multiply numerator and denominator by \(\cos \theta _{2} - i \sin \theta _{2}\); the denominator becomes \(\cos^{2}\theta _{2} + \sin^{2}\theta _{2} = 1\) and the numerator becomes \((\cos \theta _{1} \cos \theta _{2} + \sin \theta _{1} \sin \theta _{2}) + i(\sin \theta _{1} \cos \theta _{2} - \cos \theta _{1} \sin \theta _{2})\). Moduli divide, arguments subtract.

206. Write the product and quotient rules using Euler's formula.

\(z_{1} z_{2} = r_{1} r_{2} e^{i(\theta _{1} + \theta _{2})}\) and \(\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}} = \left(\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\right) e^{i(\theta _{1} - \theta _{2})}\).

207. State the key facts about \(\arg(z_{1} z_{2})\) and \(\arg\left(\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}\right)\).

\(\arg(z_{1} z_{2}) = \arg(z_{1}) + \arg(z_{2})\) and \(\arg\left(\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}\right) = \arg(z_{1}) - \arg(z_{2})\) (up to a multiple of \(2\pi\) when principal values are used).

214. What does \(\arg\left(\frac{z - 1}{z + 1}\right) = \frac{\pi}{2}\) tell you about the real and imaginary parts of the quotient?

The quotient is purely imaginary with positive imaginary part (an angle of \(\frac{\pi}{2}\) lies on the positive imaginary axis).

215. What curve does \(\arg(z - a) = \theta _{0}\) represent?

A ray (half-line) starting at the point \(a\), making the angle \(\theta _{0}\) with the positive x-direction (the point \(a\) itself is excluded). Example: \(\arg(z - 1) = -\frac{\pi}{4}\) is \(x + y = 1\) with \(x > 1\) (Ex 1.4 Q5(i)).

Section 1.10

Applications in the Real World

Simple harmonic motion, AC impedance and adding waves.

Complex numbers are used in cryptography, wave phenomena, fluid velocity and pressure, and the voltage and current of circuits; most uses are of higher level and are left to higher classes. This section uses them for simple harmonic motion, impedance, and adding AC waves.

Simple harmonic motion. The position of a microscopic particle from its mean position is \(x = x_{\mathrm{max}} e^{i\theta }\) … (1), where \(x\) is the displacement from the mean position, \(x_{\mathrm{max}}\) is the amplitude and \(e^{i\theta } = \cos \theta + i \sin \theta\) is a complex number (Euler's equation). It can also be written \(x = x_{\mathrm{max}} e^{i\omega t}\), where \(\omega\) is the angular velocity, \(t\) the time and \(\omega = 2\pi f\) with \(f\) the frequency.

Electrical engineering. For a circuit, the flow of electricity \(I\) (current), the resistance to flow \(Z\) (called impedance) and the electromotive force \(E\) (called voltage) are related by \(E = I\cdot Z\). Electrical engineers use \(j\) for the imaginary unit; the book keeps \(i\).

Adding AC voltages (waves). Write each voltage as the real part of a complex number, add the complex numbers, then take the modulus and argument of the sum.

Resistor in an AC circuit. Let \(v(t) = V_{0} \cos(\omega t) = \operatorname{Re}(V_{0} e^{i\omega t})\) (\(V_{0}\), \(\omega\) real). The current \(i\) through a resistor \(R\) satisfies \(v(t)_{R} = R\cdot i\), so \(R\cdot i = \operatorname{Re}(V_{R})\) with \(V_{R} = V_{0} e^{i\omega t}\), i.e. \(i = \operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{V_{R}}{R}\right)\). Define the impedance of a resistor \(Z_{R} = R\) (real). Then \(i = \operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{V_{R}}{Z_{R}}\right)\); with \(I = \frac{V_{R}}{Z_{R}}\) this resembles Ohm's law for DC circuits. Calculations are done with complex impedance, voltage and current, and the real part is taken at the end.

Example 25 — A micro particle is performing to and fro motion. Find its position at an angle of \(\frac{\pi}{2}\), when its amplitude is 0.05 mm.Intermediate
Question:

A micro particle is performing to and fro motion. Find its position at an angle of \(\frac{\pi}{2}\), when its amplitude is 0.05 mm.

\(x_{\mathrm{max}} = 0.05\), \(\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}\)
\(x = x_{\mathrm{max}} e^{i\theta } = 0.05 e^{\frac{i\pi}{2}} = 0.05\left(\cos \frac{\pi}{2} + i \sin \frac{\pi}{2}\right)\)
\(= 0.05(0 + i)\)

219. What are impedance, current and voltage related by in an electric circuit?
• Why do electrical engineers use \(j\) instead of \(i\)?

\(E = I\cdot Z\) (voltage = current × impedance). Engineers use \(j\) for the imaginary unit (the letter \(i\) is already used for current); the book keeps \(i\) for understanding.

\(E = I \times Z = (6 - 8i)(14 + 8i)\)
\(= 84 + 48i - 112i - 64i^{2}\)
\(= 84 - 64i + 64\)

Answer: \(E = 148 - 64i\) volts

\(= 6 + 5i + 4\)

223. Why do we use complex numbers to add two AC voltages such as \(3\cos(t + 30^{\circ})\) and \(4 \sin(t + 120^{\circ})\)?

Two sinusoids of the same frequency add to one sinusoid, but with a new amplitude and phase. Writing each as the real part of \(A e^{i(t + \varphi )}\) turns the sum into an ordinary complex addition; then the modulus gives the new amplitude and the argument gives the new phase.

225. Write the voltage of an AC source in complex form.

\(v(t) = V_{0} \cos(\omega t) = \operatorname{Re}(V_{0} e^{i\omega t})\), with \(V_{0}\) and \(\omega\) real.

226. What is the impedance of a resistor in an AC circuit?
• How is the current through a resistor found with complex numbers?

\(Z_{R} = R\), a real number. With \(V_{R} = V_{0} e^{i\omega t}\): \(I = \frac{V_{R}}{Z_{R}}\) and the actual current is \(i = \operatorname{Re}(I) = \operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{V_{R}}{Z_{R}}\right)\).

227. Which relation for AC circuits is similar to Ohm's law for DC?

\(I = \frac{V_{R}}{Z_{R}}\) (current = voltage / impedance); it makes calculations simpler because we work with complex numbers and take the real part at the end.

Connecting the Concepts

How the ideas of this unit lean on one another.

281. How is the conjugate used in division of complex numbers?

To divide, multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator. Since \((x_{2} + iy_{2})(x_{2} - iy_{2}) = x_{2}^{2} + y_{2}^{2}\) is real, the quotient reaches the form \(x + iy\).

282. How are \(z\), \(\bar{z}\) and \(|z|\) connected? Use this to write \(z^{-1}\).

\(z \bar{z} = x^{2} + y^{2} = |z|^{2}\), so \(z^{-1} = \frac{\bar{z}}{|z|}^{2} = \frac{x}{x^{2} + y^{2}} - i \frac{y}{x^{2} + y^{2}}\). This is exactly the result of Section 1.5, Type I.

283. Which part of the polar product rule is the key fact \(|z_{1} z_{2}| = |z_{1}||z_{2}|\)?

The modulus part. In polar form \(z_{1} z_{2} = r_{1} r_{2}[\cos(\theta _{1} + \theta _{2}) + i \sin(\theta _{1} + \theta _{2})]\), so the moduli multiply (\(|z_{1} z_{2}| = r_{1} r_{2}\)) and the arguments add.

284. How does the conjugate look in polar form, and what does it mean geometrically?

If \(z\) is \((r, \theta )\) then \(\bar{z}\) is \((r, -\theta )\): same distance from the origin, opposite angle, i.e. the mirror image in the x-axis.

285. How are \(x + iy\) and \(r(\cos \theta + i \sin \theta )\) related?

\(x = r \cos \theta\), \(y = r \sin \theta\), \(r = \sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}} = |z|\), and \(\theta = \arg z\) found from \(\tan \theta = \frac{y}{x}\) with the quadrant of \((x, y)\) deciding the principal value.

286. What happens to a complex number when it is multiplied by \(i\)? Check with Exercise 1.2 Q1.

\(i = \cos \frac{\pi}{2} + i \sin \frac{\pi}{2}\), so multiplying by \(i\) keeps the modulus and adds \(\frac{\pi}{2}\) to the argument (a quarter-turn). In coordinates \(i(x + iy) = -y + ix\), so \(\operatorname{Re}(iz) = -\operatorname{Im}(z)\) and \(\operatorname{Im}(iz) = \operatorname{Re}(z)\) (Exercise 1.2 Q1).

287. What do you notice about the non-real roots of quadratics with real coefficients in this unit?

They come as conjugate pairs: \(z^{2} - 2z + 5 = 0\) gives \(1 \pm 2i\); \(z^{2} - 6z + 25 = 0\) gives \(3 \pm 4i\); \(z^{2} - \frac{1}{2}z + 17 = 0\) gives \(\frac{1 \pm i\sqrt{271}}{4}\).

288. How is \(z^{2} + a^{2} = (z + ia)(z - ia)\) connected to solving \(z^{2} + a^{2} = 0\)?

The linear factors give the roots \(z = \pm ia\), a conjugate pair; completing the square gives the same: \(z^{2} = -a^{2}\), \(z = \pm ia\).

289. How is solving a system with complex coefficients like solving one with real coefficients?

The same elimination, substitution or Cramer's rule works; only the arithmetic uses complex numbers (conjugate for division). The answer is a pair \((z, w)\) of complex numbers.

290. Summarise the curves in the \(x-y\) plane given by the conditions met in Ex 1.2 Q8 and Ex 1.4 Q5.

\(|z - a| = r\): circle (Ex 1.2 Q8(i)). \(|z - a| = |z - b|\): perpendicular bisector (Ex 1.2 Q8(ii): \(|z - 1| = |\bar{z} + i| = |z - i|\) gives \(x = y\); Review Q4 gives \(y = 0\)). \(|z - a| + |z - b| = \text{const}\): ellipse (Ex 1.2 Q8(iii)). \({\operatorname{Re} z} = c\) or \({\operatorname{Im} z} = c\): vertical/horizontal line (Ex 1.2 Q8(iv), (v), (vi)). \(\arg(z - a) = \theta _{0}\): a ray from \(a\) (Ex 1.4 Q5(i)); \(\theta _{1} \le \arg(z - a) \le \theta _{2}\): an angular region (Ex 1.4 Q5(iii), (iv)).

291. How does Euler's formula connect polar form, SHM and AC circuits?

\(e^{i\theta } = \cos \theta + i \sin \theta\) gives \(z = r e^{i\theta }\), the product rule \(e^{i\theta _{1}} e^{i\theta _{2}} = e^{i(\theta _{1} + \theta _{2})}\), the SHM position \(x = x_{\mathrm{max}} e^{i\omega t}\), and the AC voltage \(v = \operatorname{Re}(V_{0} e^{i\omega t})\).

292. Complex numbers cannot be ordered, yet a unit examines \(|z_{1}| < |z_{2}|\). Why is this allowed?

\(|z|\) is a non-negative real number (Theorem 1.4), and real numbers can be ordered. So we may compare moduli even though \(z_{1} < z_{2}\) is meaningless.

293. Give the modulus and principal argument of a positive real, a negative real and a pure imaginary number.

Positive real \(a\): \(|a|\), \(\operatorname{Arg} = 0\). Negative real \(-a\): \(a\), \(\operatorname{Arg} = \pi\) (Example 20 uses \(\arg(-2) = \pi\)). \(bi\) with \(b > 0\): \(b\), \(\frac{\pi}{2}\); with \(b < 0\): \(|b|\), \(-\frac{\pi}{2}\).

294. How does impedance generalise resistance?

For a resistor, \(Z_{R} = R\) is real and \(I = \frac{V_{R}}{Z_{R}}\) gives a current in phase with the voltage. For other circuit parts the impedance can be a complex number such as \(14 + 8i\) (Example 26), and \(E = IZ\) is a complex multiplication.
