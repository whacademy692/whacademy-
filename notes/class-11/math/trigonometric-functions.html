The one idea behind this unit

1. What is the one idea that holds Unit 9 together?
• How should I study Trigonometric Functions?• What is this unit really testing?

Every trigonometric function is a wave (or a pair of waves with gaps) that you read off the unit circle. Once you can see the wave, every question in the unit becomes a question about its four controls:

  • Range and maximum/minimum come from the height: midline \(d\) and amplitude \(|a|\), so \(\text{max}=d+|a|\) and \(\text{min}=d-|a|\);
  • Period comes from the squeeze \(b\): \(2\pi/b\) for sin and cos, \(\pi/b\) for tan and cot;
  • The graph is five key points per period joined by a smooth wave;
  • Even or odd is just the mirror (cos, sec) or the half-turn (the other four);
  • A model of a Ferris wheel or the mains supply is the same formula with numbers filled in.

3. Which short tricks save the most time in the exam?
  • Maximum and minimum: ignore everything inside the bracket. \(M=a+|b|\) and \(m=a-|b|\).
  • Period: sin, cos, sec, cosec \(\to 2\pi/|b|\). tan, cot \(\to \pi/|b|\). Add, then LCM of numerators over HCF of denominators.
  • Even or odd: count the odd factors. An even count means even, an odd count means odd. A sum of an even and an odd term is neither.
  • Graphs: five points per period at \(0,\ T/4,\ T/2,\ 3T/4,\ T\).
  • Word problems: midline \(=\tfrac{\max+\min}{2}\), amplitude \(=\tfrac{\max-\min}{2}\), \(b=2\pi/T\). Start at the bottom means minus cos.

4. How are degrees and radians connected?
• Convert \(150^\circ\) to radians.

\(\pi\) radians \(=180^\circ\). To change radians to degrees multiply by \(\frac{180}{\pi}\); to change degrees to radians multiply by \(\frac{\pi}{180}\). For example \(\frac{2\pi}{3}=120^\circ\) and \(225^\circ=\frac{5\pi}{4}\). The graphs of Unit 9 use radians on the x-axis.

5. How do the six trigonometric functions come from the unit circle?
• Define sin, cos and tan using a point on the unit circle.

Take a point \(P(x,y)\) on the circle \(x^2+y^2=1\) at angle \(\theta\). Then \(\cos\theta=x\) and \(\sin\theta=y\). The other four are ratios: \(\tan\theta=\frac yx\), \(\cot\theta=\frac xy\), \(\sec\theta=\frac1x\), \(\csc\theta=\frac1y\). Drag the point in the diagram below and watch the values change.

6. What does A S T C tell us?
• In which quadrants is \(\tan\theta\) negative?

It lists which ratios are positive in each quadrant, starting from the first and going anticlockwise: All in I, Sin (and cosec) in II, Tan (and cot) in III, Cos (and sec) in IV. Everything not listed is negative in that quadrant. Memory line: All Students Take Coffee.

7. How do I find the sign and value for an angle bigger than \(360^\circ\) or negative?

Remove whole turns of \(360^\circ\) (or \(2\pi\)) to bring the angle into \([0^\circ, 360^\circ)\). For a negative angle use \(\sin(-\theta)=-\sin\theta\) and \(\cos(-\theta)=\cos\theta\). Example: \(\sin 405^\circ=\sin 45^\circ\); \(\cos(-60^\circ)=\cos 60^\circ=\frac12\).

Section 9.1

Domain and Range

What can go in, what can come out, for each of the six functions.

8. Define the domain and the range of a function.

Domain = all inputs \(x\) for which \(f(x)\) is defined. Range = all outputs \(f(x)\) can actually take. Remember: domain is “what can I plug in?”, range is “what can come out?”.

9. Why do sin and cos always lie between \(-1\) and \(1\)?
• State the domain and range of \(\sin\theta\) and \(\cos\theta\).

For a point \(P(x,y)\) on a circle of radius \(r\), neither coordinate can be bigger than \(r\) in size: \(-r\le x\le r\) and \(-r\le y\le r\). Dividing by \(r\) gives \(-1\le \frac xr\le1\) and \(-1\le\frac yr\le1\), i.e. \(-1\le\cos\theta\le1\) and \(-1\le\sin\theta\le1\). Equivalent forms: \(|\sin\theta|\le1\) and \(\sin^2\theta\le1\).

10. Why is \(\tan x\) undefined at \(\frac{\pi}{2}\)? Write its domain and range.

\(\tan\theta=\frac yx\) and at \(\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}\) the point is \((0,1)\), so \(x=0\) and we would be dividing by zero. The same happens at every odd multiple of \(\frac{\pi}{2}\). Domain \(=\mathbb{R}-\{(2n+1)\frac{\pi}{2}\}\). The fraction \(\frac yx\) can be any real number, so the range is \(\mathbb{R}\).

11. Write the domain and range of \(\cot x\).

\(\cot\theta=\frac xy\) is undefined when \(y=0\), i.e. at \(\theta=n\pi\). Domain \(=\mathbb{R}-\{n\pi\}\); range \(=\mathbb{R}\).

12. Why do sec and cosec never take values between \(-1\) and \(1\)?
• What is the range of \(\sec\theta\) and \(\csc\theta\)?

Since \(\left|\frac xr\right|\le1\), taking reciprocals gives \(\left|\frac rx\right|\ge1\), so \(|\sec\theta|\ge1\); the same argument gives \(|\csc\theta|\ge1\). The range is the union \((-\infty,-1]\cup[1,\infty)\). Use the word “or”: it is not possible for \(y\) to be both below \(-1\) and above \(1\).

13. Is there an easy way to remember where each function is undefined?

Look at the denominator of the function in terms of \(\sin\) and \(\cos\). tan and sec have \(\cos\) underneath, so they break where \(\cos=0\): odd multiples of \(\frac{\pi}{2}\). cot and cosec have \(\sin\) underneath, so they break where \(\sin=0\): multiples of \(\pi\). Pairs: sin & cosec share \(n\pi\), cos & sec share \(\frac{\pi}{2}\)-points.

14. How do I find the domain and range of \(y=\dfrac{1}{a+b\sin(kx)}\)?

Step 1: domain. Solve denominator \(=0\). Those \(x\) are excluded. If the equation has no solution (because \(|\sin|\le1\)), nothing is excluded and the domain is \(\mathbb{R}\).

Step 2: range. Start from \(-1\le\sin(kx)\le1\). Multiply by \(b\), add \(a\): this gives the interval \([m,M]\) of the denominator. Then take reciprocals:

  • denominator interval all positive or all negative: the reciprocal is \(\left[\frac1M,\frac1m\right]\) (the ends swap);
  • denominator interval contains \(0\): the range is \(\left(-\infty,\frac1m\right]\cup\left[\frac1M,\infty\right)\), two pieces with a gap in the middle.
Tip

One line to remember: a reciprocal swaps the ends, and if the interval straddles 0 there is a hole in the middle.

Example 1 (i) — Find the domain and range of \(y=4\sin 3x\).Basic
Question:

Find the domain and range of \(y=4\sin 3x\).

Domain: \(\sin3x\) exists for every real \(x\), so the domain is \(\mathbb{R}\).
Range: \(-1\le\sin3x\le1\), multiply by 4: \(-4\le4\sin3x\le4\).

Answer: Domain \(\mathbb{R}\); range \([-4,4]\). (For \(A\sin(\cdot)\) the range is always \([-|A|,|A|]\); the inside only changes the period.)

Example 1 (ii) — Find the domain and range of \(y=\dfrac{1}{2\cos x-1}\).Intermediate
Question:

Find the domain and range of \(y=\dfrac{1}{2\cos x-1}\).

Domain: \(2\cos x-1=0\iff\cos x=\frac12\iff x=2n\pi\pm\frac{\pi}{3}\). These are excluded.
Range: \(-1\le\cos x\le1\Rightarrow-2\le2\cos x\le2\Rightarrow-3\le2\cos x-1\le1\).
The denominator runs over \([-3,1]\), which contains \(0\), so the reciprocal has two pieces: \(y\le\frac{1}{-3}\) or \(y\ge\frac11\).
Check: \(x=0\) gives \(\frac{1}{1}=1\); \(x=\pi\) gives \(\frac{1}{-3}\).

Answer: Domain \(\mathbb{R}-\{2n\pi\pm\frac{\pi}{3}\}\); range \(\left(-\infty,-\frac13\right]\cup[1,\infty)\)

Example 1 (iii) — Find the domain and range of \(y=\dfrac{1}{1+2\sin\theta}\).Intermediate
Question:

Find the domain and range of \(y=\dfrac{1}{1+2\sin\theta}\).

Domain: \(1+2\sin\theta=0\iff\sin\theta=-\frac12\). Sine is negative in quadrants III and IV with reference angle \(\frac{\pi}{6}\): \(\theta=2n\pi+\frac{7\pi}{6}\) and \(2n\pi+\frac{11\pi}{6}\).
Range: \(-1\le\sin\theta\le1\Rightarrow-2\le2\sin\theta\le2\Rightarrow-1\le1+2\sin\theta\le3\). The interval contains 0, so we get two pieces.

Answer: Domain \(\mathbb{R}-\{2n\pi+\frac{7\pi}{6},\,2n\pi+\frac{11\pi}{6}\}\); range \((-\infty,-1]\cup\left[\frac13,\infty\right)\)

Example 1 (iv) — Find the domain and range of \(y=\dfrac{1}{2-\sin 3x}\).Intermediate
Question:

Find the domain and range of \(y=\dfrac{1}{2-\sin 3x}\).

Domain: \(2-\sin3x=0\) needs \(\sin3x=2\), which is impossible. So nothing is excluded.
Range: \(-1\le\sin3x\le1\Rightarrow-1\le-\sin3x\le1\Rightarrow1\le2-\sin3x\le3\). The denominator is always positive, so the reciprocal swaps the ends: \(\frac13\le y\le1\).

Answer: Domain \(\mathbb{R}\); range \(\left[\frac13,1\right]\)

19. What are the quick rules for domain and range in Section 9.1?
• Give a shortcut for domain and range questions.
  • \(A\sin\) or \(A\cos\): domain \(\mathbb{R}\), range \([-|A|,|A|]\).
  • \(A\tan(kx)\): range \(\mathbb{R}\), and \(x\ne\frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2k}\).
  • \(A\cot(kx)\) or \(A\csc(kx)\): \(x\ne\frac{n\pi}{k}\).
  • \(A\sec\) or \(A\csc\): range \((-\infty,-|A|]\cup[|A|,\infty)\).
  • \(\frac{1}{a+b\sin}\): let \(M=a+|b|\), \(m=a-|b|\). If \(|a|>|b|\) the range is \(\left[\frac1M,\frac1m\right]\); if \(|a|<|b|\) it is \(\left(-\infty,\frac1m\right]\cup\left[\frac1M,\infty\right)\).
Common mistake

Writing the domain of \(\tan(kx)\) as \(x\ne\frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2}\) and forgetting to divide by \(k\). Also: writing the range of a reciprocal through zero as one piece, and writing a square bracket next to \(\infty\) (infinity always gets a round bracket).

Section 9.2

Periodicity of Trigonometric Functions

How long before the pattern repeats, and how to find it for sums, powers and shifted waves.

20. What is a periodic function? What is its period?
• Define periodic function and period.

\(f\) is periodic if there is a positive number \(p\) with \(f(x+p)=f(x)\) for every \(x\) in the domain. The period is the smallest such \(p\). The graph over one period (one cycle) simply repeats again and again.

21. Why is the period of \(\sin x\) equal to \(2\pi\) and not \(\pi\)?

If \(\sin(\theta+p)=\sin\theta\) for all \(\theta\), putting \(\theta=0\) gives \(\sin p=0\), so \(p\in\{\pi,2\pi,3\pi,\dots\}\). Try \(p=\pi\): \(\sin(\pi+\theta)=-\sin\theta\ne\sin\theta\), so it fails. Try \(p=2\pi\): \(\sin(2\pi+\theta)=\sin\theta\), it works. The smallest positive number that works is \(2\pi\). The same argument shows \(\cos x\) has period \(2\pi\).

22. Why do \(\tan x\) and \(\cot x\) repeat after \(\pi\)?

\(\tan(\pi+\theta)=\tan\theta\) because adding an even multiple of \(\frac{\pi}{2}\) does not change a function, and \(\pi=2\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}\). Nothing smaller works, so the period of \(\tan x\) and of \(\cot x\) is \(\pi\).

23. A function and its reciprocal: do they have the same period?

Yes. If \(p\) is the period of \(f(x)\), then \(\frac{1}{f(x)}\) has the same period \(p\). So \(\sec x\) and \(\csc x\) have period \(2\pi\).

24. How do I find the period of \(f(ax+b)\)?
• State the period of \(\sin(ax+b)\) and \(\tan(ax+b)\).

If \(p\) is the period of \(f(x)\), then \(f(ax+b)\) has period \(\frac{p}{|a|}\). Why: the new function repeats when the inside \(ax+b\) grows by \(p\). If \(x\) grows by \(h\), the inside grows by \(|a|h\), so we need \(|a|h=p\), i.e. \(h=\frac{p}{|a|}\). The shift \(b\) slides the graph but never changes the period, and neither does a vertical stretch or lift.

Tip

\(b>1\) squeezes the wave (more cycles in the same width); \(0<b<1\) stretches it. Use the Sinusoid lab and the Period detective to see this.

25. What is the rule for the period of a sum of two periodic functions?
• How do you find the period of \(f(x)+g(x)\)?

Write both periods as fractions in lowest terms (with \(\pi\) kept as a common factor). Then

\[\text{period of }f+g=\frac{\text{LCM of the numerators}}{\text{HCF of the denominators}}.\]

Why it works: we need a number \(T\) that is a whole-number multiple of both periods, and the smallest such number is this fraction. No-formula method: put both periods over a common denominator and take the LCM of the numerators.

Note

The rule gives a common period, and for every textbook question it is also the smallest. Simplify a product first if you can: \(\sin x\cos x=\tfrac12\sin2x\) has period \(\pi\), not \(2\pi\).

26. What happens to the period of \(\sin^2x\), \(|\sin x|\) and \(\sin^3x\)?
• Find the period of \(\cos^2x\).

An even power erases the minus sign: \(\sin^2(x+\pi)=(-\sin x)^2=\sin^2x\), so the pattern already repeats after \(\pi\). An odd power keeps the sign: \(\sin^3(x+\pi)=-\sin^3x\), so we still need \(2\pi\). An absolute value folds the negative lobes up and behaves like an even power. For tan, \(\tan(x+\pi)=\tan x\) already, so every power has period \(\pi\). Also \(\sin^2x=\frac{1-\cos2x}{2}\), and \(\cos2x\) has period \(\frac{2\pi}{2}=\pi\).

27. Give me the three-step method for any period question.
  • Strip the vertical stuff (the coefficient \(A\) and the \(+B\) at the end) and the phase shift inside.
  • For each function take the base period (\(2\pi\) for sin, cos, sec, cosec; \(\pi\) for tan, cot) and divide by \(|a|\).
  • For a sum combine with LCM over HCF. For a power or \(|\ |\) use the even/odd rule.

Section 9.3

Maximum and Minimum Values

The highest and lowest points of a wave, from just two numbers.

34. Which four families of functions does the book study for maximum and minimum?

\(a+b\sin\theta,\quad a+b\cos\theta,\quad a+b\sin(c\theta+d),\quad a+b\cos(c\theta+d)\), and their reciprocals \(\frac{1}{a+b\sin\theta}\) and so on. Here \(a,b,c,d\) are real numbers. Let \(M\) be the maximum and \(m\) the minimum.

35. Prove that \(M=a+|b|\) and \(m=a-|b|\).
• Show that the maximum of \(a+b\sin\theta\) is \(a+|b|\).

Start with \(-1\le\sin\theta\le1\). If \(b>0\), multiplying by \(b\) keeps the direction: \(-b\le b\sin\theta\le b\); adding \(a\) gives \(a-b\le a+b\sin\theta\le a+b\). If \(b<0\), multiplying by \(b\) reverses the direction: \(b\le b\sin\theta\le-b\), i.e. \(-|b|\le b\sin\theta\le|b|\); adding \(a\) gives the same result with \(|b|\). In both cases

\[M=a+|b|,\qquad m=a-|b|.\]

The numbers \(c\) and \(d\) do not matter: inside the bracket the sine still sweeps from \(-1\) to \(1\). They change the period and where the peaks are, not how high they are.

36. What is the picture behind \(M=a+|b|\), \(m=a-|b|\)?

\(a\) is the middle (the midline) and \(|b|\) is the half-height (the amplitude). So \(M=\text{middle}+\text{amplitude}\) and \(m=\text{middle}-\text{amplitude}\). Going backwards: \(a=\frac{M+m}{2}\) and \(|b|=\frac{M-m}{2}\). You will use exactly this in Section 9.6.

41. How do I find the maximum and minimum of a reciprocal \(y=\dfrac{1}{g(\theta)}\)?
• Find the maximum and minimum of \(\frac{1}{4+3\sin\theta}\).

Find \(M\) and \(m\) of the denominator \(g=a+b\sin(\cdots)\) first, then check the signs:

  • \(M\) and \(m\) have the same sign (so \(|a|>|b|\)): the new maximum is \(\frac1m\) and the new minimum is \(\frac1M\).
  • They have opposite signs (so \(|a|<|b|\)): the denominator passes through zero, so there is no maximum and no minimum. The range is \(\left(-\infty,\frac1m\right]\cup\left[\frac1M,\infty\right)\).
  • One of them is zero (\(|a|=|b|\)): the denominator touches zero without changing sign, so the graph runs to \(\infty\) in one direction only.
Tip

Try the Reciprocal lab in Section 9.1: set \(a=3,\ b=1\) first, then slowly raise \(b\) past \(a\) and watch the maximum and minimum disappear.

42. How do I find the maximum of \(a\cos x+b\sin x\)?
• Find the greatest value of \(7\cos x+24\sin x\).

This has two trig terms, so the earlier rule does not apply. Put \(R=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\) and choose \(\alpha\) with \(\cos\alpha=\frac aR\), \(\sin\alpha=\frac bR\). Then

\[a\cos x+b\sin x=R(\cos\alpha\cos x+\sin\alpha\sin x)=R\cos(x-\alpha),\]

and since \(-1\le\cos(x-\alpha)\le1\), the maximum is \(\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\) and the minimum is \(-\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\). Example: \(7\cos x+24\sin x\) has maximum \(\sqrt{49+576}=\sqrt{625}=25\). Pythagorean triples to spot instantly: \((3,4,5),\ (5,12,13),\ (8,15,17),\ (7,24,25),\ (20,21,29)\).

48. What does the graph of \(y=\tan x\) look like?
• Sketch \(y=\tan x\) on \([-\pi,\pi]\).

The period is \(\pi\). Tangent is undefined at \(x=\pm\frac{\pi}{2},\pm\frac{3\pi}{2},\dots\), so there are vertical asymptotes there (dashed lines the curve approaches but never touches). Between two asymptotes the curve passes through \((n\pi,0)\) and is always rising from \(-\infty\) to \(+\infty\). Useful points: \(\tan\frac\pi6\approx0.58\), \(\tan\frac\pi3\approx1.73\), \(\tan\frac{\pi}{4}=1\). It is an odd function, has no amplitude, and its range is all of \(\mathbb R\).

Common mistake

Never join the two branches of tangent with a smooth curve through the asymptote; the graph breaks there.

49. How is \(y=\cot x\) different from tangent?
• Draw the graph of \(y=\cot x\) in one period.

Period \(\pi\) again, but the asymptotes are at \(x=n\pi\) (where \(\sin x=0\)) and the zeros are at \(x=\frac\pi2+n\pi\) (where \(\cos x=0\)). Inside each branch the curve is always falling, the opposite of tan. At \(x=\frac\pi2\) the value is \(\cot\frac\pi2=\frac{\cos}{\sin}=\frac01=0\), and the graph looks like tan flipped left to right: \(\cot x=\tan\left(\frac\pi2-x\right)\). Points to plot: \(\cot\frac\pi6\approx1.73\), \(\cot\frac\pi3\approx0.58\), \(\cot\frac{\pi}{2}=0\), \(\cot\frac{2\pi}{3}\approx-0.58\), \(\cot\frac{5\pi}{6}\approx-1.73\); there is no point at \(x=0\) or \(\pm\pi\). It is odd.

58. What is the counting trick for products and quotients?

Count the odd factors (numerator and denominator together). An even number of odd factors gives an even function; an odd number gives an odd function. Example: \(\dfrac{x^2\tan x}{x+\sin x}\) has odd factors \(\tan x\) and \(x+\sin x\) (the bracket is odd+odd = odd). That is 2 odd factors, so the function is even.

Common mistake

A sum of an even and an odd term (\(x^2+x\), \(3\sin x+4\cos x\), \(x^2+\tan x\)) is neither. Check every bracket separately: \(x+\tan x\) is odd but \(x^2+\tan x\) is neither.

59. Give me the step-by-step method for any "check even or odd" question.
  • Replace \(x\) by \(-x\) everywhere.
  • Use \(\sin(-x)=-\sin x\), \(\cos(-x)=\cos x\), \(\tan(-x)=-\tan x\), \(\cot(-x)=-\cot x\), \(\sec(-x)=\sec x\), \(\csc(-x)=-\csc x\), and \((-x)^n\).
  • Simplify and compare \(f(-x)\) with \(f(x)\) and with \(-f(x)\).
  • Equal to \(f(x)\): even. Equal to \(-f(x)\): odd. Otherwise: neither (a numerical example proves it).

63. What is the big idea of a sinusoidal model?

Many real things go up and down in a regular cycle: a Ferris wheel rider, the voltage in a socket, a pendulum, tides. A smooth up-and-down cycle is a sinusoid, and we write its equation with sine or cosine. Use the Ferris wheel and AC labs below to see every number in the formula change the picture.

64. Chapter opener: a rider on the Ferris wheel at Ayub Park, Rawalpindi. How do we model the height?
• Model the height of a Ferris wheel rider.

66. Radians or degrees?

If the formula has \(\pi\) in it (like \(\frac{\pi t}{30}\)) the calculator must be in radian mode. If it uses \(\frac{360^\circ}{T}\) (like \(3.6t\) or \(21\,600\,t\)) it must be in degree mode. Both are correct as long as the mode matches. Mixing them (\(b=3.6\) with the calculator in radian mode) gives nonsense.

  • Start at the bottom \(\Rightarrow\) minus cosine. A pendulum released from its highest point \(\Rightarrow\) plus cosine.
  • Period from frequency: \(T=\frac1f\). For \(N\) times per minute: \(T=\frac{60}{N}\) seconds.
  • Every height question has two times in one cycle (once going up, once coming down), symmetric about the peak or trough.
Common mistake

Choosing \(+\cos\) when the rider starts at the bottom; using the diameter as the amplitude (it is the radius); forgetting to add the platform or centre height to the midline; giving only one of the two times for "when is the height \(h\)"; mixing degree and radian forms.

Connecting the Concepts

How the ideas of this unit lean on one another.

74. How are domain, range and the graph connected?

The domain is the set of \(x\)-values the graph covers, and the range is the set of \(y\)-values it reaches. For tan and sec the gaps in the domain show up as vertical asymptotes; for sec and cosec the gap in the range shows up as the empty strip \(-1<y<1\). Reading a graph left-right gives the domain, bottom-top gives the range.

75. How are range, maximum/minimum and amplitude connected?

For \(y=a+b\sin(\cdot)\) the range is \([a-|b|,\ a+|b|]\), so the maximum and minimum are its end-points, the midline is \(a\) and the amplitude is \(|b|\). Going the other way in word problems: midline \(=\frac{\max+\min}{2}\), amplitude \(=\frac{\max-\min}{2}\).

76. Why does the reciprocal of a wave have the same period but a very different range?

\(\frac{1}{f(x)}\) repeats exactly when \(f(x)\) repeats, so the period is the same. But \(f\) passing through \(0\) makes \(\frac{1}{f}\) blow up, so the range changes from a closed interval to a union of two unbounded pieces, as for sec and cosec.

77. How does the period connect to the graph and to the model?

The period \(T\) is the length of one cycle on the \(x\)-axis. In \(y=a\sin bx\) it is \(\frac{2\pi}{b}\), the five key points sit at \(0,\frac T4,\frac T2,\frac{3T}{4},T\), and in a word problem \(b=\frac{2\pi}{T}\) with \(T\) read from the story (a full turn of the wheel, one cycle of the supply).

78. How are even and odd connected to the graphs?

Even means the graph is a mirror image across the \(y\)-axis (cos, sec). Odd means the graph returns to itself after a half-turn about the origin (sin, tan, cot, cosec). The symmetry also tells you where to expect the pattern: an odd wave always passes through the origin, an even wave has a peak or trough on the \(y\)-axis.

79. How does \(a\cos x+b\sin x\) connect to everything else?

Using the addition formula it becomes the single wave \(R\cos(x-\alpha)\) with \(R=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\), so its amplitude is \(R\), its period is \(2\pi\), and its maximum and minimum are \(\pm R\). This is the bridge between the identities of Unit 8 and the wave ideas of this unit.

Find the domain and range of \(y=7\cot\dfrac{\pi}{2}x\).

Question:

Find the period of \(y=\cot4x+\sin\dfrac{5x}{2}\).

Period \(=\frac{4\pi}{1}=4\pi\). Check: \(4\pi=16\cdot\frac\pi4\) and \(4\pi=5\cdot\frac{4\pi}{5}\).

Question:

The supply voltage has maximum \(180\) V and frequency \(56\) Hz. Model it as \(V(t)=a\sin[k(t-d)]+c\) and find the period, \(k\), the amplitude and the model.

Period \(T=\frac{1}{56}\) s.
Degree mode: \(k=360\times56=20\,160\).

Question:

A Ferris wheel has diameter \(17\) m. You board at the bottom from a platform \(2\) m above the ground and take \(25\) s to reach the top. Find the equation of your height with respect to time.

Radius \(8.5\) m; lowest \(2\) m, highest \(2+17=19\) m; midline \(10.5\) m.
