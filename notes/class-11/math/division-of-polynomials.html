Chapter Psychology

What this unit is really about.

The one idea behind this unit

1. What is the one idea that holds Unit 5 together?
• How should I study Division of Polynomials?• What is this unit really testing?

Division of polynomials works exactly like division of numbers: \(11=5\times2+1\). For polynomials we write \(p(x)=d(x)\,q(x)+r(x)\). Now the surprise: if the divisor is \(x-c\), the leftover \(r\) is simply the value \(p(c)\). On the graph of \(y=p(x)\) it is the height of the curve at \(x=c\). If that height is 0 the curve touches the x-axis there, \(c\) is a zero and \(x-c\) is a factor. Every page of this unit is a variation on that picture, so every section has a graph you can move.

2. What kinds of questions come from this unit?

Five types cover almost everything: (1) find a remainder, (2) is \(x-c\) a factor, or find an unknown constant, (3) find zeros or make a polynomial from zeros, (4) factorize a cubic, (5) a word problem (box, area, ball, tickets) that turns into a cubic equation. Types 1, 2 and 4 need only the two theorems and a little arithmetic.

Study plan

Do the graphs first (each takes one minute), then the book examples, then the free exercise samples at the end of this page, then play the game.

3. Why does the unit start with algebraic expressions?

The theorems only work for polynomials. You must be able to say, for any expression, whether it is a polynomial, a rational expression or an irrational expression. Section 5.1 teaches this with a graph test too: a polynomial graph is one smooth, unbroken curve.

Section 5.1

Algebraic Expressions

Polynomial, rational or irrational, and how the degree names a polynomial.

Opening story

The book opens with the "Father of Algebra", Al-Khwarizmi, and asks you to graph a polynomial on a calculator. In this chapter you do that graphing right here on the page.

1. What is an algebraic expression? Name its parts.

An algebraic expression is a combination of numbers and variables joined by \(+,-,\times,\div\), powers and roots, such as \(3x^2-5x+7\). Its parts: terms (separated by + or −), coefficients (the numbers in front of variables), variables (letters) and constants (numbers with no variable).

2. Name the three kinds of algebraic expression.

Polynomial, rational and irrational. Section 5.1 teaches you to tell them apart.

3. Define a polynomial and write its general form.
• What is a polynomial?• Write the general form of a polynomial.

A polynomial in \(x\) is an expression of the form \[a_nx^n+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_1x+a_0\] where \(a_n\neq0\) and every exponent is a non-negative whole number \((0,1,2,3,\ldots)\). The coefficients \(a_i\) are real numbers. The term \(a_0\) is the constant term (it has \(x^0\)).

Book wording

The book says "positive integers (whole numbers)". The constant term has exponent 0, so the correct set is the non-negative whole numbers. (Math Unit 5 F1)

4. Why is \(x^{-3}\) not a polynomial?

Because \(x^{-3}=\dfrac{1}{x^3}\): the variable is in the denominator. A polynomial may only have whole-number exponents. For the same reason \(\sqrt{x}=x^{1/2}\) is not a polynomial.

5. Is \(\sqrt2\,x^4-\pi x^2-\sqrt{10}\) a polynomial?

Yes. The coefficients \(\sqrt2,\ -\pi,\ -\sqrt{10}\) are irrational numbers, which is allowed. What matters is that the exponents of \(x\) \((4,\,2,\,0)\) are whole numbers.

6. What is the degree of a polynomial? What if there are several variables?

The degree is the highest power of the variable. With several variables, add the exponents inside each term and take the largest sum. For \(5x^2y^3-2xy+7\) the terms have degrees \(5,\ 2,\ 0\), so the degree is \(5\).

7. What is the zero polynomial? What is its degree?

The zero polynomial is the constant \(0\). The book says it has no degree, because every power of \(x\) has coefficient 0. (Math Unit 5 F2)

8. Name polynomials by their degree.

In simple words

A zero of a polynomial is where its graph touches the x-axis. A polynomial of degree 3 has a graph that can cross the x-axis at most 3 times. This is the quickest way to remember "a polynomial of degree n has at most n zeros".

9. What is a rational expression?

A rational expression is a ratio \(\dfrac{P(x)}{Q(x)}\) of two polynomials with \(Q(x)\neq0\). Examples: \(\dfrac1x,\ \dfrac{2x+4}{x^2+5x+6}\). The denominator must not be zero because division by zero is undefined; on the graph this appears as a break.

10. Is every polynomial a rational expression?

Yes. Take \(Q(x)=1\): \(P(x)=\dfrac{P(x)}{1}\). But a rational expression with a variable in the denominator, like \(\dfrac1x\), is not a polynomial.

11. What is an irrational expression?

An expression in which a variable is under a root, or has a fractional exponent, such as \(\sqrt{x}+3\) or \(x^{3/2}y\). It cannot be written as \(\dfrac{P(x)}{Q(x)}\). Is \(\sqrt{b^2-4ac}\) a polynomial? No: it is an irrational expression (a root of an expression with variables).

Section 5.2

Division of Polynomial

Long division, the division identity and the degree rules.

1. Name the four parts of a division.

Dividend (what is divided), divisor (what we divide by), quotient (the answer) and remainder (what is left over). The book's pencil story: 11 pencils shared among 5 students gives \(11=5\times2+1\), so the quotient is 2 and the remainder is 1.

2. State the division identity.
• Write the relation between dividend, divisor, quotient and remainder.

\[p(x)=d(x)\cdot q(x)+r(x)\] that is, dividend = divisor × quotient + remainder, where \(r(x)=0\) or \(\deg r<\deg d\). Use it to check any division.

3. What are the steps of long division?
• Explain the method of long division of polynomials.
  1. Write dividend and divisor in descending powers; put \(0\) coefficients for missing powers.
  2. Divide the leading term of the dividend by the leading term of the divisor. This is the next term of the quotient.
  3. Multiply the whole divisor by that term and write the product under the dividend.
  4. Subtract (change every sign and add). Bring down the next term.
  5. Repeat. Stop when the leftover has degree less than the divisor.
4. Why must missing powers be written with coefficient 0?

So that every column holds one power of \(x\) and like terms line up. Write \(x^4+3x^2+1\) as \(x^4+0x^3+3x^2+0x+1\). Without the placeholders the columns shift and unlike terms get subtracted.

Common mistake

Leaving out 0x³ and 0x in x⁴ + 3x² + 1 and then subtracting 3x² from the x³ column.

5. When do we stop dividing?

When the leftover (the remainder) has a degree smaller than the degree of the divisor. For a linear divisor the remainder is a constant; for a quadratic divisor it is at most linear.

6. What are the degree rules (Pay Heed box)?

If \(\deg p\ge\deg d\): \(\deg q=\deg p-\deg d\) and \(\deg r<\deg d\). Example: a quartic divided by a quadratic gives a quadratic quotient and a remainder of degree 1 or less. If \(\deg p<\deg d\) the quotient is \(0\) and the remainder is \(p\) itself. (Math Unit 5 F17)

7. What is the sign rule in the subtraction step?

Subtraction changes the sign of every term of the product, then you add. \((x^3-6x^2)-(x^3-2x^2)=-4x^2\) because \(-6x^2-(-2x^2)=-4x^2\), not \(-8x^2\).

8. What is synthetic division?

A shortcut (W.H. extra, not in the book) for dividing by \(x-c\). Write only the coefficients, bring down the first, then multiply by \(c\) and add, column by column. The last number is the remainder (which equals \(p(c)\)); the other numbers are the coefficients of the quotient.

Section 5.3

Remainder Theorem

The remainder is just a value of the polynomial: p(c).

1. State the Remainder Theorem.
• State and explain the Remainder Theorem.

If a polynomial \(p(x)\) is divided by \(x-c\), the remainder is \(p(c)\).

2. Prove the Remainder Theorem.

Divide \(p(x)\) by \(x-c\): the remainder is a constant \(r\) (degree less than 1), so \[p(x)=(x-c)\,q(x)+r.\] Put \(x=c\): \(p(c)=(c-c)\,q(c)+r=r\). Hence \(r=p(c)\). \(\blacksquare\)

3. How do I use it for a divisor like x + 3?

Write the divisor as \(x-c\): \(x+3=x-(-3)\), so \(c=-3\) and the remainder is \(p(-3)\). Quick rule: solve divisor \(=0\) and substitute.

Most common mistake

Using p(3) for the divisor x + 3. The sign of c is opposite to the sign inside the divisor.

4. What if the divisor is \(ax+b\)?

Solve \(ax+b=0\): \(x=-\dfrac ba\). The remainder is \(p\!\left(-\dfrac ba\right)\). Example: divide by \(2x-1\); the remainder is \(p\!\left(\dfrac12\right)\).

5. Are there remainders I can read without any work?
Three instant remainders (W.H. tips)
DivisorRemainderIn words
\(x\)\(p(0)\)the constant term
\(x-1\)\(p(1)\)the sum of all the coefficients
\(x+1\)\(p(-1)\)the sum of even-power coefficients minus the sum of odd-power coefficients
6. What does the remainder look like on the graph?

It is the height of the curve at \(x=c\). Move the point below and watch the remainder change with the curve.

Section 5.3.1

Zeros of a Polynomial

Zeros are the x-intercepts of the graph.

1. What is a zero of a polynomial?
• Define a zero of a polynomial.

A number \(a\) is a zero (or root) of \(p(x)\) if \(p(a)=0\). On the graph of \(y=p(x)\), a real zero is an x-intercept: the curve meets the x-axis at \((a,0)\).

2. How do I check whether a number is a zero?

Substitute it into the polynomial. If the value is \(0\), it is a zero; otherwise it is not. This is the same calculation as the remainder of division by \(x-a\).

3. How many zeros can a polynomial have?

A polynomial of degree \(n\) has at most \(n\) real zeros. A cubic can have 1, 2 or 3; a quadratic 0, 1 or 2.

4. How do I make a polynomial from its zeros?

Each zero \(a\) gives a factor \(x-a\) (the sign flips). Multiply the factors. The answer is not unique: \(k(x-a)(x-b)(x-c)\) has the same zeros for every non-zero constant \(k\). The book takes \(k=1\). (Math Unit 5 F4)

For a fraction zero such as \(\dfrac35\) use the factor \(5x-3\) to keep the coefficients whole.

Section 5.4

Factor Theorem

A zero of the polynomial is a factor, and a factor is a zero.

1. State the Factor Theorem.
• State and explain the Factor Theorem.

\(x-c\) is a factor of \(p(x)\) if and only if \(p(c)=0\).

2. Prove the Factor Theorem.

(\(\Rightarrow\)) If \(x-c\) is a factor, then \(p(x)=(x-c)\,q(x)\), so \(p(c)=0\). (\(\Leftarrow\)) If \(p(c)=0\), the Remainder Theorem says the remainder of \(p(x)\div(x-c)\) is \(p(c)=0\), so \(p(x)=(x-c)\,q(x)\) and \(x-c\) is a factor. \(\blacksquare\)

3. How is the Factor Theorem different from the Remainder Theorem?
Remainder TheoremFactor Theorem
Givesthe remainder \(p(c)\)a yes/no test: is \(p(c)=0\)?
Works whenany value of \(p(c)\)\(p(c)=0\) only
Meaning on the graphheight of the curve at x = cthe curve touches the x-axis at x = c
4. What are the steps to test a factor?
  1. Write the divisor as \(x-c\) (solve divisor \(=0\)).
  2. Compute \(p(c)\).
  3. If \(p(c)=0\) the divisor is a factor; if not, it is only a divisor and the remainder is \(p(c)\).
5. Is every divisor a factor?

No. A divisor leaves a remainder in general. It is a factor only when the remainder is \(0\). \(x-3\) divides \(x^3-5x^2+7x-6\) but leaves \(-3\), so it is not a factor.

Section 5.5

Factorization of a Cubic Polynomial

Guess a zero, test it, divide, finish with the quadratic.

1. What are the steps to factorize a cubic?
• Explain how to factorize a cubic polynomial by the Factor Theorem.
  1. Guess: list the divisors of the constant term, with both signs (for a leading coefficient other than 1 also use fractions \(\frac pq\)).
  2. Test: find \(c\) with \(p(c)=0\). Then \(x-c\) is a factor.
  3. Divide: divide the cubic by \(x-c\) to get a quadratic.
  4. Finish: factorize the quadratic. Check the discriminant first.
2. Why do we try the divisors of the constant term?

For a monic cubic \((x-a)(x-b)(x-c)\) the constant term is \(-abc\), so each integer zero divides the constant term. For a non-monic cubic the Rational Root Theorem says any rational zero \(\dfrac pq\) (lowest terms) has \(p\mid a_0\) (constant term) and \(q\mid a_n\) (leading coefficient).

Book limit

The book states the "product of the zeros = constant term" idea for monic cubics only. For a cubic like 2y³ + y² − 8y − 4 also try fractions such as −1/2. (Math Unit 5 F10)

3. When do I stop factorizing the quadratic?

Compute the discriminant \(b^2-4ac\). If it is negative the quadratic has no real zeros, so stop. If it is positive but not a perfect square the roots are irrational and the book stops at the quadratic factor. If it is a perfect square, factorize further. (Math Unit 5 F6, F9)

4. Two factors are known. How do I find the third?

Multiply the two known factors, then divide the cubic by that product (Book Example 9). Or divide by one factor and factorize the quotient.

The graph shows why the discriminant matters. If the quadratic has no real zeros, the cubic crosses the x-axis only once, so there is only one real linear factor.

Book Example 9 (p. 158): two factors knownIntermediate
Question:

If two linear factors of \(2y^3+y^2-8y-4\) are \((2y+1)\) and \((y-2)\), find its third factor.

\((2y+1)(y-2)=2y^2-4y+y-2=2y^2-3y-2\)
(2y + 1)(y - 2)
= 2y(y - 2) + 1(y - 2)
= 2y^2 - 4y + y - 2
= 2y^2 - 3y - 2

The third factor is \(y+2\). So \(2y^3+y^2-8y-4=(2y+1)(y-2)(y+2)\).

Book Example 10 (p. 158): factorize by the Factor TheoremIntermediate
Question:

Factorize \(x^3-5x-2\) by the Factor Theorem.

Write \(f(x)=x^3+0x^2-5x-2\). Step I: \(f(-2)=-8+10-2=0\), so \(x+2\) is a factor. Step II: divide:

f(-2) = (-2)^3 - 5(-2) - 2
      = -8 + 10 - 2
      = 0

So \(x^3-5x-2=(x+2)(x^2-2x-1)\). The discriminant of \(x^2-2x-1\) is \(4+4=8\), not a perfect square, so the book stops here.

Section 5.6

Applications of the Remainder Theorem

Real problems become polynomial equations.

1. What is the pencil analogy?

Give 10 pencils to five students out of 11: each gets 2 and one pencil remains with you. So \(11=5\times2+1\): dividend \(=\) divisor \(\times\) quotient \(+\) remainder. Polynomials follow the same rule.

2. How do I solve a word problem with the theorems?
  1. Name the unknown \(x\) (usually the smallest or the base quantity).
  2. Write the other quantities with \(x\) from the sentences ("twice", "7 more than").
  3. Write the formula (volume \(=l\times w\times h\), area \(=l\times w\)) and substitute.
  4. Multiply out and move everything to one side to get \(p(x)=0\).
  5. Find one root by trial (divisors of the constant term) with the Factor Theorem. Divide to get the other roots.
  6. Reject negative or impossible roots. Check and write the units.
3. What is the height of the ball in the book, and what does the graph show?

\(h(t)=-16t^2+80t+5\) (feet, \(t\) in seconds). At \(t=0\) the height is \(5\) ft (the y-intercept). After 4 seconds \(h(4)=-256+320+5=69\) ft, which is also the remainder of \(h(t)\div(t-4)\). The graph is a downward parabola.

Misprint in the book

The book prints 16t² + 80t + 5 but uses −16 in the next line. This page uses −16. (Math Unit 5 F3)

Extra (W.H.): the highest point is at \(t=2.5\) s, \(h=105\) ft, and the ball lands when \(h(t)=0\), at \(t\approx5.06\) s.

Book Example 11 (pp. 159–160): the dimensions of a solidIntermediate
Question:

The volume of a rectangular solid is \(72\) cubic units. The width is twice the height and the length is \(7\) units more than the height. Find the dimensions.

Let the height be \(x\); width \(=2x\); length \(=x+7\).
\(72=(2x)(x+7)(x)=2x^3+14x^2\)
\(x^3+7x^2-36=0\)
Try \(x=2\): \(8+28-36=0\) ✔

Height \(=2\), width \(=4\), length \(=9\). Check: \(2\times4\times9=72\) ✔

Other answers? Divide \(x^3+7x^2-36\) by \(x-2\) to get \(x^2+9x+18=(x+3)(x+6)\). The other zeros \(-3\) and \(-6\) are negative, so a height cannot be negative: the solid is unique, \(2\times4\times9\).

Question:

A room has volume \(x^3+9x^2+26x+24\) cubic feet and height \(x+2\) feet. Find the area of its floor.

Volume \(=\) area of floor \(\times\) height, so floor area \(=\) volume \(\div\) height. Test: \(p(-2)=-8+36-52+24=0\), so \(x+2\) divides exactly.

\(x^3+9x^2+26x+24=(x+2)(x^2+7x+12)\)
Step 1: \(x^3\div x=x^2\); \(x^2(x+2)=x^3+2x^2\); leftover \(7x^2+26x\)
Step 2: \(7x^2\div x=7x\); \(7x(x+2)=7x^2+14x\); leftover \(12x+24\)
Step 3: \(12(x+2)=12x+24\); leftover \(0\)

Floor area \(=x^2+7x+12=(x+3)(x+4)\) square feet.

Connecting the Concepts

How the ideas of this unit lean on one another.

1. How are division, the Remainder Theorem, the Factor Theorem and zeros connected?
• Explain the link: division identity, Remainder Theorem, Factor Theorem.

The division identity \(p(x)=(x-c)\,q(x)+r\) is the root of everything. Put \(x=c\): the first term vanishes and \(r=p(c)\) (Remainder Theorem). If \(r=0\) the division is exact, so \(x-c\) is a factor (Factor Theorem) and \(c\) is a zero, which is an x-intercept of the graph. Once one factor is known, division leaves a lower-degree polynomial, which is how a cubic is factorized.

3. The remainder of \(p(x)\div(x-3)\) is 5. Is 3 a zero? Is \(x-3\) a factor?

The remainder is \(p(3)=5\neq0\), so \(3\) is not a zero and \(x-3\) is not a factor. It is only a divisor.

4. If \(p(2)=0\) and \(p(-1)=0\), which quadratic divides \(p(x)\)?

\(x-2\) and \(x+1\) are both factors, so \((x-2)(x+1)=x^2-x-2\) divides \(p(x)\).

5. The divisor is \(x+4\), the quotient \(3x^2-x+32\) and the remainder \(-121\). Find the dividend.

Dividend \(=\) quotient \(\times\) divisor \(+\) remainder \(=(3x^2-x+32)(x+4)-121=3x^3+11x^2+28x+128-121=3x^3+11x^2+28x+7\).

6. The graph of \(y=p(x)\) crosses the x-axis at \(-2,\ 1,\ 3\). Write the factors.

The x-intercepts are the zeros, so the factors are \((x+2),\ (x-1),\ (x-3)\), and \(p(x)=k(x+2)(x-1)(x-3)\) for some constant \(k\neq0\).

7. A polynomial leaves remainder 4 on division by \(x-2\) and 7 on division by \(x+1\). Find the remainder on division by \((x-2)(x+1)\).

The remainder has the form \(ax+b\). Then \(2a+b=4\) and \(-a+b=7\). Subtract: \(3a=-3\), \(a=-1\), \(b=6\). Remainder \(=-x+6\). Check: at \(x=2\) it gives 4, at \(x=-1\) it gives 7.

8. Why is the last number in synthetic division equal to p(c)?

Synthetic division is long division written compactly, so its last number is the remainder, which is \(p(c)\). It is the same as evaluating \(p(c)\) by repeated \"multiply by \(c\) and add\" (Horner's method).

Ex 5.2 · Q1 — Factorize \(y^3-7y-6\).Basic
Question:

Factorize \(y^3-7y-6\) by using the Factor Theorem.

\(f(y)=y^3+0y^2-7y-6\). Divisors of 6: \(\pm1,\pm2,\pm3,\pm6\).
\(f(-1)=-1+7-6=0\), so \(y+1\) is a factor.
f(-1) = (-1)^3 - 7(-1) - 6
      = -1 + 7 - 6
      = 0
\(y^2-y-6=(y-3)(y+2)\)

Answer: \(y^3-7y-6=(y+1)(y-3)(y+2)\).

Ex 5.2 · Q4 — Factorize \(3x^3-5x^2-36\).Intermediate
Question:

Factorize \(3x^3-5x^2-36\) by using the Factor Theorem.

\(f(x)=3x^3-5x^2+0x-36\). Try \(x=3\): \(81-45-36=0\), so \(x-3\) is a factor.
f(3) = 3(3)^3 - 5(3)^2 - 36
     = 81 - 45 - 36
     = 0

The quadratic \(3x^2+4x+12\) has discriminant \(16-144=-128<0\): stop.

Answer: \(3x^3-5x^2-36=(x-3)(3x^2+4x+12)\).

Ex 5.2 · Q8 — Factorize \(h(x)=4x^3+4x^2+73x+36\) given \(h(-\tfrac12)=0\).Advance
Question:

If \(h(x)=4x^3+4x^2+73x+36\) and \(h\!\left(-\dfrac12\right)=0\), factorize \(h(x)\).

\(h(-\tfrac12)=0\Rightarrow x+\tfrac12\) is a factor, i.e. \((2x+1)\). Divide:

h(-1/2) = 4(-1/2)^3 + 4(-1/2)^2 + 73(-1/2) + 36
        = -1/2 + 1 - 73/2 + 36
        = 0

The quadratic \(2x^2+x+36\) has discriminant \(1-288=-287<0\): no further real factors.

Answer: \(h(x)=(2x+1)(2x^2+x+36)\).

Ex 5.3 · Q4 — Dimensions of a box of volume 2475.Advance
Question:

The volume of a rectangular solid is \(2475\) cubic units. The length is three units more than twice the width and the height is two units less than the width. Find the dimensions.

Let width \(=w\); length \(=2w+3\); height \(=w-2\).
\(w(2w+3)(w-2)=2475\Rightarrow 2w^3-w^2-6w-2475=0\)
Estimate: \(w^3\approx1237\), so try \(w=11\): \(2662-121-66-2475=0\) ✔
w(2w + 3)(w - 2) = 2475
(2w + 3)(w - 2) = 2w^2 - w - 6
w(2w^2 - w - 6) = 2475
2w^3 - w^2 - 6w - 2475 = 0

The quadratic \(2w^2+21w+225\) has discriminant \(441-1800<0\), so \(w=11\) is the only real root.

Width 11, length 25, height 9. Check: \(11\times25\times9=2475\) ✔
