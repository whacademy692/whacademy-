The one-sentence summary

The whole unit grows from one formula, \(\cos(\alpha-\beta)=\cos\alpha\cos\beta+\sin\alpha\sin\beta\). Put \(-\beta\) for \(\beta\), put \(\beta=\alpha\), add or subtract two copies, and every other formula in the chapter appears. Learn the way they are derived and you never need to memorise them blindly.

Section 8.0 and 8.1

Warm-up: Unit Circle and Distance

The two ideas the proof of the fundamental law stands on.

Interactive diagram (needs JavaScript).

1. Distance formula: how do we find the distance between two points?
• Find the distance between \(A(5,4)\) and \(B(3,2)\).

For \(A(x_1,y_1)\) and \(B(x_2,y_2)\) the distance is

\(d=AB=\sqrt{(x_1-x_2)^2+(y_1-y_2)^2}\)

Book example: \(A(5,4)\), \(B(3,2)\) gives \(AB=\sqrt{(5-3)^2+(4-2)^2}=\sqrt{4+4}=\sqrt8\) units.

Why it works: join the two points, draw a horizontal and a vertical line to make a right triangle with legs \(|x_1-x_2|\) and \(|y_1-y_2|\). Pythagoras gives the hypotenuse. Because the legs are squared, the order of subtraction does not matter.
2. Why does the proof of the fundamental law need the distance formula?

The proof compares two equal chords of the unit circle. The only way to write the length of a chord in terms of cosines and sines is to use the coordinates of its end points in the distance formula.

3. Where does the point \((\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\) come from?

On the unit circle (radius 1, centre at the origin) the terminal side of an angle \(\theta\) meets the circle at a point whose x-coordinate is \(\cos\theta\) and whose y-coordinate is \(\sin\theta\). That is why \(\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1\): the point is on a circle of radius 1.

4. What is the ASTC rule?

It tells the sign of a ratio in each quadrant:

  • Quadrant I: All ratios positive.
  • Quadrant II: only Sine (and cosecant) positive.
  • Quadrant III: only Tangent (and cotangent) positive.
  • Quadrant IV: only Cosine (and secant) positive.
Memory trickStart at quadrant I and go anticlockwise: All Students Take Calculus.
5. How do I convert degrees and radians?

\(180^\circ=\pi\) radians. To change degrees to radians multiply by \(\frac{\pi}{180}\); to change radians to degrees multiply by \(\frac{180}{\pi}\). So \(30^\circ=\frac{\pi}{6}\), \(45^\circ=\frac{\pi}{4}\), \(60^\circ=\frac{\pi}{3}\), \(90^\circ=\frac{\pi}{2}\).

Section 8.2

The Fundamental Law and its Family

One proof, then every sum and difference formula follows.

Interactive diagram (needs JavaScript).

The law (book equation 1)

\(\cos(\alpha-\beta)=\cos\alpha\cos\beta+\sin\alpha\sin\beta\). The book proves it for \(\alpha>\beta\); in fact it holds for every pair of real numbers because cosine is an even function.

The proof, step by step

1. State and prove the fundamental law of trigonometry.
• Prove that \(\cos(\alpha-\beta)=\cos\alpha\cos\beta+\sin\alpha\sin\beta\).• Establish the fundamental law of trigonometry.

Setting. On the unit circle let \(\overline{OC}\) and \(\overline{OD}\) be the terminal sides of the angles \(\beta\) and \(\alpha\) with \(\alpha>\beta\). Then \(D(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)\) and \(C(\cos\beta,\sin\beta)\), and \(\angle COD=\alpha-\beta\). Make the same angle in standard position: \(A(1,0)\) and \(B(\cos(\alpha-\beta),\sin(\alpha-\beta))\), so \(\angle AOB=\alpha-\beta\).

  1. The angles \(\angle COD\) and \(\angle AOB\) are equal, so the chords are equal: \(CD=AB\).
  2. Use the distance formula on each side: \(\sqrt{(\cos\alpha-\cos\beta)^2+(\sin\alpha-\sin\beta)^2}=\sqrt{[\cos(\alpha-\beta)-1]^2+[\sin(\alpha-\beta)-0]^2}\).
  3. Square both sides.
  4. Expand: \(\cos^2\alpha+\cos^2\beta-2\cos\alpha\cos\beta+\sin^2\alpha+\sin^2\beta-2\sin\alpha\sin\beta=\cos^2(\alpha-\beta)+1-2\cos(\alpha-\beta)+\sin^2(\alpha-\beta)\).
  5. Group \(\cos^2+\sin^2\) and use \(\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1\) on every pair: \(1+1-2\cos\alpha\cos\beta-2\sin\alpha\sin\beta=1+1-2\cos(\alpha-\beta)\).
  6. Cancel \(2\) and divide by \(-2\):
\(\cos(\alpha-\beta)=\cos\alpha\cos\beta+\sin\alpha\sin\beta\)

\(\blacksquare\)

Short version for the examLeft side \(=2-2(\cos\alpha\cos\beta+\sin\alpha\sin\beta)\) and right side \(=2-2\cos(\alpha-\beta)\). Equate them.
2. Why are the two chords equal?

Chords that subtend equal angles at the centre of the same circle are equal. \(\angle COD\) and \(\angle AOB\) both measure \(\alpha-\beta\).

3. Does the book check the law with numbers?

Yes: \(\cos(90^\circ-60^\circ)=\cos90^\circ\cos60^\circ+\sin90^\circ\sin60^\circ\), that is \(\cos30^\circ=0\times0.5+1\times0.866=0.866\). Exactly, \(\cos30^\circ=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\) and the right side is \(0+\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\).

4. Do we need \(\alpha>\beta\) in the law?

No. If \(\alpha<\beta\), swap their roles: \(\cos(\beta-\alpha)=\cos(\alpha-\beta)\) because cosine is even, and the right side \(\cos\beta\cos\alpha+\sin\beta\sin\alpha\) does not change when the letters are swapped. Try \(\beta>\alpha\) in the diagram above.

Getting \(\cos(\alpha+\beta)\) and the four important results

5. Derive \(\cos(\alpha+\beta)\) (identity 2).
• Prove that \(\cos(\alpha+\beta)=\cos\alpha\cos\beta-\sin\alpha\sin\beta\).

Replace \(\beta\) by \(-\beta\) in the fundamental law:

\(\cos(\alpha+\beta)=\cos(\alpha-(-\beta))=\cos\alpha\cos(-\beta)+\sin\alpha\sin(-\beta)\)

Since \(\cos(-\beta)=\cos\beta\) and \(\sin(-\beta)=-\sin\beta\):

\(\cos(\alpha+\beta)=\cos\alpha\cos\beta-\sin\alpha\sin\beta\)
6. Result (i): prove \(\cos(-\beta)=\cos\beta\).

Put \(\alpha=0\) in the fundamental law: \(\cos(0-\beta)=\cos0\cos\beta+\sin0\sin\beta=1\cdot\cos\beta+0\), so \(\cos(-\beta)=\cos\beta\). Cosine is an even function.

7. Result (ii): prove \(\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\beta\right)=\sin\beta\).

Put \(\alpha=\frac{\pi}{2}\): \(\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\beta\right)=\cos\frac{\pi}{2}\cos\beta+\sin\frac{\pi}{2}\sin\beta=0\cdot\cos\beta+1\cdot\sin\beta=\sin\beta\).

8. Result (iii): prove \(\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha\right)=-\sin\alpha\).

Put \(\beta=-\frac{\pi}{2}\) in the law: \(\cos\left(\alpha+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=\cos\alpha\cos\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)+\sin\alpha\sin\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=\cos\alpha\cdot0+\sin\alpha\cdot(-1)=-\sin\alpha\).

9. Result (iv): prove \(\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha\right)=\cos\alpha\).

In result (ii) write \(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha\) for \(\beta\): \(\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha\right)\right)=\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha\right)\), that is \(\cos(-\alpha)=\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha\right)\). Since \(\cos(-\alpha)=\cos\alpha\), we get \(\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha\right)=\cos\alpha\).

10. Result (v): derive \(\sin(\alpha+\beta)\) (identity 3).
• Prove that \(\sin(\alpha\pm\beta)=\sin\alpha\cos\beta\pm\cos\alpha\sin\beta\).

In \(\cos(\alpha+\beta)\) replace \(\alpha\) by \(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha\):

\(\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+(\alpha+\beta)\right)=\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha\right)\cos\beta-\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha\right)\sin\beta\)

By result (iii) the left side is \(-\sin(\alpha+\beta)\). On the right use (iii) and (iv): \(-\sin(\alpha+\beta)=(-\sin\alpha)\cos\beta-(\cos\alpha)\sin\beta\). Multiply by \(-1\):

\(\sin(\alpha+\beta)=\sin\alpha\cos\beta+\cos\alpha\sin\beta\)

Now put \(-\beta\) for \(\beta\) (identity 4):

\(\sin(\alpha-\beta)=\sin\alpha\cos\beta-\cos\alpha\sin\beta\)
11. Check Point: is \(\sin\alpha=\cos(90^\circ-\alpha)\) true for every real \(\alpha\) or only for \(0<\alpha<90^\circ\)?

It is true for every real \(\alpha\). Result (ii) was proved with no condition on the angle. "Complementary angles" are needed only in the right-triangle definition, not in the unit-circle definition.

12. Check Point: Amna says \(\sin100^\circ=\cos(-10^\circ)\) without a calculator. Do you agree?

Yes. \(\sin100^\circ=\cos(90^\circ-100^\circ)=\cos(-10^\circ)\). As a check, \(\cos(-10^\circ)=\cos10^\circ\) and \(\sin100^\circ=\sin80^\circ=\cos10^\circ\).

13. Check Point: prove \(\sin(-\alpha)=-\sin\alpha\) and \(\tan(-\alpha)=-\tan\alpha\).

Put \(\alpha=0\) in identity (4): \(\sin(0-\alpha)=\sin0\cos\alpha-\cos0\sin\alpha=-\sin\alpha\). Then \(\tan(-\alpha)=\frac{\sin(-\alpha)}{\cos(-\alpha)}=\frac{-\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha}=-\tan\alpha\). Sine and tangent are odd functions.

Textbook printing note

The "Key Facts" box on page 197 prints \(\cos(-\theta)=\sin\theta\) and \(\tan(-\theta)=-\sin\theta\). These are misprints. The correct facts, proved above, are \(\sin(-\theta)=-\sin\theta\), \(\cos(-\theta)=\cos\theta\) and \(\tan(-\theta)=-\tan\theta\).

Tangent of a sum and a difference

14. Example 1 (book): prove \(\tan(\alpha+\beta)=\dfrac{\tan\alpha+\tan\beta}{1-\tan\alpha\tan\beta}\).
• Prove the formula for \(\tan(\alpha+\beta)\).

Write tangent as sine over cosine and use identities (2) and (3):

\(\tan(\alpha+\beta)=\dfrac{\sin(\alpha+\beta)}{\cos(\alpha+\beta)}=\dfrac{\sin\alpha\cos\beta+\cos\alpha\sin\beta}{\cos\alpha\cos\beta-\sin\alpha\sin\beta}\)

Divide the numerator and the denominator by \(\cos\alpha\cos\beta\):

\(=\dfrac{\frac{\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha}+\frac{\sin\beta}{\cos\beta}}{1-\frac{\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha}\cdot\frac{\sin\beta}{\cos\beta}}=\dfrac{\tan\alpha+\tan\beta}{1-\tan\alpha\tan\beta}\)

\(\blacksquare\)

15. Prove \(\tan(\alpha-\beta)=\dfrac{\tan\alpha-\tan\beta}{1+\tan\alpha\tan\beta}\).

Same method with \(\sin(\alpha-\beta)\) over \(\cos(\alpha-\beta)\), or replace \(\beta\) by \(-\beta\) in Example 1 and use \(\tan(-\beta)=-\tan\beta\).

16. For which angles are the tangent formulas valid?

Whenever \(\cos\alpha\neq0\), \(\cos\beta\neq0\) and the tangent on the left, \(\tan(\alpha\pm\beta)\), is defined, that is \(\cos(\alpha\pm\beta)\neq0\). The book states this as a Key Fact.

17. What are the six boxed formulas I must know?
\(\cos(\alpha\pm\beta)=\cos\alpha\cos\beta\mp\sin\alpha\sin\beta\)
\(\sin(\alpha\pm\beta)=\sin\alpha\cos\beta\pm\cos\alpha\sin\beta\)
\(\tan(\alpha\pm\beta)=\dfrac{\tan\alpha\pm\tan\beta}{1\mp\tan\alpha\tan\beta}\)
Memory tricks
  • cos: "cos-cos, sin-sin, SIGN OPPOSITE".
  • sin: "sin-cos, cos-sin, SIGN SAME".
  • tan: the top keeps the sign, the bottom flips it, and the bottom always starts with \(1\).
18. How do I find \(\sin(\alpha\pm\beta)\) when ratios are given?

Use the three-step recipe:

  1. Find the other ratios of each angle (a right triangle with Pythagoras, and the sign from the quadrant).
  2. Substitute into the correct formula with brackets, watching every minus sign.
  3. If asked for the quadrant of \(\alpha+\beta\), read it from the signs of \(\sin(\alpha+\beta)\) and \(\cos(\alpha+\beta)\).
Common mistakes in this section

  • \(\sin(\alpha+\beta)=\sin\alpha+\sin\beta\) is wrong. Test \(\alpha=\beta=90^\circ\): the left side is \(0\), the right side is \(2\).
  • \(\cos(\alpha-\beta)=\cos\alpha-\cos\beta\) is wrong.
  • Forgetting that \(\cos(\alpha+\beta)\) has a minus in the middle.
  • Writing \(1+\tan\alpha\tan\beta\) in the denominator of \(\tan(\alpha+\beta)\); that belongs to \(\tan(\alpha-\beta)\).

Section 8.3

Allied Angles and Exact Values

Quarter-turns and half-turns around the unit circle, and what they do to a ratio.

Interactive diagram (needs JavaScript).

What are allied angles?

Angles joined to a basic angle \(\theta\) by a right angle or a multiple of it: \(\frac{\pi}{2}\pm\theta,\ \pi\pm\theta,\ \frac{3\pi}{2}\pm\theta,\ 2\pi\pm\theta\) and so on. Their ratios are the ratios of \(\theta\) itself, with at most a change of name (sin and cos swap) and a change of sign.

1. What are the book's four Key Facts about allied angles?
  1. When the allied angle contains an odd multiple of a right angle (\(90^\circ\) or \(270^\circ\), that is \(\frac{\pi}{2}\) or \(\frac{3\pi}{2}\)), the ratio changes to its co-ratio (sin and cos, tan and cot, sec and csc swap).
  2. When it contains an even multiple (\(180^\circ\) or \(360^\circ\)), the ratio does not change name.
  3. The sign is decided by the quadrant of the terminal arm (ASTC).
  4. The same results hold for the reciprocal ratios csc, sec and cot.

3. How do I solve any allied angle without memorising the table?

Ask two questions about \(k\cdot90^\circ\pm\theta\), taking \(\theta\) as a small acute angle:

QuestionRule
Q1. Does the name change?If \(k\) is odd (1 or 3): yes, sin \(\leftrightarrow\) cos and tan \(\leftrightarrow\) cot. If \(k\) is even (2 or 4): no.
Q2. What is the sign?Find the quadrant where \(k\cdot90^\circ\pm\theta\) falls. Use the sign of the original function there (ASTC).
Three quick examples
  • \(\cos(270^\circ+\theta)\): \(270=3\times90\), odd, so cos becomes sin. The angle is in quadrant IV, where cos is positive. Answer \(+\sin\theta\).
  • \(\tan(180^\circ-\theta)\): \(180=2\times90\), even, so the name stays. Quadrant II, tan negative. Answer \(-\tan\theta\).
  • \(\sin(90^\circ+\theta)\): odd, so sin becomes cos. Quadrant II, sin positive. Answer \(+\cos\theta\).
Biggest mistake: taking the sign of the new function. In \(\cos(270^\circ+\theta)\) the sign comes from cos in quadrant IV (positive), not from sin (negative there).
4. How is the table proved from the fundamental formulas?
• Prove the allied-angle identities.

Use \(\sin\pi=0,\ \cos\pi=-1,\ \sin\frac{3\pi}{2}=-1,\ \cos\frac{3\pi}{2}=0,\ \sin2\pi=0,\ \cos2\pi=1\) with the sum and difference formulas:

  • \(\sin(\pi-\theta)=\sin\pi\cos\theta-\cos\pi\sin\theta=0+\sin\theta=\sin\theta\)
  • \(\cos(\pi+\theta)=\cos\pi\cos\theta-\sin\pi\sin\theta=-\cos\theta\)
  • \(\cos\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-\theta\right)=\cos\frac{3\pi}{2}\cos\theta+\sin\frac{3\pi}{2}\sin\theta=0-\sin\theta=-\sin\theta\)
  • \(\sin\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}+\theta\right)=\sin\frac{3\pi}{2}\cos\theta+\cos\frac{3\pi}{2}\sin\theta=-\cos\theta\)
  • \(\cos(2\pi-\theta)=\cos2\pi\cos\theta+\sin2\pi\sin\theta=\cos\theta\)

The tangent rows follow from \(\tan=\frac{\sin}{\cos}\); for example \(\tan(\pi+\theta)=\frac{-\sin\theta}{-\cos\theta}=\tan\theta\).

Exact values from the sum and difference formulas

Interactive diagram (needs JavaScript).

6. Example 2 (book): use \(60^\circ-45^\circ=15^\circ\) to find \(\cos15^\circ\), \(\sin15^\circ\) and \(\tan15^\circ\).
• Find the exact value of \(\cos15^\circ\), \(\sin15^\circ\) and \(\tan15^\circ\).

Let \(\alpha=60^\circ\), \(\beta=45^\circ\). The book works with decimals:

\(\cos15^\circ=\cos60^\circ\cos45^\circ+\sin60^\circ\sin45^\circ=0.5(0.707)+0.866(0.707)=0.966\)
\(\sin15^\circ=\sin60^\circ\cos45^\circ-\cos60^\circ\sin45^\circ=0.866(0.707)-0.5(0.707)=0.259\)
\(\tan15^\circ=\dfrac{\sin15^\circ}{\cos15^\circ}=\dfrac{0.259}{0.966}=0.268\)

Exact (surd) form, which is what an exercise means by "exact value":

\(\cos15^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4},\quad\sin15^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt6-\sqrt2}{4},\quad\tan15^\circ=2-\sqrt3\)
Easy memory: \(\cos15^\circ=\sin75^\circ\) is the bigger one (plus sign), \(\sin15^\circ=\cos75^\circ\) the smaller one (minus sign). Also \(\tan15^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt3-1}{1+\sqrt3}=\dfrac{(\sqrt3-1)^2}{2}=2-\sqrt3\).
7. Example 3 (book): \(\sin A=\frac15\), \(\sin B=\frac13\) with \(A\), \(B\) in quadrant II. Find \(\cos(A+B)\) and the quadrant of \(A+B\).

Find the cosines first, with the quadrant II sign:

\(\cos^2A=1-\left(\frac15\right)^2=\frac{24}{25}\Rightarrow\cos A=-\dfrac{\sqrt{24}}{5}\)
\(\cos^2B=1-\left(\frac13\right)^2=\frac89\Rightarrow\cos B=-\dfrac{\sqrt8}{3}\)
\(\cos(A+B)=\cos A\cos B-\sin A\sin B=\dfrac{\sqrt{24}}{5}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt8}{3}-\dfrac15\cdot\dfrac13=\dfrac{\sqrt{192}}{15}-\dfrac1{15}=\dfrac{8\sqrt3-1}{15}\approx0.857\)

\(\cos(A+B)\) is positive, so the terminal arm of \(A+B\) is in quadrant IV. (Reason: \(A,B\in(90^\circ,180^\circ)\) gives \(A+B\in(180^\circ,360^\circ)\), and cos is positive only in IV there.)

Simplify surds: \(\sqrt{192}=\sqrt{64\times3}=8\sqrt3\).
8. Check Point (book): find \(\tan y\) when \(\tan(y-45^\circ)=\frac13\).

\(\tan(y-45^\circ)=\dfrac{\tan y-1}{1+\tan y}=\dfrac13\). Cross-multiply: \(3\tan y-3=1+\tan y\), so \(2\tan y=4\) and \(\tan y=2\). Check: \(\dfrac{2-1}{1+2}=\dfrac13\).

9. Check Point (book): find the exact value of (i) \(\cos105^\circ\) (ii) \(\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{3}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\).

(i) \(\cos105^\circ=\cos(60^\circ+45^\circ)=\frac12\cdot\frac{\sqrt2}{2}-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt2}{2}=\dfrac{\sqrt2-\sqrt6}{4}=-\dfrac{\sqrt6-\sqrt2}{4}\).

(ii) \(\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{3}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)=\cos\frac{\pi}{12}=\frac12\cdot\frac{\sqrt2}{2}+\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt2}{2}=\dfrac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}\).

10. Example 4 (book): if \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) are the angles of a triangle, prove \(\cot\beta\cot\gamma+\cot\alpha\cot\gamma+\cot\alpha\cot\beta=1\).
• Prove the cotangent identity for the angles of a triangle.

Since \(\alpha+\beta+\gamma=180^\circ\), we have \(\alpha+\beta=180^\circ-\gamma\). Take the cotangent of both sides and then reciprocate:

\(\tan(\alpha+\beta)=\tan(180^\circ-\gamma)=-\tan\gamma\)
\(\dfrac{\tan\alpha+\tan\beta}{1-\tan\alpha\tan\beta}=-\tan\gamma\Rightarrow\tan\alpha+\tan\beta=-\tan\gamma+\tan\alpha\tan\beta\tan\gamma\)
\(\Rightarrow\tan\alpha+\tan\beta+\tan\gamma=\tan\alpha\tan\beta\tan\gamma\)

Divide every term by \(\tan\alpha\tan\beta\tan\gamma\):

\(\cot\beta\cot\gamma+\cot\alpha\cot\gamma+\cot\alpha\cot\beta=1\)

\(\blacksquare\)

11. What is the general pattern for triangle identities?
  1. Join two angles: \(\alpha+\beta=180^\circ-\gamma\).
  2. Take sin, cos or tan of both sides. The right side is an allied angle and turns into a ratio of \(\gamma\).
  3. Expand the left side with a sum formula and simplify.
Result to put on your formula sheet: in a triangle \(\tan\alpha+\tan\beta+\tan\gamma=\tan\alpha\tan\beta\tan\gamma\).

1. Idea: why does \(a\sin\theta+b\cos\theta\) become a single sine?

\(a\sin\theta\) and \(b\cos\theta\) are waves with the same period \(2\pi\). Their sum is again a wave of period \(2\pi\), only taller (height \(r\)) and shifted sideways (by \(\varphi\)). The widget shows the thick red sum landing exactly on the dashed black \(r\sin(\theta+\varphi)\).

2. Derive \(a\sin\theta+b\cos\theta=r\sin(\theta+\varphi)\).
• Express \(a\sin\theta+b\cos\theta\) in the form \(r\sin(\theta+\varphi)\).

Let \(P(a,b)\) be a point and \(\varphi\) the angle that \(\overrightarrow{OP}\) makes with the x-axis. From the right triangle:

\(a=r\cos\varphi,\quad b=r\sin\varphi,\quad r=\sqrt{a^2+b^2},\quad\varphi=\tan^{-1}\dfrac ba\)

Substitute into the expression:

\(a\sin\theta+b\cos\theta=r\cos\varphi\sin\theta+r\sin\varphi\cos\theta=r(\sin\theta\cos\varphi+\cos\theta\sin\varphi)\)
\(a\sin\theta+b\cos\theta=r\sin(\theta+\varphi),\qquad r=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\)
\(a\) is the coefficient of \(\sin\theta\), \(b\) the coefficient of \(\cos\theta\). Always \(r>0\), and \(\cos\varphi=\frac ar\), \(\sin\varphi=\frac br\).
3. Example 5 (book): express \(12\sin\theta+5\cos\theta\) in the form \(r\sin(\theta+\varphi)\).

\(12\sin\theta+5\cos\theta=13\sin(\theta+\varphi),\quad\varphi=\tan^{-1}\dfrac5{12}\approx22.62^\circ\)

Double angle identities (book 8.5, equations 4 to 11)

1. Derive the double angle identities.
• Prove that \(\sin2\alpha=2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\).• Prove that \(\cos2\alpha=\cos^2\alpha-\sin^2\alpha=2\cos^2\alpha-1=1-2\sin^2\alpha\).

Put \(\beta=\alpha\) in the sum formulas.

\(\sin(\alpha+\alpha)=\sin\alpha\cos\alpha+\cos\alpha\sin\alpha\ \Rightarrow\ \sin2\alpha=2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\)
\(\cos(\alpha+\alpha)=\cos\alpha\cos\alpha-\sin\alpha\sin\alpha\ \Rightarrow\ \cos2\alpha=\cos^2\alpha-\sin^2\alpha\)

With \(\sin^2\alpha+\cos^2\alpha=1\), the cosine one has two more forms:

\(\cos2\alpha=2\cos^2\alpha-1=1-2\sin^2\alpha\)
\(\tan2\alpha=\dfrac{\tan\alpha+\tan\alpha}{1-\tan\alpha\tan\alpha}=\dfrac{2\tan\alpha}{1-\tan^2\alpha}\)
2. Which of the three forms of \(\cos2\alpha\) should I choose?
Pick the form that matches what you know
  • You know \(\sin\alpha\): use \(1-2\sin^2\alpha\).
  • You know \(\cos\alpha\): use \(2\cos^2\alpha-1\).
  • You know both, or want a product: use \(\cos^2\alpha-\sin^2\alpha=(\cos\alpha-\sin\alpha)(\cos\alpha+\sin\alpha)\).
  • You know \(\tan\alpha\): use \(\dfrac{1-\tan^2\alpha}{1+\tan^2\alpha}\).
3. Derive the power-reduction identities.
• Prove that \(\cos^2\alpha=\frac{1+\cos2\alpha}{2}\).

Rearrange the second and third forms of \(\cos2\alpha\):

\(\cos^2\alpha=\dfrac{1+\cos2\alpha}{2},\qquad\sin^2\alpha=\dfrac{1-\cos2\alpha}{2},\qquad\tan^2\alpha=\dfrac{1-\cos2\alpha}{1+\cos2\alpha}\)

(The third is the second divided by the first.) They remove squares, which is why they are the tool for writing \(\sin^4x\) with cosines of power 1.

4. When is \(\tan2\alpha\) undefined?

When \(\cos2\alpha=0\), that is \(2\alpha=90^\circ,270^\circ,\dots\) and also when \(\tan\alpha\) itself is undefined. In the formula \(\frac{2\tan\alpha}{1-\tan^2\alpha}\) this shows up as a zero denominator at \(\tan\alpha=\pm1\). Write "undefined", never a number.

Left side \(=\sin^2\theta-2\sin\theta\cos\theta+\cos^2\theta=(\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta)-\sin2\theta=1-\sin2\theta\). \(\checkmark\)

Sum or difference to product (book 8.6.2)

4. Derive the four sum-to-product formulas.
• Express a sum or difference of sines and cosines as a product.

Put \(\alpha=\frac{p+q}{2}\) and \(\beta=\frac{p-q}{2}\). Then \(\alpha+\beta=p\) and \(\alpha-\beta=q\). Substituting in the product-to-sum formulas gives

\(\sin p+\sin q=2\sin\dfrac{p+q}{2}\cos\dfrac{p-q}{2}\)
\(\sin p-\sin q=2\cos\dfrac{p+q}{2}\sin\dfrac{p-q}{2}\)
\(\cos p+\cos q=2\cos\dfrac{p+q}{2}\cos\dfrac{p-q}{2}\)
\(\cos p-\cos q=-2\sin\dfrac{p+q}{2}\sin\dfrac{p-q}{2}\)
5. How do I remember them?
Two memory rules
  • In a sum the two functions in the answer are the same kind as in the question: sin+sin gives sin·cos, cos+cos gives cos·cos. In a difference they swap: sin−sin gives cos·sin.
  • Only \(\cos p-\cos q\) carries a −2.
  • The angles are always the average \(\frac{p+q}{2}\) and the half-difference \(\frac{p-q}{2}\).
If \(q>p\) the half-difference is negative: use \(\cos(-\theta)=\cos\theta\) and \(\sin(-\theta)=-\sin\theta\). Example: \(\cos2\alpha-\cos4\alpha=-2\sin3\alpha\sin(-\alpha)=2\sin3\alpha\sin\alpha\).
6. When should I use sum to product?
  • Two sines or two cosines are added or subtracted.
  • Several terms have angles in an arithmetic progression (Example 17).
  • A fraction has such sums above and below and a common factor will cancel (Example 16).

\(\sin30^\circ=\tfrac12\Rightarrow\text{L.H.S}=\tfrac14(2\sin70^\circ\cos20^\circ)\cos10^\circ=\tfrac14[\sin90^\circ+\sin50^\circ]\cos10^\circ\)
\(=\tfrac14\cos10^\circ+\tfrac18(2\sin50^\circ\cos10^\circ)=\tfrac14\cos10^\circ+\tfrac18[\sin60^\circ+\sin40^\circ]\)
\(=\tfrac14\cos10^\circ+\dfrac{\sqrt3}{16}+\tfrac18\sin40^\circ\)
Numeric check: left \(\approx0.4348\), right \(\approx0.2462+0.1083+0.0804=0.4348\). Strategy: take the factors two at a time and apply one product-to-sum at each step.
Common mistakes in this section

  • Writing \(\sin\alpha\sin\beta=\frac12[\cos(\alpha+\beta)-\cos(\alpha-\beta)]\); the correct order is \(\frac12[\cos(\alpha-\beta)-\cos(\alpha+\beta)]\).
  • Forgetting the factor \(-2\) in \(\cos p-\cos q\).
  • Not making the coefficient 2 before using a product-to-sum formula.
  • Using \(\cos\alpha\sin\beta\) with the angles in the wrong order, which flips the sign of \(\sin(\alpha-\beta)\).

Connecting the Concepts

How the ideas of this unit lean on one another.

1. How is every formula in this unit connected to one another?

Follow the arrows:

  1. Distance formula + unit circle \(\Rightarrow\) \(\cos(\alpha-\beta)\).
  2. Replace \(\beta\) by \(-\beta\), use \(\cos(-\beta)=\cos\beta\) and \(\sin(-\beta)=-\sin\beta\) \(\Rightarrow\) \(\cos(\alpha+\beta)\).
  3. Use \(\cos(90^\circ-\theta)=\sin\theta\) \(\Rightarrow\) \(\sin(\alpha\pm\beta)\), then divide \(\Rightarrow\) \(\tan(\alpha\pm\beta)\).
  4. Put \(\alpha\) or \(\beta\) equal to \(90^\circ,180^\circ,270^\circ,360^\circ\) \(\Rightarrow\) the allied angle rules.
  5. Put \(\beta=\alpha\) \(\Rightarrow\) double angle; \(\beta=2\alpha\) \(\Rightarrow\) triple angle; replace \(\alpha\) by \(\frac\theta2\) \(\Rightarrow\) half angle.
  6. Add or subtract the sum and difference formulas \(\Rightarrow\) product to sum, and read them backwards \(\Rightarrow\) sum to product.
  7. Expand \(r\sin(\theta+\varphi)\) and compare with \(a\sin\theta+b\cos\theta\) \(\Rightarrow\) the amplitude form.

3. How do I prove an identity without getting lost?
  1. Start from the more complicated side and move towards the simpler one. Never move terms across the \(=\) sign.
  2. Convert everything to \(\sin\) and \(\cos\) if you are unsure.
  3. Spot a double angle: break it (\(\sin2x\to2\sin x\cos x\)) or make it (\(2\sin x\cos x\to\sin2x\)).
  4. In a fraction, take a common denominator, or look for \((\cos x-\sin x)(\cos x+\sin x)=\cos2x\).
  5. End with the other side of the identity and write \(\blacksquare\) (proved).
Not sure the identity is even true? Test it at \(30^\circ\) before you spend ten lines on it.
4. Why does the unit stress exact values?

Exact values such as \(\sin15^\circ=\frac{\sqrt6-\sqrt2}{4}\) let you check an answer without a calculator, and they appear in nearly every board paper. Learn the table in the cheat sheet below and the six values \(\sin15^\circ,\cos15^\circ,\tan15^\circ,\sin75^\circ,\cos75^\circ,\tan75^\circ\).

Important terms you must know: learn these definitions for exam questions.

TermMeaning
Trigonometric identityAn equation true for every angle for which both sides are defined, for example \(\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1\).

Ex 8.1 · Q2 (a), (b) — Find \(\cos15^\circ\) and \(\cos165^\circ\).Basic
Question:

Find the exact value of \(\cos15^\circ\) using \(\cos(45^\circ-30^\circ)\), then of \(\cos165^\circ\).

\(\cos15^\circ=\cos45^\circ\cos30^\circ+\sin45^\circ\sin30^\circ=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2}+\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\frac12=\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}\)
\(\cos165^\circ=\cos(180^\circ-15^\circ)=-\cos15^\circ\)

Answer: \(\cos15^\circ=\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}\), \(\cos165^\circ=-\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}\)

Ex 8.1 · Q5 — Find \(\cos(\alpha+\beta)\) and \(\cos(\alpha-\beta)\).Intermediate
Question:

If \(\sin\alpha=\frac45\) and \(\tan\beta=-\frac5{12}\), and both angles lie in quadrant II, find \(\cos(\alpha+\beta)\) and \(\cos(\alpha-\beta)\).

\(\cos\alpha=-\sqrt{1-\frac{16}{25}}=-\frac35\). The 5, 12, 13 triangle with \(\tan\beta=-\frac5{12}\) in QII gives \(\sin\beta=\frac5{13}\), \(\cos\beta=-\frac{12}{13}\)
\(\cos(\alpha+\beta)=\left(-\frac35\right)\left(-\frac{12}{13}\right)-\frac45\cdot\frac5{13}=\frac{36}{65}-\frac{20}{65}=\frac{16}{65}\)
\(\cos(\alpha-\beta)=\frac{36}{65}+\frac{20}{65}=\frac{56}{65}\)

Answer: \(\cos(\alpha+\beta)=\frac{16}{65}\), \(\cos(\alpha-\beta)=\frac{56}{65}\)

Ex 8.1 · Q9 — Obtuse angles: find \(\sin(\alpha\pm\beta)\) and \(\tan(\alpha\pm\beta)\).Intermediate
Question:

If \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are obtuse, \(\sin\alpha=\frac1{\sqrt2}\) and \(\cos\beta=-\frac35\), find \(\sin(\alpha\pm\beta)\) and \(\tan(\alpha\pm\beta)\).

Obtuse means quadrant II: \(\cos\alpha=-\frac1{\sqrt2}\), \(\sin\beta=\frac45\)
\(\sin(\alpha+\beta)=\frac1{\sqrt2}\left(-\frac35\right)+\left(-\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)\frac45=-\frac7{5\sqrt2}\)
\(\sin(\alpha-\beta)=-\frac3{5\sqrt2}+\frac4{5\sqrt2}=\frac1{5\sqrt2}\)
\(\cos(\alpha+\beta)=\frac3{5\sqrt2}-\frac4{5\sqrt2}=-\frac1{5\sqrt2}\), \(\cos(\alpha-\beta)=\frac3{5\sqrt2}+\frac4{5\sqrt2}=\frac7{5\sqrt2}\)
\(\tan(\alpha+\beta)=\frac{-7}{-1}=7\), \(\tan(\alpha-\beta)=\frac{1}{7}\)

Answer: \(\sin(\alpha\pm\beta)=-\frac{7\sqrt2}{10},\ \frac{\sqrt2}{10}\); \(\tan(\alpha\pm\beta)=7,\ \frac17\)

Ex 8.1 · Q11 (i) — Show that the fraction equals 1.Intermediate
Question:

Show that \(\dfrac{\sin(180^\circ+\lambda)\cos(270^\circ+\lambda)}{\sin(180^\circ-\lambda)\cos(270^\circ-\lambda)}=1\).

\(\sin(180^\circ+\lambda)=-\sin\lambda\); \(\cos(270^\circ+\lambda)=\sin\lambda\); \(\sin(180^\circ-\lambda)=\sin\lambda\); \(\cos(270^\circ-\lambda)=-\sin\lambda\)
\(\text{L.H.S}=\dfrac{(-\sin\lambda)(\sin\lambda)}{(\sin\lambda)(-\sin\lambda)}=1=\text{R.H.S}\)

Answer: Proved.

Express \(12\sin\theta-5\cos\theta\) in the form \(r\sin(\theta+\varphi)\).

\(\varphi=-\tan^{-1}\frac5{12}\approx-22.6^\circ\)
