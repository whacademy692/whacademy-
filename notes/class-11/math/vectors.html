Chapter Psychology

What this unit is really about, and where the exercise marks are.

The one idea behind this unit

1. What is the one idea that holds Unit 3 together?
• How should I study Vectors in Space?• What is this unit really testing?

A vector is an arrow: it has a length and a direction. In space we write it with three numbers, \(\vec r=x\hat i+y\hat j+z\hat k\), and every operation becomes a small piece of arithmetic on those three numbers:

  • adding and subtracting arrows is adding or subtracting the matching numbers;
  • the dot product multiplies matching numbers and adds them: it gives one number (angle, projection, work);
  • the cross product is a determinant: it gives a new arrow that is perpendicular to both (area, moment);
  • the triple product is a \(3\times3\) determinant: it gives one number (volume, coplanar test).
In simple words

Draw the arrow first. Then ask: "Do I need a number (dot, triple product) or an arrow (cross product)?" That one question picks the right tool for most exercise questions. Every diagram on this page can be moved with your finger.

Section 3.1

Vectors: Arrows with Direction

Scalars and vectors, the basic words, and how to add, subtract and scale vectors.

Scalar, vector and the picture of a vector

2. What is the difference between a scalar and a vector?
• Define scalar and vector with examples.• Which of these is a vector quantity?

A scalar has only a size (magnitude): mass, time, distance, speed, temperature, work. A vector has a size and a direction: displacement, velocity, force, acceleration, weight, moment.

ScalarVector
distance \(=5\) kmdisplacement \(=5\) km towards north
speed \(=60\) km/hvelocity \(=60\) km/h towards east
mass, time, energy, workforce, momentum, moment of a force
Tricky pair

Speed and velocity are not the same. A car going round a circle at \(60\) km/h has constant speed but changing velocity, because its direction keeps changing.

3. How is a vector drawn, and what are its tail and head?
• What is the geometrical representation of a vector?

A vector is drawn as an arrow. The starting end is the tail (initial point) and the pointed end is the head (terminal point). For the arrow from \(A\) to \(B\) we write \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) or \(\vec a\). The length of the arrow is the magnitude \(|\overrightarrow{AB}|\) and the arrow head shows the direction. Two arrows with the same length and the same direction are the same vector, wherever they are drawn.

Basic words you must know

• Define unit vector and position vector.• When are two vectors parallel?

\(\hat a\) (with a hat) always has length \(1\). To make a unit vector, divide the vector by its own length.

5. How do I find \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) from the points \(A\) and \(B\)? (Head \(-\) Tail)
• Find the vector joining two points.• What is the Head minus Tail rule?

If \(A\) and \(B\) have position vectors \(\vec a\) and \(\vec b\), then \(\overrightarrow{AB}=\overrightarrow{OB}-\overrightarrow{OA}\).

\(\overrightarrow{AB}=\vec b-\vec a=\text{Head}-\text{Tail}\)

Worked example. For \(A(2,-1,3)\) and \(B(5,3,-2)\):

\(\overrightarrow{AB}=(5-2,\ 3-(-1),\ -2-3)=(3,4,-5)=3\hat i+4\hat j-5\hat k\)
\(|\overrightarrow{AB}|=\sqrt{9+16+25}=\sqrt{50}=5\sqrt2\)
Common mistake

Writing \(A-B\) instead of \(B-A\). The head comes first: the point where the arrow ends, minus the point where it starts.

Adding, subtracting and scaling

6. How do I add two vectors? (triangle law and parallelogram law)
• State the triangle law of addition.• What is the parallelogram law?

Triangle law (head to tail): put the tail of \(\vec b\) on the head of \(\vec a\). The arrow from the start of \(\vec a\) to the end of \(\vec b\) is \(\vec a+\vec b\). Parallelogram law: draw \(\vec a\) and \(\vec b\) from the same point; the diagonal of the parallelogram is \(\vec a+\vec b\). Polygon law: for many vectors, join them head to tail; the closing arrow is the sum.

In components just add the matching numbers: \((a_1\hat i+a_2\hat j+a_3\hat k)+(b_1\hat i+b_2\hat j+b_3\hat k)=(a_1+b_1)\hat i+(a_2+b_2)\hat j+(a_3+b_3)\hat k\).

7. Book Example 1: check that \(\vec a+\vec b=\vec b+\vec a\) for \(\vec a=2\hat i+3\hat j-5\hat k\), \(\vec b=\hat i-5\hat j+2\hat k\).
\(\vec a+\vec b=(2+1)\hat i+(3-5)\hat j+(-5+2)\hat k=3\hat i-2\hat j-3\hat k\)
\(\vec b+\vec a=(1+2)\hat i+(-5+3)\hat j+(2-5)\hat k=3\hat i-2\hat j-3\hat k\)

Both orders give the same vector, so addition is commutative.

Golden rule

Add \(\hat i\) with \(\hat i\), \(\hat j\) with \(\hat j\), and \(\hat k\) with \(\hat k\), like counting mangoes with mangoes and bananas with bananas.

8. How do I subtract vectors, and what does \(\vec a-\vec b\) look like?
• What is the negative of a vector and how do I subtract?

\(\vec a-\vec b=\vec a+(-\vec b)\): add the negative. Component by component, subtract the matching numbers. Geometrically \(\vec a-\vec b\) is the arrow from the tip of \(\vec b\) to the tip of \(\vec a\) (tick "show a − b" in the diagram above).

Worked example. \(\vec a=4\hat i+\hat j-2\hat k\), \(\vec b=\hat i-3\hat j+5\hat k\):

\(\vec a-\vec b=(4-1)\hat i+(1+3)\hat j+(-2-5)\hat k=3\hat i+4\hat j-7\hat k\)
9. Book Example 3: what is \(\lambda\vec a\) for \(\lambda=5\) and \(\vec a=10\hat i+9\hat j+17\hat k\)?
• What is scalar multiplication of a vector?

Multiplying by a scalar multiplies every component.

\(5\vec a=5(10)\hat i+5(9)\hat j+5(17)\hat k=50\hat i+45\hat j+85\hat k\)

If \(\lambda>0\) the direction stays the same; if \(\lambda<0\) it reverses; \(|\lambda\vec a|=|\lambda||\vec a|\).

Section Formula and Geometry with Vectors

How to find a point that divides a segment in a given ratio, and how to prove geometry results using vectors.

The section formula

10. How do I find the point \(P\) that divides \(\overline{AB}\) in the ratio \(AP:PB=m:n\)?
• State the section formula.• Find the position vector of the point dividing a line in the ratio m:n.

Let \(A\) and \(B\) have position vectors \(\vec a\) and \(\vec b\). For a point \(P\) between them (internal division) and for a point \(P\) outside the segment (external division):

Internal: \(\vec r=\dfrac{n\vec a+m\vec b}{m+n}\)    External: \(\vec r=\dfrac{m\vec b-n\vec a}{m-n}\)    Midpoint (\(m=n=1\)): \(\dfrac{\vec a+\vec b}{2}\)
Memory trick (cross-weights)

In \(AP:PB=m:n\), \(\vec b\) is multiplied by \(m\) (the part \(AP\)) and \(\vec a\) is multiplied by \(n\) (the part \(PB\)). So \(P\) is pulled towards the vector with the larger weight: if \(m

11. Worked example: internal and external division in space

Let \(A(2,4,-1)\) and \(B(7,-1,9)\).

Internal, \(AP:PB=2:3\): here \(m=2,\ n=3\).

\(\vec r=\dfrac{3\vec a+2\vec b}{5}=\dfrac{3(2,4,-1)+2(7,-1,9)}{5}=\dfrac{(6+14,\ 12-2,\ -3+18)}{5}=(4,2,3)\)

External, \(AP:PB=3:2\): here \(m=3,\ n=2\).

\(\vec r=\dfrac{3\vec b-2\vec a}{3-2}=3(7,-1,9)-2(2,4,-1)=(21-4,\ -3-8,\ 27+2)=(17,-11,29)\)

Check: \(\overrightarrow{AP}=(15,-15,30)\) and \(\overrightarrow{PB}=(-10,10,-20)\); their lengths are in the ratio \(3:2\).

Common mistake

Mixing up \(m\) and \(n\). Always write down which part is \(AP\) and which is \(PB\), then check the answer by finding \(\overrightarrow{AP}\) and \(\overrightarrow{PB}\).

Geometry proofs with vectors

12. How do I prove that the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other?

Take \(ABCD\) with position vectors \(\vec a,\vec b,\vec c,\vec d\). In a parallelogram \(\overrightarrow{AB}=\overrightarrow{DC}\), so \(\vec b-\vec a=\vec c-\vec d\), which gives \(\vec a+\vec c=\vec b+\vec d\).

  • The midpoint of \(AC\) is \(\dfrac{\vec a+\vec c}{2}\).
  • The midpoint of \(BD\) is \(\dfrac{\vec b+\vec d}{2}\).
  • These are equal, so the diagonals bisect each other.
Why this is useful

Fourth vertex of a parallelogram: if \(A,B,C\) are given in order, then \(\vec d=\vec a+\vec c-\vec b\).

13. How do I prove that the line joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side?

Triangle \(ABC\); \(M\) and \(N\) are the midpoints of \(AB\) and \(AC\). Then \(\vec m=\dfrac{\vec a+\vec b}{2}\) and \(\vec n=\dfrac{\vec a+\vec c}{2}\).

\(\overrightarrow{MN}=\vec n-\vec m=\dfrac{\vec c-\vec b}{2}=\tfrac12\overrightarrow{BC}\)

So \(MN\parallel BC\) and \(MN=\tfrac12 BC\).

Section 3.2

Vectors in Three-Dimensional Space

Axes, octants, components, magnitude, unit vectors and the distance formula.

Coordinates in space

14. How do I locate a point in space, and what are octants?
• What are the coordinate planes and octants?• How do I find the octant of a point?

Three mutually perpendicular axes \(Ox,\ Oy,\ Oz\) meet at the origin \(O\). The point \(P(x,y,z)\) is reached by moving \(x\) along the \(x\)-axis, then \(y\) parallel to the \(y\)-axis, then \(z\) parallel to the \(z\)-axis. The three coordinate planes (\(xy\), \(yz\), \(zx\)) cut space into eight octants, decided by the signs of \(x,y,z\).

OctantIIIIIIIVVVIVIIVIII
Signs of \(x,y,z\)+ + +− + +− − ++ − ++ + −− + −− − −+ − −
  • One coordinate \(=0\): the point lies in a coordinate plane (for example \(z=0\) is the \(xy\)-plane).
  • Two coordinates \(=0\): the point lies on an axis (for example \((0,0,z)\) is on the \(z\)-axis).

Examples. \((3,-2,5)\) has signs \((+,-,+)\): octant IV. \((0,4,-3)\) is in the \(yz\)-plane. \((0,0,-6)\) is on the \(z\)-axis.

Components, magnitude and unit vectors

15. What are \(\hat i,\hat j,\hat k\), and how do I write a vector in components?
• What are the components of a vector in space?

\(\hat i,\hat j,\hat k\) are the unit vectors along the positive \(x\)-, \(y\)- and \(z\)-axes. The position vector of \(P(x,y,z)\) is

\(\overrightarrow{OP}=\vec r=x\hat i+y\hat j+z\hat k\)

\(x,y,z\) are the components of \(\vec r\). Two vectors are equal exactly when all three components are equal.

16. How do I find the magnitude of \(\vec r=x\hat i+y\hat j+z\hat k\)?
• Find the magnitude of a vector in space.• How do I find a unit vector?

Use Pythagoras twice: first on the floor triangle, then on the upright triangle.

\(|\vec r|=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}\)

Worked example. \(\vec a=3\hat i-4\hat j+12\hat k\):

\(|\vec a|=\sqrt{9+16+144}=\sqrt{169}=13\)
\(\hat a=\dfrac{\vec a}{|\vec a|}=\dfrac{3}{13}\hat i-\dfrac{4}{13}\hat j+\dfrac{12}{13}\hat k\)
17. How do I find the distance between two points \(P\) and \(Q\)?
• State the distance formula in space.

The distance is the magnitude of \(\overrightarrow{PQ}=Q-P\):

\(PQ=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2+(z_2-z_1)^2}\)

Worked example. \(P(1,2,3)\) and \(Q(4,6,15)\):

\(\overrightarrow{PQ}=(3,4,12)\),   \(PQ=\sqrt{9+16+144}=13\)
Pythagorean quadruples

Some triples of squares add up to a perfect square: \((1,2,2)\to3\), \((2,3,6)\to7\), \((3,4,12)\to13\), \((2,6,9)\to11\). Examiners love these because the answer is a whole number.

18. How do I find a vector of given magnitude in a given direction?

A vector of magnitude \(M\) along \(\vec v\) is \(M\hat v=M\dfrac{\vec v}{|\vec v|}\). For the opposite direction use \(-M\hat v\).

Worked example. A vector of magnitude \(10\) along \(\vec v=2\hat i-\hat j+2\hat k\): \(|\vec v|=3\), so the vector is \(\dfrac{10}{3}(2,-1,2)=\left(\dfrac{20}{3},-\dfrac{10}{3},\dfrac{20}{3}\right)\).

Section 3.5

The Dot or Scalar Product

Angle, perpendicular vectors, direction cosines, projection and work done.

Definition and rules

21. What is the dot product \(\vec a\cdot\vec b\) and how do I calculate it?
• Define the scalar product of two vectors.• State the dot product in component form.

The dot product multiplies the lengths and the cosine of the angle between the vectors. The result is a number (a scalar).

\(\vec a\cdot\vec b=|\vec a||\vec b|\cos\theta=a_1b_1+a_2b_2+a_3b_3\),   \(0\le\theta\le\pi\)
RuleStatement
Commutative\(\vec a\cdot\vec b=\vec b\cdot\vec a\)
Distributive\(\vec a\cdot(\vec b+\vec c)=\vec a\cdot\vec b+\vec a\cdot\vec c\)
Square of a vector\(\vec a\cdot\vec a=|\vec a|^2\)
Unit vectors\(\hat i\cdot\hat i=\hat j\cdot\hat j=\hat k\cdot\hat k=1\);   \(\hat i\cdot\hat j=\hat j\cdot\hat k=\hat k\cdot\hat i=0\)
Scalar factor\((m\vec a)\cdot\vec b=m(\vec a\cdot\vec b)\)

Worked example. \(\vec a=2\hat i-3\hat j+\hat k\), \(\vec b=\hat i+4\hat j-2\hat k\):

\(\vec a\cdot\vec b=(2)(1)+(-3)(4)+(1)(-2)=2-12-2=-12\)
Common mistake

Writing \(\vec a\cdot\vec b\) as a vector. It is a single number. Also \(\vec a\cdot\vec b\cdot\vec c\) has no meaning, because a number cannot be dotted with a vector.

Angle between two vectors

23. How do I find the angle between two vectors?
• Find the angle between two given vectors.
\(\cos\theta=\dfrac{\vec a\cdot\vec b}{|\vec a||\vec b|}\)
  • Find the dot product.
  • Find both magnitudes.
  • Divide, then take \(\cos^{-1}\).

Book Example 11. Find the angle between \(\hat i-2\hat j+\hat k\) and \(2\hat i-3\hat j+\hat k\).

\(\vec a\cdot\vec b=(1)(2)+(-2)(-3)+(1)(1)=2+6+1=9\)
\(|\vec a|=\sqrt{1+4+1}=\sqrt6,\quad |\vec b|=\sqrt{4+9+1}=\sqrt{14}\)
\(\cos\theta=\dfrac{9}{\sqrt6\sqrt{14}}=\dfrac{9}{\sqrt{84}}\ \Rightarrow\ \theta\approx10.89^\circ\)
24. When are two vectors perpendicular, and how do I find an unknown \(\lambda\)?
• State the condition for two vectors to be orthogonal.

Perpendicular \(\Leftrightarrow\ \vec a\cdot\vec b=0\). Worked example. Find \(\lambda\) so that \(\vec a=3\hat i-2\hat j+6\hat k\) and \(\vec b=\hat i-\lambda\hat j+3\lambda\hat k\) are perpendicular.

\(\vec a\cdot\vec b=3+2\lambda+18\lambda=3+20\lambda=0\ \Rightarrow\ \lambda=-\dfrac{3}{20}\)

Direction cosines

25. What are direction cosines \(l,m,n\)?
• Define direction cosines and direction angles.• Find the third direction angle when two are given.

If \(\vec r\) makes angles \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) with the positive \(x,y,z\)-axes, then \(l=\cos\alpha,\ m=\cos\beta,\ n=\cos\gamma\). Since \(\cos\alpha=\dfrac{\vec r\cdot\hat i}{|\vec r|}\):

\(l=\dfrac{x}{|\vec r|},\ m=\dfrac{y}{|\vec r|},\ n=\dfrac{z}{|\vec r|}\),   \(l^2+m^2+n^2=1\),   \(\hat r=l\hat i+m\hat j+n\hat k\)

Book Example 10. Two direction angles of \(\vec r\) are \(30^\circ\) and \(60^\circ\). Find the third and \(\hat r\).

\(\cos^2 30^\circ+\cos^2 60^\circ+\cos^2\gamma=1\)
\(\tfrac34+\tfrac14+\cos^2\gamma=1\ \Rightarrow\ \cos\gamma=0\ \Rightarrow\ \gamma=90^\circ\)
\(\hat r=\cos30^\circ\,\hat i+\cos60^\circ\,\hat j+\cos90^\circ\,\hat k=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\hat i+\tfrac12\hat j\)
Why 90 and not 270?

A direction angle always lies between \(0^\circ\) and \(180^\circ\).

Projection and work done

26. How do I find the projection of one vector along another?
• What is the projection of a vector along another?
Projection of \(\vec b\) along \(\vec a\) \(=\dfrac{\vec a\cdot\vec b}{|\vec a|}\)   (a number)

Book Example 12. \(\vec a=\hat i-\hat j+\hat k\), \(\vec b=-\hat i+\hat j+3\hat k\). Find the projection of \(\vec a\) along \(\vec b\) and of \(\vec b\) along \(\vec a\).

\(\vec a\cdot\vec b=-1-1+3=1,\quad |\vec a|=\sqrt3,\quad |\vec b|=\sqrt{11}\)
Projection of \(\vec a\) along \(\vec b\) \(=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{11}}\)
Projection of \(\vec b\) along \(\vec a\) \(=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\)
Common mistake

Dividing by the wrong length. For the projection along \(\vec a\), divide by \(|\vec a|\), the vector you project onto.

27. What is work done by a constant force?
• Define work done by a constant force.

If a force \(\vec F\) moves a body through a displacement \(\vec S\), the work done is the dot product:

\(W=\vec F\cdot\vec S=|\vec F||\vec S|\cos\theta\)

Worked example. \(\vec F=2\hat i+3\hat j+\hat k\) N moves a body from \(P(1,0,2)\) to \(Q(4,2,3)\).

\(\vec S=\overrightarrow{PQ}=(3,2,1)\)
\(W=\vec F\cdot\vec S=6+6+1=13\) J
Units

Work is in joules (J) when force is in newtons and distance in metres.

A proof with the dot product

28. Book Example 13: prove that \(\cos(\alpha-\beta)=\cos\alpha\cos\beta+\sin\alpha\sin\beta\).
• Prove a trigonometric identity using vectors.

Take two unit vectors in the plane: \(\hat u=\cos\alpha\,\hat i+\sin\alpha\,\hat j\) and \(\hat v=\cos\beta\,\hat i+\sin\beta\,\hat j\). The angle between them is \(\alpha-\beta\).

By the definition: \(\hat u\cdot\hat v=|\hat u||\hat v|\cos(\alpha-\beta)=\cos(\alpha-\beta)\)
By components: \(\hat u\cdot\hat v=\cos\alpha\cos\beta+\sin\alpha\sin\beta\)

Equating the two values proves the result.

Section 3.6

The Cross or Vector Product

A new arrow perpendicular to both: right-hand rule, determinant, area and moment.

Definition and the right-hand rule

29. What is the cross product \(\vec a\times\vec b\)?
• Define the vector product of two vectors.• State the right-hand rule.

The cross product of two vectors is a vector perpendicular to both of them:

\(\vec a\times\vec b=|\vec a||\vec b|\sin\theta\ \hat n\)

Here \(\hat n\) is the unit vector perpendicular to \(\vec a\) and \(\vec b\) given by the right-hand rule: curl the fingers of your right hand from \(\vec a\) towards \(\vec b\) (through the smaller angle); your thumb points along \(\vec a\times\vec b\).

RuleStatement
Anti-commutative\(\vec b\times\vec a=-\,\vec a\times\vec b\)
Distributive\(\vec a\times(\vec b+\vec c)=\vec a\times\vec b+\vec a\times\vec c\)
Parallel vectors\(\vec a\times\vec b=\vec 0\Leftrightarrow\vec a\parallel\vec b\), so \(\vec a\times\vec a=\vec 0\)
Unit vectors\(\hat i\times\hat j=\hat k,\ \hat j\times\hat k=\hat i,\ \hat k\times\hat i=\hat j\); reversing the order gives a minus sign
30. How do I calculate \(\vec a\times\vec b\) from components?
• Find the cross product of two vectors in component form.

Use a determinant with \(\hat i,\hat j,\hat k\) in the first row:

\(\vec a\times\vec b=\begin{vmatrix}\hat i&\hat j&\hat k\\a_1&a_2&a_3\\b_1&b_2&b_3\end{vmatrix}=\hat i(a_2b_3-a_3b_2)-\hat j(a_1b_3-a_3b_1)+\hat k(a_1b_2-a_2b_1)\)
Common mistake

Forgetting the minus sign in front of the \(\hat j\) term. The signs go \(+,\,-,\,+\).

Uses of the cross product

31. How do I find the area of a parallelogram and of a triangle?
• Find the area of a parallelogram from adjacent sides.• Find the area of a triangle from its vertices.
Parallelogram \(=|\vec a\times\vec b|\)    Triangle \(=\tfrac12|\vec a\times\vec b|\)

Book Example 16. \(\vec a=2\hat i+5\hat j-2\hat k\) and \(\vec b=\hat i-\hat j+3\hat k\) are adjacent sides of a parallelogram. Find its area.

\(\vec a\times\vec b=\begin{vmatrix}\hat i&\hat j&\hat k\\2&5&-2\\1&-1&3\end{vmatrix}=(15-2)\hat i-(6+2)\hat j+(-2-5)\hat k=13\hat i-8\hat j-7\hat k\)
Area \(=|\vec a\times\vec b|=\sqrt{169+64+49}=\sqrt{282}\) sq. units

Book Example 17. Find the area of the triangle with vertices \(A(0,0)\), \(B(2,9)\), \(C(3,5)\). Take \(\vec a=\overrightarrow{AC}=(3,5,0)\) and \(\vec b=\overrightarrow{AB}=(2,9,0)\).

\(\vec a\times\vec b=\begin{vmatrix}\hat i&\hat j&\hat k\\3&5&0\\2&9&0\end{vmatrix}=(27-10)\hat k=17\hat k\)
Area \(=\tfrac12|\vec a\times\vec b|=\dfrac{17}{2}\) sq. units
32. Book Example 18: find the angle between \(\vec a+\vec b\) and \(\vec a+\vec c\), where \(\vec a=2\hat i-\hat j+\hat k\), \(\vec b=-\hat i+2\hat j-\hat k\), \(\vec c=\hat i+\hat j-3\hat k\).
• Find the angle between two vectors using the cross product.
\(\vec a+\vec b=\hat i+\hat j\),   \(\vec a+\vec c=3\hat i-2\hat k\)
\((\vec a+\vec b)\times(\vec a+\vec c)=\begin{vmatrix}\hat i&\hat j&\hat k\\1&1&0\\3&0&-2\end{vmatrix}=-2\hat i+2\hat j-3\hat k\), magnitude \(\sqrt{4+4+9}=\sqrt{17}\)
\(|\vec a+\vec b|=\sqrt2,\quad |\vec a+\vec c|=\sqrt{13}\)
\(\sin\theta=\dfrac{\sqrt{17}}{\sqrt2\sqrt{13}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{17}{26}}\ \Rightarrow\ \theta\approx53.96^\circ\)

Check with the dot product: \((\vec a+\vec b)\cdot(\vec a+\vec c)=3\), so \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{26}}\), the same angle.

33. How do I find a unit vector perpendicular to two vectors?
• Find a unit vector perpendicular to two given vectors.

Compute \(\vec a\times\vec b\) and divide by its length: \(\hat n=\pm\dfrac{\vec a\times\vec b}{|\vec a\times\vec b|}\). Use \(\pm\) because both directions are perpendicular. For a vector of length \(M\), multiply \(\hat n\) by \(M\).

Worked example. \(\vec a=\hat i+\hat j\), \(\vec b=\hat j+\hat k\).

\(\vec a\times\vec b=\begin{vmatrix}\hat i&\hat j&\hat k\\1&1&0\\0&1&1\end{vmatrix}=\hat i-\hat j+\hat k\), length \(\sqrt3\)
\(\hat n=\pm\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(\hat i-\hat j+\hat k)\)
34. Book Example 19: show that \(\sin(\alpha-\beta)=\sin\alpha\cos\beta-\cos\alpha\sin\beta\).
• Prove a trigonometric identity using the cross product.

Let \(\hat u=\cos\alpha\,\hat i+\sin\alpha\,\hat j\) and \(\hat v=\cos\beta\,\hat i+\sin\beta\,\hat j\). The angle between them is \(\alpha-\beta\).

\(\hat v\times\hat u=\begin{vmatrix}\hat i&\hat j&\hat k\\\cos\beta&\sin\beta&0\\\cos\alpha&\sin\alpha&0\end{vmatrix}=(\sin\alpha\cos\beta-\cos\alpha\sin\beta)\,\hat k\)
so \(|\hat v\times\hat u|=\sin\alpha\cos\beta-\cos\alpha\sin\beta\)   (1)
Also \(|\hat v\times\hat u|=|\hat v||\hat u|\sin(\alpha-\beta)=\sin(\alpha-\beta)\)   (2)

From (1) and (2) the identity follows.

Moment (torque) of a force

35. What is the moment of a force, and how do I find it?
• Define the moment of a force.• Find the moment of a force about a point.

A force \(\vec F\) applied at a point \(P\) tries to turn a body about a point \(O\). The moment is:

\(\vec M=\vec r\times\vec F,\quad \vec r=\overrightarrow{OP}\),   \(|\vec M|=rF\sin\theta\)

Book Example 20. Find the moment of \(\vec F=3\hat i-2\hat j+5\hat k\) about the point \((2,1,-1)\) when it is applied at \((3,0,2)\).

\(\vec r=(3-2,\ 0-1,\ 2+1)=\hat i-\hat j+3\hat k\)
\(\vec M=\vec r\times\vec F=\begin{vmatrix}\hat i&\hat j&\hat k\\1&-1&3\\3&-2&5\end{vmatrix}=(-5+6)\hat i-(5-9)\hat j+(-2+3)\hat k=\hat i+4\hat j+\hat k\)

Book Example 21. A force \(\vec F=7\hat i+4\hat j+2\hat k\) acts at the door handle \((2,1,4)\); the hinge is at \((0,0,1)\).

\(\vec r=(2,1,3)\)
\(\vec M=\begin{vmatrix}\hat i&\hat j&\hat k\\2&1&3\\7&4&2\end{vmatrix}=(2-12)\hat i-(4-21)\hat j+(8-7)\hat k=-10\hat i+17\hat j+\hat k\)
Which point is the tail?

\(\vec r\) goes from the point you turn about to the point where the force acts: \(\vec r=\) (point of force) \(-\) (pivot).

Section 3.7

The Scalar Triple Product

A determinant that measures volume and tests for coplanarity.

Definition and determinant form

36. What is the scalar triple product \(\vec a\cdot\vec b\times\vec c\)?
• Define the scalar triple product.• Write the scalar triple product as a determinant.

Take the cross product \(\vec b\times\vec c\) first (a vector), then dot it with \(\vec a\). The answer is a number.

\(\vec a\cdot\vec b\times\vec c=\begin{vmatrix}a_1&a_2&a_3\\b_1&b_2&b_3\\c_1&c_2&c_3\end{vmatrix}\)
PropertyStatement
Dot and cross can swap\(\vec a\cdot(\vec b\times\vec c)=(\vec a\times\vec b)\cdot\vec c\)
Cyclic order\(\vec a\cdot\vec b\times\vec c=\vec b\cdot\vec c\times\vec a=\vec c\cdot\vec a\times\vec b\)
Swapping two vectorschanges the sign of the answer
Unit vectors\(\hat i\cdot\hat j\times\hat k=1\)
Repeated or parallel vectorgives zero
Common mistake

Writing \((\vec a\cdot\vec b)\times\vec c\). The dot product of the first two is a number, and a number cannot be crossed with a vector, so the brackets must go around \(\vec b\times\vec c\).

37. Book Example 23: find \(\vec a\cdot\vec b\times\vec c\) for \(\vec a=2\hat i-3\hat j+\hat k\), \(\vec b=-3\hat i+2\hat j+3\hat k\), \(\vec c=-\hat i+\hat j-\hat k\).
• Find the scalar triple product of three given vectors.
\(\vec a\cdot\vec b\times\vec c=\begin{vmatrix}2&-3&1\\-3&2&3\\-1&1&-1\end{vmatrix}\)
\(=2(-2-3)-(-3)(3+3)+1(-3+2)\)
\(=-10+18-1=7\)
Expanding a 3 by 3 determinant

Go along the first row with signs \(+,-,+\). Each number multiplies the \(2\times2\) determinant left after deleting its row and column. When the middle number is negative, \(-(-3)\) becomes \(+3\).

38. Book Example 24: if \(\vec b\parallel\vec c\), what is \(\vec a\cdot\vec b\times\vec c\)?
• What is the scalar triple product when two vectors are parallel?

Since \(\vec b\parallel\vec c\), write \(\vec b=\lambda\vec c\). Then row 2 of the determinant is \(\lambda\) times row 3:

\(\vec a\cdot\vec b\times\vec c=\begin{vmatrix}a_1&a_2&a_3\\\lambda c_1&\lambda c_2&\lambda c_3\\c_1&c_2&c_3\end{vmatrix}=\lambda\begin{vmatrix}a_1&a_2&a_3\\c_1&c_2&c_3\\c_1&c_2&c_3\end{vmatrix}=\lambda(0)=0\)

Two identical rows make a determinant zero. Directly: \(\vec b\times\vec c=\vec 0\), so \(\vec a\cdot\vec 0=0\).

Volume of a parallelepiped and a tetrahedron

39. How do I find the volume of a parallelepiped and of a tetrahedron?
• Find the volume of a parallelepiped from three edges.• Find the volume of a tetrahedron from its vertices.

The base area is \(|\vec b\times\vec c|\) and the height is the part of \(\vec a\) perpendicular to the base. So:

Parallelepiped \(=|\vec a\cdot\vec b\times\vec c|\)    Tetrahedron \(=\dfrac16|\vec a\cdot\vec b\times\vec c|\)

Book Example 25. Find the volume of the parallelepiped with adjacent sides \(\vec a=\hat i-2\hat j+\hat k\), \(\vec b=3\hat i-3\hat j+\hat k\), \(\vec c=-\hat i-\hat j+2\hat k\).

\(\vec a\cdot\vec b\times\vec c=\begin{vmatrix}1&-2&1\\3&-3&1\\-1&-1&2\end{vmatrix}\)
\(=1(-6+1)+2(6+1)+1(-3-3)=-5+14-6=3\)
Volume \(=3\) cubic units

Book Example 26. Find the volume of the tetrahedron with vertices \(A(0,0,0)\), \(B(1,3,-1)\), \(C(2,2,1)\), \(D(1,6,5)\).

\(\vec a=\overrightarrow{AB}=(1,3,-1),\ \ \vec b=\overrightarrow{AC}=(2,2,1),\ \ \vec c=\overrightarrow{AD}=(1,6,5)\)
\(\vec a\cdot\vec b\times\vec c=\begin{vmatrix}1&3&-1\\2&2&1\\1&6&5\end{vmatrix}=1(10-6)-3(10-1)-1(12-2)=4-27-10=-33\)
Volume \(=\dfrac16|-33|=\dfrac{11}{2}\) cubic units
Volume is never negative

A negative determinant only means the vectors are in left-handed order. Take the absolute value.

Coplanar vectors

40. How do I test whether three vectors are coplanar?
• State the condition for three vectors to be coplanar.

Three vectors lying in one plane are coplanar. Then \(\vec a\times\vec b\) is perpendicular to the plane, so it is also perpendicular to \(\vec c\):

\(\vec a,\vec b,\vec c\) coplanar \(\Longleftrightarrow\ \vec a\cdot\vec b\times\vec c=0\)

Worked example. Find \(\lambda\) so that \(\vec a=\hat i+2\hat j-\hat k\), \(\vec b=2\hat i+\lambda\hat j+\hat k\), \(\vec c=3\hat i+5\hat j\) are coplanar.

\(\begin{vmatrix}1&2&-1\\2&\lambda&1\\3&5&0\end{vmatrix}=1(0-5)-2(0-3)+(-1)(10-3\lambda)\)
\(=-5+6-10+3\lambda=3\lambda-9=0\ \Rightarrow\ \lambda=3\)

For four points \(A,B,C,D\), test \(\overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{AC}\times\overrightarrow{AD}=0\).

Quick check

If two of the three vectors are parallel, or one is the zero vector, the three are always coplanar.

Vectors in the real world

41. Where are vectors used outside the classroom?
• Give two applications of vectors.

Wherever a quantity needs both a size and a direction: air-traffic controllers tracking planes, meteorologists describing wind, programmers building virtual worlds, and in physics for work, torque and magnetic force.

ApplicationVector idea
Work done by a forcedot product \(\vec F\cdot\vec S\)
Torque (turning effect)cross product \(\vec r\times\vec F\)
Projectile motionsplitting the velocity into components
Sharpening a pencilforce split into parts perpendicular and parallel to the pencil
Earth's magnetic fielda vertical part \(B\) and a horizontal part \(H\)
42. How does adding vectors help a pilot who flies in wind?
• How do vectors help in finding the ground velocity of a plane?

The velocity of the plane over the ground is the vector sum of its velocity through the air and the velocity of the wind.

Worked example. A plane flies east at \(400\) km/h through the air while a wind blows north at \(100\) km/h.

\(\vec v_{\text{ground}}=400\hat i+100\hat j\)
Speed \(=\sqrt{400^2+100^2}=100\sqrt{17}\approx412.31\) km/h
Direction: \(\tan\phi=\dfrac{100}{400}\ \Rightarrow\ \phi\approx14.04^\circ\) north of east
43. How do components describe projectile motion?
• Write the components of the velocity of a projectile.

A stone thrown with speed \(u\) at angle \(\phi\) to the horizontal has the components:

\(v_x=u\cos\phi\) (constant),    \(v_y=u\sin\phi-gt\)

Worked example. \(u=20\) m/s, \(\phi=30^\circ\), \(t=1\) s, \(g=9.8\) m/s\(^2\).

\(v_x=20\cos30^\circ=17.32\) m/s
\(v_y=20\sin30^\circ-9.8(1)=10-9.8=0.2\) m/s
Speed \(=\sqrt{17.32^2+0.2^2}\approx17.32\) m/s

Question:

Show that \(\vec a=-4\hat i-6\hat j-54\hat k\), \(\vec b=-\hat i+4\hat j+3\hat k\) and \(\vec c=-\hat i+2\hat j-3\hat k\) are coplanar.

\(\vec a\cdot\vec b\times\vec c=\begin{vmatrix}-4&-6&-54\\-1&4&3\\-1&2&-3\end{vmatrix}\)
\(=-4(-12-6)+6(3+3)-54(-2+4)=72+36-108=0\)

Answer: The triple product is \(0\), so the vectors are coplanar.

Question:

Find the volume of the tetrahedron with vertices \(A(0,1,0)\), \(B(2,0,1)\), \(C(3,1,2)\), \(D(5,6,-1)\).

\(\overrightarrow{AB}=(2,-1,1)\),   \(\overrightarrow{AC}=(3,0,2)\),   \(\overrightarrow{AD}=(5,5,-1)\)
\(\begin{vmatrix}2&-1&1\\3&0&2\\5&5&-1\end{vmatrix}=2(0-10)+1(-3-10)+1(15-0)=-20-13+15=-18\)
\(V=\tfrac16|-18|\)

Answer: \(3\) cubic units

Find \(\lambda\) so that \(\vec a=3\hat i-2\hat j+6\hat k\) and \(\vec b=\hat i-\lambda\hat j+3\lambda\hat k\) are (i) parallel, (ii) perpendicular.

If \(\vec a=-3\hat i+2\hat j+4\hat k\) and \(\vec b=\hat i-2\hat j+4\hat k\), find the component of \(\vec a+\vec b\) along \(\vec a-\vec b\).
