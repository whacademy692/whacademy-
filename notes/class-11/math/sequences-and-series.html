1. What is the one idea that holds Unit 4 together?
• How should I study sequences and series?• What is this unit really testing?

Everything in this unit is a list of numbers that follows a rule (a sequence) and the total of that list (a series). There are only three rules to remember:

  • multiply by the same number each time: G.P. (geometric);

Everything else (means, sigma, A.G.S., word problems) is a tool built on those three. The Sequence Gallery in Section 4.1 and the Sequence → Series picture in Section 4.3 show all three side by side.

Sequence and its Types

Terms, the general term and how to recognise A.P., G.P. and H.P.

A sequence is the ordered list \(a_1,a_2,a_3,\dots\) and a series is the indicated sum \(a_1+a_2+a_3+\cdots\). Start with the gallery below: pick A.P., G.P., H.P. or a general rule, then move the sliders and watch the boxes and the dots.

4. What is a sequence?
• Define a sequence.• What is the general term of a sequence?

A sequence is a list of numbers written in a definite order, each one following from a rule. We write it \(a_1,a_2,a_3,\dots\) where \(a_1\) is the first term, \(a_2\) the second, and \(a_n\) the \(n\)th term. In other words a sequence is a function of the position \(n=1,2,3,\dots\)

\(a_n=f(n),\qquad n=1,2,3,\dots\)
\(2,4,6,8,\dots\)   has rule   \(a_n=2n\)
\(1,4,9,16,\dots\)   has rule   \(a_n=n^2\)
In simple words

A sequence is a row of boxes numbered 1, 2, 3… and the rule tells you what goes into box number \(n\).

5. What is the difference between a finite and an infinite sequence?
• What does the ellipsis in a sequence mean?

A finite sequence stops: it has a last term, for example \(3,6,9,12\) (4 terms). An infinite sequence goes on for ever, for example \(3,6,9,12,\dots\) The three dots (an ellipsis) mean “and so on by the same rule”.

6. How do I find terms when the \(n\)th term formula is given?
• Find the first four terms of a given general term.

Substitute \(n=1,2,3,\dots\) in turn. For \(a_n=n^2+1\):

\(a_1=1^2+1=2\),   \(a_2=5\),   \(a_3=10\),   \(a_4=17\)
\(a_{10}=10^2+1=101\),   \(a_{15}=15^2+1=226\)
Mistake: using \(n=0\) as the first term. In this unit the first term is always \(a_1\).

9. How do I recognise the type of a sequence in 10 seconds?
• How can I identify a sequence as arithmetic or geometric?

Run three quick tests on the first few terms:

  1. Subtract neighbours. All the same? It is an A.P.
  2. Divide neighbours. All the same? It is a G.P.
  3. Flip every term and subtract. All the same? It is an H.P.
  4. None of the three? It is a general sequence (use the rule or the differences).

Use the Pattern Detective above: it generates a new puzzle every time.

Section 4.2

Arithmetic Sequence (A.P.) and A.M.

Common difference, the \(n\)th term and arithmetic means.

An arithmetic progression goes up (or down) by the same step every time. Its terms lie on a straight line, and that is why a few simple formulas solve nearly every A.P. question.

10. What is an arithmetic progression (A.P.)?
• Define an arithmetic sequence.• What is the common difference?

A sequence in which each term is obtained from the previous one by adding the same fixed number \(d\). That fixed number is the common difference.

\(d=a_{n+1}-a_n\quad\text{(the same for every }n\text{)}\)
  • \(d>0\): the terms go up (increasing A.P.).
  • \(d<0\): the terms go down (decreasing A.P.).
  • \(d=0\): every term is the same (a constant A.P.).
\(5,8,11,14,\dots\):   \(d=3\)
\(20,17,14,\dots\):   \(d=-3\)
11. How do I prove that a sequence is an A.P.?
• Show that a given sequence is arithmetic.

Show that \(a_{n+1}-a_n\) is a constant that does not depend on \(n\).

\(a_n=3n+2\):   \(a_{n+1}-a_n=[3(n+1)+2]-[3n+2]=3\)
The difference is always \(3\), so it is an A.P. with \(d=3\) and \(a_1=5\).
A formula that is linear in \(n\) (\(a_n=dn+c\)) always gives an A.P., and the coefficient of \(n\) is \(d\).
12. What is the formula for the \(n\)th term, and why is it \(n-1\)?
• Derive the nth term of an A.P.• Why is it n minus 1?

To go from the 1st term to the \(n\)th term you take \(n-1\) steps of size \(d\) (see the staircase picture).

\(a_n=a_1+(n-1)d\)

Interactive diagram (needs JavaScript).

Mistake: writing \(a_n=a_1+nd\). For \(n=1\) it would give \(a_2\), not \(a_1\).
13. Find the 20th term of \(5,8,11,\dots\) and say which term equals \(98\).
• Which term of the A.P. is 98?• Find a given term of an A.P.
\(a=5,\ d=3\)
\(a_{20}=5+19(3)=5+57=\mathbf{62}\)
\(5+(n-1)3=98\Rightarrow(n-1)3=93\Rightarrow n-1=31\Rightarrow\mathbf{n=32}\)
When you solve for \(n\) it must be a positive whole number. If you get a fraction, the number is not in the sequence.
14. Two terms are given. How do I find the sequence?
• Find the A.P. if two terms are given.

Subtract the two terms: the difference between term \(p\) and term \(q\) is \((q-p)d\).

\(d=\frac{a_q-a_p}{q-p}\)
\(a_3=7,\ a_8=22\):   \(d=\dfrac{22-7}{8-3}=3\)
\(a_1=a_3-2d=7-6=1\)
So the A.P. is \(1,4,7,10,\dots\) and \(a_{20}=1+19(3)=58\)
Trick:You do not need simultaneous equations: one subtraction gives \(d\), then step back to \(a_1\).
15. What is the arithmetic mean (A.M.)?
• Define arithmetic mean.• Find the A.M. between two numbers.

If \(a,A,b\) are in A.P. then \(A\) is the arithmetic mean of \(a\) and \(b\). Because \(A-a=b-A\):

\(A=\frac{a+b}{2}\)
A.M. between \(5\) and \(23\):   \(\dfrac{5+23}{2}=\mathbf{14}\)
16. How do I insert \(n\) arithmetic means between two numbers?
• Insert n A.M.s between a and b.

If you insert \(n\) means between \(a\) and \(b\) you create \(n+2\) terms and \(n+1\) equal gaps.

\(d=\frac{b-a}{n+1},\qquad A_k=a+kd\ (k=1,\dots,n)\)
Three A.M.s between \(4\) and \(24\):   \(d=\dfrac{24-4}{4}=5\)
Sequence: \(4,\ \mathbf9,\ \mathbf{14},\ \mathbf{19},\ 24\)

Most common mistake: dividing by \(n\) instead of \(n+1\). Count the gaps, not the means.

• Three numbers in A.P. have sum S and product P.

18. What are the properties of an A.P. worth remembering?
• Properties of arithmetic progression.
  • If \(a,b,c\) are in A.P. then \(2b=a+c\) (the middle is the A.M. of the other two).
  • Terms equally far from the two ends add to the same total: \(a_1+a_n=a_2+a_{n-1}=\cdots\)
  • Adding the same number to every term, or multiplying every term by a non-zero number, gives another A.P.
  • The number of terms from \(a\) to \(l\) is \(n=\dfrac{l-a}{d}+1\) (do not forget the +1).

19. What is an arithmetic series and how is \(S_n\) written?
• Define arithmetic series.• What is the sum of an A.P. called?

An arithmetic series is the sum of the terms of an A.P. The sum of the first \(n\) terms is \(S_n=a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_n\). For example, for \(3,6,9,12\): \(S_4=3+6+9+12=30\).

20. What are the two formulas for \(S_n\), and when do I use which?
• Sum of n terms of an A.P.
\(S_n=\frac n2\,(a_1+a_n)\qquad\text{and}\qquad S_n=\frac n2\,\big[2a_1+(n-1)d\big]\)
You knowUse
\(a_1,\ a_n,\ n\)\(S_n=\dfrac n2(a_1+a_n)\)
\(a_1,\ d,\ n\)\(S_n=\dfrac n2[2a_1+(n-1)d]\)
\(a_1,\ a_n,\ d\) (no \(n\))first find \(n=\dfrac{a_n-a_1}{d}+1\)
\(a_1,\ a_n,\ S_n\)\(n=\dfrac{2S_n}{a_1+a_n}\)
Mean-value picture:\(S_n=n\times\) (average of the first and last term). For an odd number of terms that average is the middle term, so \(S_n=n\times a_{\text{middle}}\).
21. Why is \(S_n=\frac n2(a_1+a_n)\)? (Gauss\' trick)
• Derive the formula for the sum of an arithmetic series.• Prove S_n = n/2 (a + l).

Write the sum forwards and backwards and add. Every pair of terms (first and last, second and second-last, …) adds to the same number \(a_1+a_n\), and there are \(n\) pairs:

\(2S_n=n(a_1+a_n)\ \Rightarrow\ S_n=\dfrac n2(a_1+a_n)\)

24. How do I find the \(n\)th term when only \(S_n\) is given?
• Find a_n from S_n.

Use the difference of two consecutive sums: \(a_n=S_n-S_{n-1}\) for \(n\ge2\), and \(a_1=S_1\).

\(S_n=3n^2+2n\):   \(a_1=S_1=5\)
\(a_n=(3n^2+2n)-\big[3(n-1)^2+2(n-1)\big]=6n-1\ (n\ge2)\), which also gives \(5\) for \(n=1\)
So \(a_n=6n-1\): an A.P. with \(a_1=5,\ d=6\)
If \(S_n=An^2+Bn\) then \(d=2A\) and \(a_1=A+B\). Always check that your \(a_n\) also fits \(n=1\).
25. How many terms must I take to get a given sum?
• Find n if the sum is given.
A.P. \(2,5,8,\dots\) with \(S_n=187\):
\(\dfrac n2[4+3(n-1)]=187\Rightarrow n(3n+1)=374\Rightarrow3n^2+n-374=0\)
Discriminant \(=1+4488=4489=67^2\), so \(n=\dfrac{-1+67}{6}=\mathbf{11}\)
Check: \(S_{11}=\dfrac{11}{2}(4+30)=187\ \checkmark\)
Reject a negative or fractional \(n\). If \(d<0\) both roots of the quadratic can be valid (the terms go positive, then negative and cancel).
26. How do I solve a word problem on arithmetic series?
• Application of arithmetic series.

Identify the pattern: “each row has 2 more seats” means an A.P. with \(d=2\).

A theatre has \(20\) seats in the first row and \(2\) more in each next row. Seats in \(15\) rows?
\(a_{15}=20+14(2)=48\);   \(S_{15}=\dfrac{15}{2}(20+48)=15\times34=\mathbf{510}\)
  • Write the model first: “A.P., \(a=20,\ d=2,\ n=15\)” (method marks).
  • Decide: is the question asking for one term or the total?
  • Check the answer is sensible (510 seats for 15 rows of 20 to 48 seats).
Sections 4.4 and 4.5

Geometric Sequence (G.P.) and G.M.

Common ratio, the \(n\)th term and geometric means.

A geometric progression multiplies by the same ratio each time. Its terms grow or shrink by a constant percentage, which is why it describes compound interest, populations and bouncing balls.

27. What is a geometric progression (G.P.)?
• Define a geometric sequence.• What is the common ratio?

A sequence in which every term is obtained from the previous one by multiplying by the same non-zero number \(r\), the common ratio.

\(r=\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\quad(\text{the same for every }n)\)
\(3,6,12,24,\dots\):   \(r=2\)
\(81,27,9,3,\dots\):   \(r=\tfrac13\)
\(2,-6,18,-54,\dots\):   \(r=-3\)

30. What is the \(n\)th term of a G.P.? Find the 8th term of \(3,6,12,\dots\)
• Derive the nth term of a G.P.
\(a_n=a_1\,r^{\,n-1}\)
\(a_1=3,\ r=2\):   \(a_8=3\cdot2^7=3\times128=\mathbf{384}\)
The exponent is \(n-1\), not \(n\). Keep brackets for a negative ratio: \((-3)^5=-243\), not \(3^5\).
31. Two terms of a G.P. are given. How do I find \(r\) and \(a_1\)?
• Find the G.P. if two terms are given.

Divide the two terms: \(\dfrac{a_q}{a_p}=r^{\,q-p}\). Then take the root.

\(a_3=12,\ a_6=96\):   \(r^3=\dfrac{96}{12}=8\Rightarrow r=2\)
\(a_1=\dfrac{a_3}{r^2}=\dfrac{12}{4}=3\), so the G.P. is \(3,6,12,24,\dots\)
If \(q-p\) is even there may be two real ratios \(\pm\); both can be valid unless the question rules one out.
32. What is the geometric mean (G.M.)?
• Define geometric mean.

If \(a,G,b\) are in G.P. then \(\dfrac Ga=\dfrac bG\), so \(G^2=ab\):

\(G=\pm\sqrt{ab}\)
G.M. between \(4\) and \(9\):   \(\pm\sqrt{36}=\pm6\) (the positive mean \(6\) is usually used)
If \(a\) and \(b\) have opposite signs, \(ab<0\) and there is no real G.M.
33. How do I insert \(n\) geometric means between \(a\) and \(b\)?
• Insert n G.M.s between a and b.

Again \(n+1\) steps, but multiplying: \(b=ar^{\,n+1}\).

\(r=\left(\frac ba\right)^{\frac1{n+1}},\qquad G_k=ar^k\)
Two G.M.s between \(3\) and \(81\):   \(r^3=27\Rightarrow r=3\)
Sequence: \(3,\ \mathbf9,\ \mathbf{27},\ 81\)

35. What are the properties of a G.P.?
• Properties of geometric progression.
  • If \(a,b,c\) are in G.P. then \(b^2=ac\).
  • Terms equally far from the ends have the same product: \(a_1a_n=a_2a_{n-1}=\cdots\)
  • Multiplying every term by a non-zero constant gives another G.P.
  • The logarithms of the terms of a G.P. form an A.P. (that is why the G.P. is a straight line on a log scale).
Section 4.6

Geometric Series and Recurring Decimals

Finite sum, infinite sum and where they are used.

A geometric series adds the terms of a G.P. The same \(r\) that built the sequence now decides whether the total settles down (when \(|r|<1\)) or runs away.

36. What is the sum of the first \(n\) terms of a G.P.?
• Sum of n terms of a G.P.• Which formula for S_n when r is greater than 1?
\(S_n=\frac{a_1(1-r^n)}{1-r}=\frac{a_1(r^n-1)}{r-1}\quad(r\ne1)\)
\(r<1\): use \(\dfrac{a_1(1-r^n)}{1-r}\)   (denominator positive)
\(r>1\): use \(\dfrac{a_1(r^n-1)}{r-1}\)   (denominator positive)

If the last term \(l\) is known instead of \(n\): \(S_n=\dfrac{a_1-lr}{1-r}\). If \(r=1\) every term equals \(a_1\) and \(S_n=na_1\).

37. Why is \(S_n=\dfrac{a(1-r^n)}{1-r}\)? (multiply by \(r\) and subtract)
• Derive the sum of a geometric series.
\(S_n=a+ar+ar^2+\cdots+ar^{n-1}\)
\(rS_n=\ \ \ \ \ ar+ar^2+\cdots+ar^{n-1}+ar^n\)
Subtract:   \((1-r)S_n=a-ar^n\ \Rightarrow\ S_n=\dfrac{a(1-r^n)}{1-r}\)
In simple words

Shift the sum by one place and subtract: all the middle terms cancel, leaving only the first and one extra last term.

38. Find \(3+6+12+\cdots\) (8 terms) and \(81+27+9+\cdots\) (6 terms).
• Find the sum of the geometric series.
\(a=3,\ r=2,\ n=8\):   \(S_8=\dfrac{3(2^8-1)}{2-1}=3\times255=\mathbf{765}\)
\(a=81,\ r=\tfrac13,\ n=6\):   \(S_6=\dfrac{81\left(1-\frac1{729}\right)}{1-\frac13}=\dfrac{81\cdot\frac{728}{729}}{\frac23}=\dfrac{364}{3}\approx\mathbf{121.33}\)

39. When does an infinite geometric series have a sum?
• Define the sum to infinity.• When does S infinity exist?

Only when \(|r|<1\). Then \(r^n\to0\) as \(n\) grows, so the extra term in \(S_n\) disappears:

\(S_\infty=\frac{a_1}{1-r},\qquad |r|<1\)

Interactive diagram (needs JavaScript).

If \(|r|\ge1\) the terms do not shrink, the totals keep growing or swinging, and there is no sum to infinity. Always state the condition \(|r|<1\).

Section 4.7

Harmonic Sequence (H.P.) and H.M.

Reciprocals of an A.P.

The word harmonic comes from music: the lengths of strings giving harmonics are \(1,\tfrac12,\tfrac13,\dots\) of a base length. An H.P. has no formula of its own; it always works through the A.P. of reciprocals.

44. What is a harmonic progression (H.P.)?
• Define harmonic progression.• What is the relation between A.P. and H.P.?

A sequence is an H.P. when the reciprocals of its terms form an A.P.

\(\tfrac12,\tfrac15,\tfrac18,\dots\) is an H.P. because \(2,5,8,\dots\) is an A.P. (\(d=3\))
\(1,\tfrac12,\tfrac13,\tfrac14,\dots\) is the classic H.P. (reciprocals \(1,2,3,4,\dots\))

Interactive diagram (needs JavaScript).

No term of an H.P. can be \(0\) (its reciprocal would not exist).
45. What is the \(n\)th term of an H.P.?
• Find the nth term of an H.P.

Turn it into an A.P., use the A.P. formula, then flip back:

\(a_n=\dfrac{1}{A+(n-1)d},\qquad A=\dfrac1{a_1},\quad d=\dfrac1{a_2}-\dfrac1{a_1}\)
\(\tfrac12,\tfrac15,\tfrac18,\dots\): reciprocals \(2,5,8\), \(d=3\)
\(10\)th reciprocal \(=2+9(3)=29\), so \(a_{10}=\dfrac1{29}\)
46. What is the harmonic mean (H.M.)?
• Define harmonic mean.

If \(a,H,b\) are in H.P., then \(\tfrac1a,\tfrac1H,\tfrac1b\) are in A.P., so \(\dfrac2H=\dfrac1a+\dfrac1b\):

\(H=\frac{2ab}{a+b}\)
H.M. of \(4\) and \(12\):   \(\dfrac{2(4)(12)}{16}=\mathbf6\)
Speed trick:For a round trip at speeds \(a\) and \(b\), the average speed is the harmonic mean, not the A.M. (\(40\) and \(60\) km/h gives \(48\), not \(50\)).
47. How do I insert \(n\) harmonic means between \(a\) and \(b\)?
• Insert harmonic means.

Invert, insert A.M.s, invert back.

Two H.M.s between \(\tfrac13\) and \(\tfrac1{12}\): reciprocals \(3\) and \(12\)
A.P. with \(d=\dfrac{12-3}{3}=3\):   \(3,6,9,12\)
H.P.: \(\tfrac13,\ \mathbf{\tfrac16},\ \mathbf{\tfrac19},\ \tfrac1{12}\)
48. How are A.M., G.M. and H.M. related?
• Relation between A.M., G.M. and H.M.• Prove G squared equals A times H.
\(A=\frac{a+b}2,\qquad G=\sqrt{ab},\qquad H=\frac{2ab}{a+b}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad G^2=AH,\quad A\ge G\ge H\)

Interactive diagram (needs JavaScript).

\(a=4,\ b=12\):   \(A=8,\ G=\sqrt{48}\approx6.93,\ H=6\)
Check: \(AH=48=G^2\ \checkmark\) and \(8\ge6.93\ge6\ \checkmark\)
Equality \(A=G=H\) happens only when \(a=b\). The inequality needs positive numbers.

Section 4.9

Arithmetico-Geometric Series

A.P. times G.P.

An A.G.S. mixes the two main sequences. It is less common in the exercise, but the multiply-and-subtract idea is worth practising.

56. What is an arithmetico-geometric series (A.G.S.)?
• Define arithmetico-geometric series.

Each term is an A.P. term times a G.P. term:

\(a+(a+d)r+(a+2d)r^2+\cdots+\big[a+(n-1)d\big]r^{n-1}\)
\(1+2\cdot2+3\cdot4+4\cdot8=49\) (A.P. \(1,2,3,4\) times G.P. \(1,2,4,8\))
57. How do I find the sum? (multiply by \(r\) and subtract)
• Find the sum of the A.G.S.

Same trick as the G.P., but the subtraction leaves a G.P. in the middle.

Interactive diagram (needs JavaScript).

\(S=1+4+12+32\),   \(r=2\)
\((1-r)S=a+d(r+r^2+r^3)-(a+3d)r^4=1+(2+4+8)-4(16)=-49\)
\(S=\mathbf{49}\ \checkmark\)
58. Is there a formula for the sum to infinity?
• Sum of an infinite A.G.S.
\(S_\infty=\frac a{1-r}+\frac{dr}{(1-r)^2},\qquad|r|<1\)
\(1+\dfrac22+\dfrac34+\dfrac48+\cdots\):   \(a=1,\ d=1,\ r=\tfrac12\)
\(S_\infty=\dfrac1{1/2}+\dfrac{1/2}{1/4}=2+2=\mathbf4\)
Do not memorise the finite formula. Learn the method (multiply by \(r\), subtract) and rebuild it in the exam.

Shortcuts that save time in the exam

  • Recognise the type in ten seconds: subtract consecutive terms (constant means A.P.); divide them (constant means G.P.); take reciprocals (A.P. means H.P.).
  • A.P.: with two terms known, \(d=\dfrac{a_q-a_p}{q-p}\); no simultaneous equations needed.
  • A.P.: for an odd number of terms, \(S_n=n\times\) the middle term.
  • A.P.: if \(S_n=An^2+Bn\) then \(d=2A\) and \(a_1=A+B\).
  • A.P.: \(n\) means give \(n+1\) equal steps. Counting terms is \(\dfrac{\text{last}-\text{first}}{d}+1\); never forget the \(+1\).
  • G.P.: two terms known means divide them and take the root of the gap: \(r^{q-p}=\dfrac{a_q}{a_p}\).
  • G.P.: three numbers with product \(P\): the middle number is \(\sqrt[3]{P}\).
  • G.P.: an infinite sum exists only if \(|r|<1\); state the condition.
  • Percent: \(x\%\) increase means \(r=1+\tfrac x{100}\); \(x\%\) decrease means \(r=1-\tfrac x{100}\).
  • Bouncing ball: total distance \(=h\,\dfrac{1+e}{1-e}\) (up and down, the factor 2).
  • H.P.: flip, solve as an A.P., flip back. For H.M.s insert A.M.s between the reciprocals.
  • Means: \(G^2=AH\), so any one of the three follows from the other two.
  • Sigma: memorise \(\sum k,\ \sum k^2,\ \sum k^3\) and always check your closed form at \(n=1\) and \(n=2\).
  • A.G.S.: do not memorise the long formula. Multiply by \(r\) and subtract; the A.P. part collapses.
  • Writing: write the model first (“A.P., \(a=5,\ d=3,\ n=20\)”) for method marks, and substitute back to check.
